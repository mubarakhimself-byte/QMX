# The workflow engine, in plain words

**What it is.** One machine inside QMX that runs jobs you set up once and then leave alone: "every six hours, look for new papers and write me a note", "every weekday morning, build my brief", "take this idea through the funnel and stop before paper trading". Every job is drawn as boxes and arrows on the Lab's graph canvas. A box can be a script (free, no AI), a rule, a wait, one AI call, a whole AI agent with a few helpers, a data kernel that chews through big tables, a check that grades the result, or another job. A job can also be just one agent, or no AI at all. Templates are jobs with sensible defaults; you or the copilot copy one and change it.

**How a job gets made.** You describe it roughly in chat. The copilot asks you a few numbered questions, each with a suggested answer, so "yes" is enough. It then draws the draft on the canvas. Before anything goes live, the draft is checked (do the boxes connect, is every setting there, nothing touches money) and then test-run with sample data and real, cheap models, so you see it actually works. When the test passes and you say yes once, the draft is frozen as version 1 and a schedule is switched on. Saving a draft and test-running it never ask you; publishing and switching a schedule on are your own click on a card the copilot shows, because the copilot itself is never allowed to do either. Drafts never sit in the trusted copy (`main`): if a job itself makes a new job, app, pod or skill, it arrives as a draft for your yes. Nothing is ever deleted; old versions and old runs are archived and stay in the Library.

**Where agents live.** Your Contabo server is the always-on home: the Library, the key safe, market data, monitoring, the scheduler, and a few agents kept warm. When you need more agents at once, the engine rents them from a provider (Amp, Cloudroom, Cloudflare, E2B, Modal, whichever is cheap and reliable; none is paid for yet and none is locked in). Your PC runs about three at a time and your server about four today (it has 12 GB of memory, and the Library, the key safe, market data and monitoring take their share first); more go to rented machines. A new provider is set up by the copilot from the instructions that provider publishes for agents; you click once to apply it and type any key once. The thinking part of an agent, with its permissions and its conversation, stays in QMX on a machine you control. On your PC an agent and its scripts run like Claude Code does, as ordinary programs; on a server or a rented machine, the commands it wants to run go into a throw-away box that is destroyed after the step. Some providers insist on running the whole agent in their own machine; that is allowed for cheap burst work with key-based providers only, and the engine still keeps the diary and the results. Backtests never share a machine with agents: one dedicated QMB machine does them, and agents just ask it.

**Keys once, everywhere.** You type a key once in Settings on your PC. It is stored in Windows' credential store, and an encrypted copy goes to a small safe on your server. No agent and no rented machine ever receives a key. Each step gets a short-lived pass instead, good for that one step, that one machine and the models that step may use; a small relay on your PC or server adds the real key to each model call. Rented machines hold no key at all: they send their calls to the relay on your server, which is the one central relay. Change a key in Settings and every machine uses the new one within a minute; delete it and every machine stops within a minute. If your server is unreachable, running work continues on the copies the relays already hold; nothing new starts there until it is back. If a pass were stolen, the thief could at most make model calls with the models that step may use until the pass expires (it ends with its step) or is cancelled, and write junk into that run's folder; never read a key, never touch your main Library, never touch money. Your Claude plan sign-in stays on the machines you own: you sign in once per machine, and it never goes to a rented one.

**What changed in the rerun (2026-10-18 evening).** Four things you asked for are now in the design. (1) **No QMX limits at all.** No $5 per chat, no 50 % share, no token, step or time ceiling. The only limits are your providers' own; when one says "wait", the work waits and carries on by itself. QMX stops an agent only when it is stuck (nothing happening) or looping (the same call, the same answer, again and again), and then it saves a summary of where it got to. (2) **Your copilot on Claude can use Kimi, Qwen, DeepSeek and the rest as helpers in the same chat**, with thinking, tools and logs intact, and so can every workflow. QMX passes your Claude requests to Anthropic exactly as Claude Code sent them and never stores your sign-in; only the other providers' requests are routed through your pool. (3) **The pool is ours, built in QMX**, spreads work across your accounts (least-used first, never "fill one up first"), and uses your daily copilot account last. (4) **Agents may set up strategies in your MT5 and cTrader on a demo account** that the manifest pins; live accounts stay blocked and the money gate still checks every step.

**Scripts and your money (changed again 2026-10-18, your rulings 115-117).** Agents have no road to your money: live trading starts only after a backtest, a stress test and your own yes; withdrawals happen only through your broker in your own browser; MT5 and cTrader cannot withdraw; and agents never sign in to your broker or provider accounts. So the extra money machinery is gone: no check that reads every script and prompt for words like "place an order", no sealed Windows box, no rule that edited scripts must run in Docker or on your server. Scripts and agents now run on your PC the way Claude Code does, as ordinary programs. Running a job in a container or on your server or a rented machine is your choice, per job, never a safety rule. What stays, because it stops real bugs: each script works in its own folder outside QMX's home, so two steps never overwrite each other's files; if QMX crashes, every script it started dies with it, so nothing is left running; a script's settings never include your keys or your Claude sign-in folder; anything that looks like a key is blanked before it reaches the diary, the inbox, the Library or the screen; your keys stay in Windows' credential store. Live runs and publishing are still your clicks only.

**Mixed models in one job.** One agent can run on Kimi while its helpers run on DeepSeek and the judge on GLM, in the same conversation; the relay sends each call to the right provider by the model's name. Any provider that speaks the Claude-style API joins by a web address and a key, no code.

**What happens when it runs.** The engine keeps a diary of every step: what started, what came out, how long it took, what it cost, and on which machine. If the computer restarts in the middle, the engine reads the diary and carries on from the first unfinished step. Finished steps are never done twice, so you never pay twice for the same AI work. Before building anything, a step first asks the Library "was this exact thing built before?" and reuses it if so. Big jobs are cut into chunks, not one agent per item, and long conversations are compacted early (at 40 % of what the model can hold) so the AI stays sharp; every tool an agent uses is logged as its own line in the trace.

**Nothing waits for you.** A job never stops to ask you a question while it runs. The only exception is a step that would do something outside QMX that cannot be undone, like sending a message; that one waits for an answer in your inbox. Everything else, including writing to the Library, just happens.

**When something fails.** The engine tries a few things in a fixed order, by itself: try again, use another account, use another model, then ask a cheap "doctor" AI to look at the step. If all of that fails, the run is marked failed and a line appears in your daily digest: what broke, what was tried, what it cost, where it ran, and a link to the provider's own page for that machine. Only a provider failure (a sign-in that stopped working, every account used up for hours) or a harness error reaches your inbox. The next scheduled run starts fresh anyway. There is no spending cap and nobody asks you about usage: nothing stops on how much it uses. A stuck or looping job (nothing happening, or the same call with the same answer again and again) stops, saves a summary of where it got to, and tells you in the digest.

**Watching the machines.** You do not need a fleet screen in QMX. The copilot can open each provider's own dashboard for you. QMX itself shows on every run card where the step ran, whether that machine is healthy, what it has cost, and a link out. A live picture of a desktop appears only when you open it.

**Using a desktop or a browser.** When a job needs to click around a website or a desktop app, a small driver (Cua) is loaded for that step only, with a list of what it may touch that the agent cannot widen: which apps, which websites, for how long. Broker and trading-platform windows are always off limits. Web and Linux apps run on a rented Linux desktop; Windows apps like MT5 run on your PC or on a Windows virtual machine on your server. Every click is kept as a screenshot in the run's trace.

**How it remembers and improves.** Every run notes what it learned. A nightly job proposes one small change, replays it against past failures and successes, and only an improvement that breaks nothing takes over; it rolls itself back if it does worse. You set one rule per job once; nobody asks you per change, and the version you approved by hand is never overwritten.

**One conversation, any screen.** A chat keeps the place it was born (the Lab, Portfolio, the hub, the Library, an app). You can open it from any screen and carry on; it never forgets where it came from, and "open in its home" takes you back.

**Where everything is stored.** Jobs, versions, run summaries, produced files, traces, memory notes and agent-made tools all live in the one Library, on your PC now and on your server as the main copy later. The engine's own step diary, schedules and inbox live in a small database file beside QMX on each machine that runs it.

**What the copilot, the Lab canvas and the chat have in common.** They are three views of the same engine. The canvas edits the draft as a graph or as code, the copilot builds and explains, the chat shows previews and run cards. None of them has its own rules.

**Helpers on other models, honestly (after the review of the rerun).** Full helper chats on Kimi, Qwen, DeepSeek and the rest under your Opus copilot are designed and do not depend on anything unproven. Having those models as built-in sub-agents inside the same Opus chat depends on four checks; if the key one fails, that part is simply not built and the full helper chats do the job. A copilot whose main model is Kimi or GPT gets built-in helpers on other providers either way: those do not depend on your Claude sign-in, so that check does not apply to them.

**MT5 and cTrader on demo (simplified 2026-10-18).** Agents use only the demo accounts you set up for them, in the MT5 copy or the demo-only cTrader sign-in you keep for that. Your live accounts are never in an agent's reach. There an agent may install a strategy, set it up, run the tester and switch algo trading on. The old five-step proof (QMX checking the account itself every minute, a guard compiled into every strategy) is gone.

**What the three studies changed (2026-10-18, after your choice of Hindsight).** (1) **Every tool is one row in one list,** grouped into small sets, and each chat or workflow step gets only the sets it needs: an overnight intake on Kimi sees about fifteen tools instead of eighty-two, and a helper never gets more than its parent. (2) **One place decides what each chat is told and given,** and records a fingerprint of it, so "what did the model see" always has an answer. (3) **Hindsight is the memory,** and memory follows the thing you worked on (a pod, a workflow, an app), not the folder: what a Lab chat learned about a pod is found by a new hub chat and by that night's workflow. Lessons carry the period and market regime they were true for, and "forget" hides one. (4) **Point at anything with @:** a pod, a run's output, an app's latest result, a folder, a website or a video; the chat keeps the list when you reopen it elsewhere. (5) **Things talk two ways only:** ask an app to do something, or leave a result in the Library that something else is watching for; it works across your PC and server with nothing new to run. (6) **Bad news travels:** mark a data release as bad and every result, lesson and idea built on it shows it, with the path, and the schedules that use it stop; clear the mark and everything comes back. (7) **Ideas have a status** (supported, refuted, open) computed from the tests behind them. (8) **One pause button** stops all autonomous work on every machine. (9) **A test set for the copilot itself** runs whenever its instructions, skills or default model change.

**What changed when you unlocked the whole app (2026-10-18, your ruling 118).** Nothing in QMX is protected any more, so the design stopped working around its own parts. (1) **One program instead of three.** Today the copilot, the job engine and the control panel each start their own program, and they refuse to share your QMX folder, so the copilot and the job engine cannot run at the same time. From now on one program starts everything. (2) **Agent steps can actually build things.** Until now an agent step in a job could only read and answer, because every request to write a file was refused. Now it writes freely inside its own private folder; only the files the job says it produces are kept. (3) **Helpers and tools are added directly in the AI program, not around it.** The copilot's helper chats, attachments, standing loops and browser tools are now plain changes to QMX's own code, with nothing waiting for your yes. (4) **Mini apps are real Claude plugins.** An app's skills, helper agents and commands load the way Claude Code loads any plugin, and you can add an app to a running chat without restarting it. (5) **The money gate is replaced by a simpler guard.** It keeps what really protects you: no agent reads your sign-ins or keys, writes into QMX's own databases or your personal Claude folder, or runs a destructive command; your own always-allow and never-allow lists still apply. The word-matching for "place an order" is gone, because agents have no road to your money. (6) **One place for every run.** Remote jobs, app schedules and workflow runs all become workflow runs, with one list and one diary. (7) **About 122,600 lines of old code are deleted** (the old daemon, its wire format and core, and the LangChain runtime), once nothing uses them. (8) **Small AI calls no longer start a whole Claude Code program**: a one-shot call to Kimi, DeepSeek or GPT goes straight through QMX's router, which makes big jobs faster and lighter. Your only open call is still which rented provider to pay for, and when.

**What the review of 18 October fixed (review 48).** (1) **Work on your server shows on your PC.** A run, a problem or a question from a job running on your server appears in your PC's inbox, run lists and copilot within seconds, and pressing "retry" on the PC acts on the server. If the server cannot be reached, the PC says so and shows the last thing it knew. (2) **Your server becomes the Library's main copy as soon as you set it up,** so chains of jobs on the server keep going while your PC sleeps; your PC keeps a copy and catches up when it wakes. (3) **A job can move to another machine** if its machine dies: you click "continue on this machine" and finished steps are not paid for again. (4) **Nothing that works remotely is deleted until its replacement works** on a real machine. (5) **An honest rule about writes:** an agent step's file tools write only in its own folder, but if you let a step run shell commands, those commands can write elsewhere on your PC, the same as Claude Code; QMX keeps only the files the step declared, and never lets anything touch its own databases, your sign-ins or your keys. If a job must not touch the rest of the PC, give it a container. (6) **Added apps:** their tools work at once, their skills and helpers from your next message. (7) **Cheaper long jobs:** every step's instructions now hit the AI providers' cache even though each step works in its own folder, and a changed instruction really reaches the AI after a chat is reopened. (8) **The copilot's test set runs on the model you are switching to,** not on the small test model. (9) **One question about money words for you** (W3 in the open decisions); nothing waits on it.

# The workflow engine, in plain words

**What it is.** One machine inside QMX that runs jobs you set up once and then leave alone: "every six hours, look for new papers and write me a note", "every weekday morning, build my brief", "take this idea through the funnel and stop before paper trading". Every job is drawn as boxes and arrows on the Lab's graph canvas. A box can be a script (free, no AI), a rule, a wait, one AI call, a whole AI agent with a few helpers, a data kernel that chews through big tables, a check that grades the result, or another job. A job can also be just one agent, or no AI at all. Templates are jobs with sensible defaults; you or the copilot copy one and change it.

**How a job gets made.** You describe it roughly in chat. The copilot asks you a few numbered questions, each with a suggested answer, so "yes" is enough. It then draws the draft on the canvas. Before anything goes live, the draft is checked (do the boxes connect, is every setting there, nothing touches money) and then test-run with sample data and real, cheap models, so you see it actually works. When the test passes and you say yes once, the draft is frozen as version 1 and a schedule is switched on. Nothing is ever deleted; old versions and old runs are archived and stay in the Library.

**Where it runs.** The thinking part of an agent, with its sign-in, its permissions and its conversation, always stays in QMX itself, on your PC or on your own server. Only the commands it wants to run (a shell command, a file write, a Python cell) are sent into a throw-away box, a container that is created for that one step and destroyed after it. So a mistake inside the box cannot reach your files, your keys or the trading node, and there is nothing in the box worth stealing. About three boxes run at a time on your PC, about twenty on your server, more on a rented machine when you want. Your server for agents is never the trading node.

**What happens when it runs.** The engine keeps a diary of every step: what started, what came out, how long it took, what it cost. If the computer restarts in the middle, the engine reads the diary and carries on from the first unfinished step. Finished steps are never done twice, so you never pay twice for the same AI work. Before building anything, a step first asks the Library "was this exact thing built before?" and reuses it if so.

**Nothing waits for you.** A job never stops to ask you a question while it runs. The only exception is a step that would do something outside QMX that cannot be undone, like sending a message; that one waits for an answer in your inbox. Everything else, including writing to the Library, just happens. A job that has run for weeks without you touching it is working as intended.

**When something fails.** The engine tries a few things in a fixed order, by itself: try again, use another account, use another model, then ask a cheap "doctor" AI to look at the step and retry it with a hint or hand the lesson to the improvement loop. If all of that fails, the run is parked and an item appears in your inbox: what broke, what was tried, what it cost, and buttons. The next scheduled run starts fresh anyway. One item per problem, not one per retry.

**When a usage limit hits.** Your accounts are a pool. On the PC the copilot and PC jobs may use your Claude plans; the server's twenty agents use key-based providers (Kimi, GLM, Ollama Cloud and others), because one plan cannot feed twenty agents. Each AI step picks the account with the most room and keeps it for the whole step. When every account is out of room, the step waits until the limit resets and continues by itself. You only hear about it if the wait goes on for hours.

**How it remembers and improves.** Every run notes what it learned: what worked, what was a dead end, what not to retry. Those notes live in the Library like everything else, so they are backed up and reachable from the server. A nightly job reads the lessons, proposes one small change to a job, replays it against past failures and past successes, and only if it fixes the former without breaking the latter does the improved version take over. If it then does worse in real runs, it rolls itself back. You set one rule per job once (improve on its own, only suggest, or off); nobody asks you per change, and the version you approved by hand is never overwritten.

**Where everything is stored.** Jobs, versions, run summaries, produced files, traces, memory notes and agent-made tools all live in the one Library, on your PC now and on your server later. The engine's own step diary, schedules and inbox live in a small database file beside QMX. Passwords and keys never appear in a job and never enter a box; a job only names the key it needs.

**What the copilot, the Lab canvas and the chat have in common.** They are three views of the same engine. The canvas edits the draft as a graph or as code, the copilot builds and explains, the chat shows previews and run cards. None of them has its own rules.

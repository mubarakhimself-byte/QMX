# Arena pick: base, grafts, rejections

Lead, 6 Nov 2026. Three candidates under `architecture/candidate-1`, `-2`, `-3`. Skills were checked from each transcript: all three opened bmad-architecture, codebase-design and domain-modeling.

The lead read candidate 1 in full, plus candidates 2 and 3 through their contracts, with their summaries and rationales. No separate cross-judge was run, to save usage (M45, M46). The fold agent reads all three end to end, and one independent check follows the fold.

## Rubric the pick used

1. Expresses the twelve core parts with no workaround.
2. Extension by row and plug, no rework (new brake, sizer base, class, style; Lab, MCP, agents).
3. Answers "how does it self configure" and "what if I want to change the way it is derived".
4. Performance: no re-stacked cuts, healthy unit above the choke budget.
5. Fit to the code and honest build size.
6. Simplicity: he called an old version "too much machine".
7. Survival and the hard lines.

## Base: candidate 1

Why: it is the most complete at the lowest stated size (29 PRs against 38 and 28), it carries his Kubernetes model in the document's shape, its status file meets R22's snapshot simply, and it answers all the hand-offs. All three candidates hold the healthy unit at about 0.9 of baseline, so performance did not separate them.

## Fork results

- **F-B: one guard above the position.** Candidates 1 and 2 argue it. Candidate 3 is a serious version, and its fact kernel and shadow rule are good. But cross-unit brakes become round trips, there are two arbitration stages, and its rank needs a property test to prove it equals one table. The hedge comes from candidate 2: brake blocks keep the shipped `HaltInstance` shape, hosted by the guard. So a later move into the unit is a move, not a rewrite.
- **F-A: the shape of the full model, the simple modes on.** The document, status and receipts carry the Kubernetes and VPA shape from day one: spec and status, a recommendation with low and high bounds (filled later), an authority mode per number. At go-live the modes on are `user_only`, `off`, `at_anchor` and `at_flat` (`at_flat` because the shipped motion engine already moves logic numbers at flat). `at_period_open` and `live` parse and refuse as `authority_not_built`. This costs nothing later and needs no reader changed. Candidate 3's rule removes candidate 1's main argument for `live`: **facts move live, numbers move at anchors**. A cost cell or a spread is a fact the sizer reads, so it acts at once with no learned number moving. Candidate 1's sim shows the applier's extra modes earn only in that one case. He must hear this plainly: Kubernetes is the model; the first version runs it in its simplest mode.

## Grafts into the base

From candidate 2:
1. The group document (`qmx.risk-group/1`): the base names a portfolio config but gives it no contract. Keep `display_name` and a reserved `parent`, so Book, BMS, cluster and portfolio stay open until Q8.
2. **A sizer base is a derivation, not a socket.** A base produces `baseline_risk_usd`; the sizer block only turns dollars at risk into lots through value per point. This replaces the base's wide `SizerInputs` with a thin surface, makes a new sizer base a row, and fixes the USDJPY fault at its root.
3. Compile refuses a second `mode: live` row reading the same ledger at the same scope, which makes one-reader-per-loss structural.
4. The total choke budget blocks shipping in CI: a healthy unit under 0.75 of baseline fails any change touching a binding factor.
5. A stop is an absolute level at every internal seam; only the venue encoder derives a distance, signed by side. This replaces the base's signed `new_distance` and answers the stop-past-entry hand-off more simply.
6. Safe by type: `before_order` takes entry intents only, and the effect union has no variant that lowers a kill level.
7. A registry row is a `guard` section on the dictionary entry, so a new part is one folder. This ties into the QML dictionary expansion. Brake blocks keep `HaltInstance`, hosted by the guard through a block factory, so `qmf-risk` stays free of `qml`.
8. Identity rows `profile_sizing_change` and `profile_exit_change`, with a `logic_fingerprint`.
9. Anchors are clocks plus a closed trigger list; `anchor_id` is a pure function; at most one level up per anchor; permission to exceed 1.0 is itself a raise; a restart changes nothing a brake can see.
10. Its findings against the funnel:
    - DP9's level-weighted B cancels the level at N = 1, so freed swing goes only to units at level 1.00.
    - The day stop's k derives from the group's own p1 day.
    - `tzdata` is pinned on Windows.
    - The right bars for a 1-minute cut arrive only after G-DEMO. He must be told.

From candidate 3:
11. Facts live, numbers at anchors (above).
12. A named fact kernel inside the guard package, shared with the harness, with one conformance tape through both ports in CI.
13. **The drift finding:** at h = 17 with "two warnings inside 500 trades", 42 % of healthy one-unit paths go to paper inside 120 days and need his click back. So a second warning is one more level down, a third is paper, and the rule is a derivation on the drift row. Verify this against `candidate-3/out-c3.txt` before relying on it.
14. The rank table property test over random control states, and the guard folds the highest state even when the unit already refused.

How a trade gets its size

Size comes from survival. It starts from the cushion above your floor and asks: how big can each trade be so that touching the floor stays rare, even if the edge turns out to be zero? A real edge is then checked three ways: may the unit trade, what level does it run at, and later, may it go above base size.

This also settles your split question. Adding units neither keeps full size nor divides by the count. Size falls with the square root of the count, more when units lose together. Move the sliders.

    How it configures itself

    You found the right model: Kubernetes, the self-healing software. The first version runs it in its simplest modes, and the document already has the fields for the full version, so growing needs no rework.

    How does a number get its value?

    • Each number names a rule and a ladder: its own record, then its class, then the preset, then refuse.
    • At the day or week mark the rule runs and writes a receipt saying why.
    • The number's mode decides whether the value moves: never, watch only, at the mark, or when the unit is flat.
    • If what the rule assumed breaks, it drops down the ladder, and it freezes after repeated trips.
    • A key number with no rule stops the unit compiling. No blanks.

    What if you want to change how it derives?

    • Change the value, swap the rule or reorder the ladder, in a new version of the document, then click.
    • Floor, buffer, kill line and kill level never tune themselves.
    • Facts move live. A broker cost or spread change acts at once.
    • Learned numbers move at the marks. Cuts act at once; raises wait.
    • A new brake, sizing method or scope is one folder in the dictionary. A new instrument class still needs its venue work.

    Brakes and accelerators, defined

    Both are measured against a declared base size. These definitions came from how you used the words, plus the research.

    Brake

    Holds risk at or below base. Your circuit breaker keeps its name.

    Accelerator

    Raises risk above base. It stays off while any brake is active.

    Restore

    Growing back after a cut. It never goes above base, so it is not an accelerator.

    Clamp

    A fixed limit that reads no evidence, such as margin headroom.

    House money and your reverse house money

    The old versions were not mirrors of each other. On one ledger they become one rule: size eases down as the day loses and can press as it wins. Your reverse half binds from day one at portfolio level. The press after wins measures only, until a unit's own trades show that it pays, because in the model no accelerator beats a steady size when trades are independent.

    BINDS at go-live

    Twenty-three things bind, and three of them read results:

    • Unit bench: trips at the unit's own worst-1% money loss, counting fast-invalidation cuts by size.
    • Day pair: a taper, then a stop for the whole book until the next day.
    • Drift monitor: each warning drops one level for 20 days; the third goes to paper.

    The rest are mechanics: costs, the split, level, bet key, cushion clip, margin, order checks, SQS, news, admission.

    What the model shows

    "Kept" is dollars risked over base size on every chance to trade, counting refused trades. The first funnel kept a weak unit near 0.15 of size on a kinder measure; that was the handbrake.     What you must hear

    Your scalper may not exist yet

    A 55% scalper at 1:1 and a 5-pip stop loses after costs, because break-even is 58%. It only works if the fast-invalidation cut catches few winners. At 10% of winners caught it just survives; at 20% it loses. No paper tests this, so QMX's own two-arm trial is the first job.

    Your frequency waits on the cushion

    At $500 over a $300 floor with a $0.50 lot, about 3 of 10 proved units can trade, and 1 of a weak ten. The others wait in paper and join as the cushion grows.

    The first version is plainer than the vision

    15-minute bars on the node until after the demo, so no 1-minute cut before then. No trails, breakeven or partial closes at first. One position per market per unit. The first live entry waits hours for a live SQS baseline.

    The soft spot is many bleeding units

    5 or 10 units with overstated proof lose half the cushion in 11 to 14% of model runs. A unit can re-enter the same move once its stops fire, and the class isolation that would help measures only at first.

    Some things wait for your click

    A third bench within 40 days stops a unit until you resume it. About one healthy unit in three hits that per 120 days when it runs alone. If you never click, that book falls under the safety line.

    Some asks come later

    The sweep cap returns as a ratchet, after go-live. Several positions per unit wait. Book against tags is a question for after go-live. Agents get read, propose and cut-only evidence for now.

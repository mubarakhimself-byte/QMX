## Turn 1 (topic question, model: Smart) - answer

The strongest directly transferable result is that **conservative bandits can preserve an anytime performance floor while paying an additive exploration cost in the stochastic setting**—but the literature does not give a universal percentage of trading growth sacrificed per unit of drawdown avoided. Trading, clinical trials, and software rollouts use different safety measures, so their results do not combine into one growth-versus-loss number.

### Most useful work

| Paper | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| [Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282) (2016) | The clearest model of learning against a trusted incumbent: cumulative reward must stay above a chosen fraction of the baseline **at every time**, not merely at the end. Conservative UCB interleaves exploration with baseline play; under its stochastic assumptions, the safety cost is additive in the regret bound, while “build a budget, then explore” can impose a much larger multiplicative cost. The 2016 bandit result is particularly relevant to the incumbent-strategy idea: the learner maintains a cumulative “safety budget,” spends it on exploration when the estimated downside allows, and returns to the baseline when the budget gets low. Its guarantee depends on assumptions about rewards and a meaningful baseline; it is **not** a guarantee against fat-tail losses, regime changes, or correlated positions. The 2024 contextual work broadens the approach beyond linear models, but its assumptions and performance constraint still need to be translated carefully before applying it to a trading book.

Clinical dose escalation contributes a useful *shape* for the policy: begin conservatively, make decisions in batches, and only escalate when evidence clears explicit boundaries. But a clinical trial’s cohort-level toxicity evidence is not a calibration recipe for market risk. Similarly, software canaries and progressive delivery suggest exposing only a small slice first and expanding after monitoring it; they are good operational analogies, but the work retrieved here does not establish a quantitative trading-style tradeoff between growth surrendered and losses prevented.

### A practical ramp-up and ramp-down policy

I would control **risk budget**, not a strategy’s raw notional alone. Measure strategy risk using realized and stressed loss, tail exposure, liquidity, correlation with the rest of the book, and behavior across relevant market conditions. Set a per-strategy cap *and* a whole-book cap: several individually small strategies can still create a large common-factor or venue exposure.

For a newly promoted strategy:

1. **Shadow first, then trade at a tiny cap.** Keep shadow results separate from live P&L. Once live, start around **5–10% of its intended risk allocation**. This is an operational starting point, not a number established by the papers.
2. **Escalate in evidence-based steps, not on a calendar alone.** A reasonable ladder is 10% → 20% → 35% → 55% → 75% → 100%. At each level, require a minimum amount of *independent* evidence—enough trades and distinct sessions or market conditions—and a conservative estimate of risk-adjusted performance versus the incumbent or an explicit no-trade baseline. Pause at a level if evidence is inconclusive; do not treat a good short run as proof.
3. **Use both a baseline floor and hard risk limits.** Keep a bandit-style cumulative performance budget against the benchmark, but add absolute limits for drawdown, expected shortfall or stress loss, and liquidity. A relative baseline constraint alone can approve a strategy when both it and the book are losing.
4. **Make increases slower than cuts.** Require two consecutive qualifying review windows before increasing risk; cap any one increase at roughly **25–50% of the current allocation**. Use non-overlapping windows where possible, so repeated looks at the same lucky observations do not create false confidence.
5. **Use hysteresis.** Set a clear entry threshold for increasing risk and a separate, more conservative threshold for holding. This avoids repeated up/down resizing around one noisy boundary. Do not increase and decrease on the same signal.
6. **Cut promptly on breached limits; re-risk only after recovery.** A hard loss, data-quality, execution, or exposure-limit breach should cause an immediate reduction or kill-switch, not a vote from the growth optimizer. After a soft breach, step down one or more tiers and require a fresh qualification period before climbing again. After a hard breach, require human review and explicit re-promotion.

Apply the same asymmetric rule at book level: aggregate correlated and stressed exposures, throttle new risk when the book approaches a limit, and allow quick reductions but only gradual restoration. The key tradeoff in the bandit literature is **baseline safety versus slower or more costly exploration**; it does not quantify how much trading alpha you should surrender to prevent a given drawdown. That calibration needs to come from your own risk limits, execution costs, and live evidence.

## Turn 2 (follow-up a: top three methods and a ramp ladder, model: Smart) - answer

**The best three templates are Conservative UCB, the newer Reserve-C4B conservative bandit, and Bayesian interval dose-escalation designs.** Together they offer three useful controls: preserve a cumulative baseline budget, avoid overreacting to uncertainty in the baseline, and make risk changes through explicit “increase / hold / decrease” bands. None was validated on a multi-strategy trading book, and none gives a reliable universal figure for trading growth surrendered versus drawdown avoided.

## 1. Conservative UCB: explore only when the safety budget permits

[Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282) (2016) is the clearest starting point for a trusted incumbent strategy. It assumes a default action, a set of alternatives, and a tolerance for how far cumulative performance may fall below the default. Its constraint is **anytime**: it must hold at every point, not just at the end. The method keeps a running safety budget and uses the default to replenish it when exploration would otherwise risk crossing the floor. [Baseline Budget](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=3) [Conservative UCB](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=5)

**Step by step:** At each decision, estimate each strategy’s reward and confidence interval; choose the candidate the underlying UCB learner wants to try; estimate a conservative lower bound on the cumulative budget *if that candidate is traded*; trade it only if the lower bound stays nonnegative; otherwise use the baseline. Evidence needed is the observed reward history for each candidate and a baseline return estimate—known in the simplest form, but the paper also gives an unknown-baseline variant. Output is a candidate trade or a fallback to the baseline.

**What it reports:** In its stochastic, bounded, sub-Gaussian setting, the anytime constraint holds with high probability, and the extra regret from the conservative constraint is an **additive** term of order $O(KL/(\alpha\mu_0))$, rather than the larger multiplicative penalty of “wait, accumulate budget, then explore.” The paper’s synthetic experiments report that constrained performance approaches ordinary UCB over time, but do not give a generally applicable numerical “percentage of return surrendered” for trading. Its adversarial variant has a larger regret penalty than the stochastic one. [Regret Bound](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=6) [Synthetic Experiments](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=8)

**Where it can fail in trading:** The guarantee concerns cumulative **mean reward**, under assumptions that do not cover fat-tailed returns or abrupt regime shifts. A strategy can satisfy a mean-return constraint while suffering a ruinous realized loss. It assumes reward feedback arrives round by round; trading P&L may not be final until positions close, financing and slippage are booked, or data corrections arrive. Its per-arm constraint also does not protect a portfolio against several strategies losing together.

**Later work:** [Conservative Contextual Linear Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1611.06426) (2017) adds observable context; [Conservative Contextual Bandits: Beyond Linear Representations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.06165) (2024) extends the idea to nonlinear reward models. The newer work [Reserve-Aware Contrast Certificates for Conservative Bandits with Uncertain Baselines](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106) (2026) points out that separately bounding candidate and baseline uncertainty can make the gate needlessly pessimistic.

## 2. Reserve-C4B: compare candidate and baseline uncertainty directly

[Reserve-Aware Contrast Certificates for Conservative Bandits with Uncertain Baselines](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106) (2026) makes the comparison itself the unit of safety. Instead of separately asking whether the candidate is bad and the baseline is good, it estimates the uncertainty of the **candidate-minus-baseline contrast**. It also offers a prefix-refresh rule: as more evidence arrives, recertify earlier decisions without throwing away credit that was already certified. [Contrast and Refresh](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106?page=2)

**Step by step:** Supply candidate and baseline features, observed rewards, prior/history data, a confidence level, and an allowed reserve. Construct a shared confidence set; calculate a pessimistic lower confidence bound on the candidate’s relative value; and check whether the remaining reserve plus that bound stays above zero. If so, choose the highest-scoring admissible candidate; otherwise use the baseline. At the next decision, either carry forward the certified balance or refresh the cumulative prefix using the updated model. Output is an admissible candidate or baseline fallback, plus an updated reserve ledger. Historical data improve estimation but do not themselves count as live deployment credit. [Decision Rule](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106?page=2) [Algorithm](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106?page=3)

**What it reports:** In a controlled linear simulation with 256 episodes, diverse history, and zero initial reserve, the contrast certificate reduced baseline fallback from **88.2% to 19.4%** and increased reward relative to baseline from **1.0084 to 1.0557**; prefix refresh reduced fallback further to **0.5%** and raised the ratio to **1.0606**. With baseline-only history, contrast alone still fell back on 91.8% of rounds, while refresh reduced that to 12.1%. Gated methods had no conditional-mean prefix violations in the tested settings; unconstrained LinUCB violated the zero-reserve constraint in 16.4% of episodes in one reported setting. These are simulation results, **not** live trading results, and the safety statement is about conditional means, not realized P&L paths. [Simulation Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106?page=3) [Safety Findings](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106?page=4)

**Where it can fail in trading:** The analyzed model is linear, stationary, and sub-Gaussian; the paper itself says it does not establish robustness to nonlinear rewards, drift, or misspecified noise bounds. Shared uncertainty helps only where candidate and baseline risk really is shared. A new strategy trading another instrument, time of day, venue, or volatility regime may have little useful overlap with the baseline. Like Conservative UCB, this is not a tail-loss or portfolio-correlation guarantee.

**Later work:** Reserve-C4B explicitly builds on earlier conservative bandits, including [Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282), [Conservative Contextual Linear Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1611.06426), and the 2024 nonlinear contextual work. Its main challenge to simpler gates is that independent candidate/baseline confidence bounds can charge twice for uncertainty that is actually shared. It does not overturn the earlier anytime-budget idea; it refines how that budget is certified.

## 3. Bayesian interval dose escalation: make each move a deliberate step

[Bayesian Decision-optimal Interval Designs for Phase I Clinical Trials](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1309.5019) (2013) is a useful analogue for controlled ramping. At each dose, the design compares observed toxicity with a lower and upper boundary: below the lower boundary, escalate; above the upper, de-escalate; between them, hold. The authors choose boundaries to reduce decision errors around an agreed target and include a rule to eliminate doses judged too toxic. This is a *decision template*, not a trading algorithm. [Interval Rule](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1309.5019?page=5) [Elimination Rule](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1309.5019?page=13)

**Step by step:** Specify an ordered set of risk levels, a target risk tolerance, and what counts as materially below or above that target. At the current level, collect a fixed batch of outcomes; calculate the observed risk rate; compare it with the two decision boundaries; and output one of three actions: move up one level, stay, or move down one level. If posterior evidence that the level exceeds the unacceptable-risk boundary is strong enough, remove that level and all higher ones. In trading, a “dose” could be an allocation tier; evidence should be based on matured P&L and risk outcomes, not just a count of trades.

**What it reports:** In 10,000 simulated 36-patient trials, for scenarios with an average probability difference of 0.1 around the target, the local optimal interval design selected the target dose in 46.2% of trials versus 44.7% for CRM; its reported “high toxicity” risk was 14.8% versus 16.9%, and “poor allocation” risk was 28.7% versus 43.0%. Average sample sizes were very similar (35.3 vs. 35.2), so in those simulations the safety improvement did not require a larger average trial. These are clinical simulation endpoints—not estimates of lost trading return—and the comparison was against CRM and other designs, not a direct comparison to the standard 3+3 rule. [Simulation Table](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1309.5019?page=37)

**Where it can fail in trading:** Clinical designs assume a meaningful ordered dose ladder and a toxicity response tied to that dose. Trading allocations are not necessarily ordered by risk: doubling size can alter market impact, slippage, fills, and correlation. The paper’s toxicity observations are also much simpler than clustered, serially dependent, fat-tailed P&L. With delayed trade outcomes, escalating after a small batch can be dangerously premature. The transferable idea is “move one tier at a time and define hold / increase / decrease boundaries,” not the trial’s probability thresholds.

**Later work:** Clinical methods have continued to address practical complications, including [Introduction of accelerated BOIN design and facilitation of its application](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2309.08616) (2023) and [BE-BOIN: A Dose Optimization Design Accommodating Backfill and Late-Onset Toxicity](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11333) (2025). Late-onset toxicity is a useful reminder for trading: do not count unresolved outcomes as reassuring evidence.

## A concrete policy for one newly promoted strategy

The following is an **operational proposal**, not a ladder tested in those papers. Define 100% as the strategy’s intended risk budget after promotion. Keep the strategy’s cap separate from the book’s aggregate cap.

**Use six live allocation tiers: 5%, 10%, 20%, 35%, 60%, and 100%.** The first allocation should be small enough that a complete strategy failure at 5% is tolerable under the book’s loss budget. Do not step up on calendar time alone.

At each tier, require all of the following before advancing:

- **Two non-overlapping review windows** with fully matured P&L and execution costs. For a continuously trading system, use complete market sessions and wait for the instrument’s close/settlement and all material fees or funding to post.
- **At least 200 effectively independent trade clusters and 20 distinct sessions** at that tier. Treat trades from the same signal burst, session, or market event as a cluster rather than counting every fill as independent. These are sensible initial operating floors, not a claim that 200 trades validate rare tail risk.
- A **one-sided 95% lower confidence bound** on net performance versus the incumbent/no-trade alternative above the specified minimum edge, and a conservative upper bound on drawdown, expected shortfall, and stress loss below the strategy’s hard limits.
- No unresolved data-quality, order, exposure, or kill-switch incident.

If evidence is insufficient, **hold the tier**. Do not interpret “not proven unsafe” as “proven good.” If the candidate is still uncertain, retain the smaller allocation and keep collecting evidence.

**Ramp down faster than ramp up.** Immediately cut to zero and require human re-promotion after a hard loss-limit breach, bad marks/data, runaway orders, or an exposure-limit breach. On a soft breach—such as drawdown-budget use crossing 80%, or two completed windows with the lower confidence bound below the minimum acceptable edge—drop one tier immediately; if the 100% loss budget is breached, stop. After a soft reduction, impose a **two-session cool-down** before another increase, and require one clean review window to hold and two clean, non-overlapping windows to re-risk. After a hard stop, remain in shadow until a human approves re-promotion and the strategy has cleared the 5% tier again. A delayed or unresolved loss can trigger a cut, but it must not count as positive evidence for stepping up.

## How to change the policy for the whole book

Do **not** ramp each strategy independently and assume the book is safe because every strategy passed its own gate. For book-level decisions, aggregate risk first: current and stressed loss, liquidity, factor and instrument concentration, and correlated strategy exposures. Measure the book against a stable portfolio baseline, with explicit drawdown and tail-loss limits in addition to a cumulative performance floor.

Use the same six tiers for **new book-level risk**, but require simultaneous qualification at both the strategy and portfolio levels. Limit each book-level increase to the smallest change that keeps stress loss inside the approved portfolio budget; in correlated clusters, treat several strategies as one exposure for gating. On a book soft breach, pause all increases and reduce the most correlated or highest marginal-risk strategies first. On a hard book breach, invoke the book kill-switch even if each strategy is individually “within limit.” Allow de-risking immediately; require **two clean portfolio review windows** before restoring aggregate risk. This asymmetry—fast cuts, slower adds—is the most important practical lesson to carry over.

## Turn 3 (follow-up b: asset classes, growth kept vs given up, trading ideas and combinations, model: Smart) - answer

Use **different ramp clocks by instrument, but one shared portfolio risk gate**: size steps should follow executable liquidity and loss capacity, while promotion evidence should wait for *matured, independent* outcomes. The papers support conservative exploration and sequential testing; they do **not** establish a universal safe allocation ladder or a cross-asset return-versus-drawdown curve.

## 1. Make the ladder instrument-aware

First define risk in a common unit—such as stressed loss or expected shortfall at a specified horizon—not nominal dollars, lots, or shares. For each proposed step, round the target **down** to tradable units, estimate its participation and market impact at the intended execution times, then re-check the portfolio’s aggregate exposure. Minimum order sizes, contract multipliers, tick values, trading hours and margin rules vary by venue and product; do not hard-code a generic “lot.”

| Market | How I’d adapt the ramp |
|---|---|
| **FX** | For liquid major pairs, use smaller *notional* increments only if they remain above venue minimums and below the strategy’s spread/impact budget. Gather evidence by session and pair: London, New York, Asia and rollover conditions are not interchangeable. Spot FX is generally 24/5, not 24/7; account for weekend gaps, rollover/funding, holidays and liquidity changes around session boundaries. Combine pair positions into currency-factor exposure: several USD pairs are not independent tests. |
| **Stock indices** | Prefer the smallest practical exposure—such as micro futures where available, or appropriately sized ETF positions—then increase in integer-contract steps. Separate regular-session evidence from overnight futures evidence; news, macro releases and cash-market closes create gap risk. At each step measure depth and expected impact in the actual execution window, not just daily volume. |
| **Metals** | Treat gold, silver and other metals as separate risk buckets; do not infer one metal’s liquidity or gap behavior from another’s. Round futures exposure to valid contracts, account for delivery/roll constraints, and test both active-session and thinner-hours execution. For precious metals, include overnight news and currency-rate exposures; for other metals, include contract-specific supply/event risk. |
| **Energy** | Use the slowest ramp and smallest feasible exposure. Contract expiry, delivery rules, inventory releases, geopolitical shocks and price-limit or liquidity events can make yesterday’s spread and volatility poor guides to tomorrow’s exit. Avoid stepping up across a roll, inventory release or other predeclared event window unless that regime has its own evidence. A backtest with continuous futures must model rolls and contract-specific economics. |
| **Crypto** | Monitor continuously because there is no daily close, but do **not** treat 24/7 observations as independent evidence. Use event/session blocks and separate weekday/weekend, venue and funding regimes. Step sizes must respect exchange minimum notional and quantity increments; reduce exposure for thin books, funding spikes, liquidation cascades, exchange outages and weekend gaps in depth. Include counterparty and venue limits in the risk cap. |
| **Futures generally** | Risk is per contract: integer lots, multiplier, tick value, margin and expiry all matter. Use smaller contracts if available; if one minimum contract exceeds the approved loss budget, the correct allocation is **zero**, not a rounded-up position. Model rolls, first-notice/delivery restrictions, exchange halts and overnight gaps. Re-estimate tradable capacity at every size tier. |
| **Single stocks** | Start smaller and demand evidence across distinct names or market regimes—not just many trades in one name. Whole-share minimums can make a small-risk tier infeasible; fractional shares may not be available in the execution venue or shorting/financing setup. Earnings, corporate actions, halts, borrow recalls and overnight gaps justify event-specific reductions or a no-hold rule. Aggregate sector, issuer and common-factor exposure across strategies. |

**At every tier, size to the tightest constraint:** instrument-specific loss budget, estimated impact, available depth, operational limits, and remaining book capacity. A trade-size ladder is not credible if the next rung pushes the order into a different execution regime. Estimate costs from fills or order-book data at each intended size—not by multiplying tiny-size backtest costs linearly.

## 2. Growth kept versus safety bought—and what might be strategies

The main performance lesson is conditional: conservative methods can keep most of the growth **when the baseline is already reasonably good, the edge is persistent, and evidence accumulates quickly**. They can give up substantial growth when the baseline is weak, candidate uncertainty is high, the safety allowance is tight, or the strategy needs many observations to demonstrate an edge. There is no paper here that quantifies that tradeoff on your multi-asset book.

For one scenario family, high-toxicity risk was 14.8% versus 16.9% and poor-allocation risk 28.7% versus 43.0%. That shows how explicit hold/escalate/de-escalate bands can reduce bad decisions without using more average observations; it does not estimate returns forgone in trading. [Simulation Table](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1309.5019v1?page=37) |

### Market ideas present in the papers

These are **testable hypotheses**, not recommendations to deploy them live.

- **Gold futures trend/momentum:** [Forecast-to-Fill: Benchmark-Neutral Alpha and Billion-Dollar Capacity in Gold Futures (2015–2025)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.08571) tests smoothed trend and momentum, volatility targeting, ATR exits and fractional-Kelly sizing on gold futures, with daily settlement inputs and explicit execution costs. Its abstract reports Sharpe 2.88, but the paper’s own results distinguish a realized-volatility series with roughly 2.65% CAGR and 0.91% volatility from a volatility-normalized 15%-target series. Treat its very high normalized return and drawdown claims cautiously: those are not the same realized-capital result, and the paper itself notes simplified, static friction assumptions and a single-asset scope. If adapting the idea, test settlement/roll data, executable fills, changing spread/impact and non-overlapping periods. [Reported Performance](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.08571v1?page=26) [Friction Limits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.08571v1?page=24)
- **Short-dated SPX put writing:** [Sizing the Risk: Kelly, VIX, and Hybrid Approaches in Put-Writing on Index Options](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2508.16598) tests 0–5 DTE SPXW puts, moneyness, realized-volatility estimates and VIX-based size scaling. It reports that far-OTM short-dated configurations often had higher information ratios and lower drawdowns than near-the-money variants in its study, while near-the-money configurations could have very large drawdowns. The out-of-sample period highlighted is 2024, just one year and not an extreme-dislocation sample; option-writing’s short-put tail risk is not made safe by a high historical Sharpe. Use option surfaces, greeks, implied/realized volatility, spread/depth, fills, margin and crash scenarios—not just VIX rank. [Out-of-Sample Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2508.16598v1?page=20)
- **Calendar effects:** the 2026 promotion-test paper includes turn-of-month, first/last-three-trading-days, weekday, quarter-end, January and third-Friday rules on ETF and industry panels. None of the 24 candidates crossed its funded promotion threshold; one reversed-industry January rule promoted under repeated unadjusted testing and then lost after costs in the full shadow path. That is a warning against promoting calendar patterns from repeated peeking, not evidence for a calendar strategy. [Market Replay](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=5)

## 3. Combinations I’d actually build

The combinations below are engineering proposals built from methods with complementary jobs; the individual papers do not prove the combined system’s end-to-end financial performance.

1. **Conservative UCB + Reserve-C4B + anytime-valid promotion tests.** Use [Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282) for the cumulative live-risk budget, [Reserve-C4B](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39106) to avoid needlessly pessimistic candidate/baseline comparisons, and [Promote](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) for the adaptive-research false-promotion gate. The first two answer “may this strategy trade at this size now?”; Promote answers “was this frozen strategy definition justified for promotion at all?” Keep the evidence ledger separate from the live-risk ledger, and require evidence strictly after the strategy definition is frozen. This combination addresses optional stopping and baseline uncertainty, **not** alpha decay after promotion.

2. **Promotion test + alpha-decay/change detection + funded revalidation.** Pair [Promote](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) with [Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/0710.3742). Promotion evidence should accumulate on post-registration, cost-adjusted outcomes; a separately monitored change-point alarm should reduce or suspend risk when the return/cost distribution shifts. After a change signal, re-enable only through a fresh, pre-funded validation epoch using new evidence—not by resetting an old test or recycling its accumulated evidence. This separation matters because the promotion paper explicitly warns that historical evidence does not certify current profitability.

3. **Conservative sizing + uncertain-edge sizing + drawdown control.** Combine [Conservative Contextual Linear Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1611.06426) or Reserve-C4B with the fractional-Kelly and volatility-targeting components in [Forecast-to-Fill](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.08571), then add a drawdown controller such as [On Drawdown-Modulated Feedback Control in Stock Trading](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1710.01503). The bandit determines whether and how much exploration the baseline budget permits; uncertainty-shrunk fractional Kelly sets the size inside that allowance; the drawdown controller throttles portfolio risk as losses accumulate. Do **not** plug a noisy point estimate of edge into full Kelly: estimation error and fat tails can turn nominal growth maximization into excessive leverage. A simple alternative to full Kelly is to size from a conservative lower confidence bound on edge and cap by volatility, liquidity and stress loss.

4. **Risk-off regime detection + an always-on hard risk layer.** Pair [Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/0710.3742) with the safety-filter architecture in [Provably Safe, Yet Scalable Reinforcement Learning](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.14536) as an architectural analogy: the regime detector proposes a lower risk budget; a deterministic exposure filter enforces book, instrument, margin and loss caps regardless of what the signal or agent proposes. Add a kill switch on data integrity, order control and hard risk-limit breaches. Re-enable after a hard stop should require human approval plus a new small-tier qualification, not merely the regime indicator returning to normal. The RL paper’s formal safety is for modeled robotics dynamics, so its financial analogue would need separate validation and conservative model-error treatment.

5. **Correlated-strategy testing + portfolio-level conservative gating.** Use an agent-based market simulator as a *test environment* for execution and crowded/correlated failures, then apply the same portfolio-level conservative gate to the resulting candidate book. The simulator should vary liquidity, spreads, order-book depth, latency, volatility shocks, correlated strategy exits and venue outages; pass only after stress and replay testing. I don’t have a retrieved, verified market-simulation paper here that would justify a specific simulator choice or quantify the benefit, so this is a testing recommendation, not a paper-backed performance claim. Simulation should challenge live-risk limits; it cannot replace live, size-specific evidence.

For the **whole book**, promotion and size gates must use portfolio marginal risk, not isolated strategy scores. Aggregate correlated positions by instrument, currency, sector, factor, venue and event exposure; evaluate stress loss and liquidity under simultaneous de-risking. During a risk-off or changepoint alarm, freeze all adds and cut the strategies with the largest marginal stressed-loss contribution first. Restore exposure more slowly than it was cut, and require both strategy-level and book-level evidence to pass. A system can be conservative strategy by strategy and still be unsafe as a portfolio if the strategies share the same failure mode.

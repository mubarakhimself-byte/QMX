## Turn 1 (topic question, model: Smart) - answer

The strongest 2026 lesson is that **more search can improve a backtest while weakening the evidence for it**: a harness should count every candidate evaluation, make future information inaccessible by design, and keep final out-of-sample results out of the agent’s feedback loop.

### What the systems do—and what their results establish

Current systems span several useful design patterns, but their performance figures are not apples-to-apples: markets, horizons, portfolios, costs, and test-window lengths differ.

### What to copy into your harness

**Keep proposal, evaluation, and approval as separate capabilities.** Let research agents read approved, point-in-time data and submit a typed strategy artifact to a deterministic evaluator. They should have no broker credentials, order APIs, secret access, or permission to promote their own output. Make the evaluator the sole route from candidate to result; this makes the experiment count auditable and the agent’s action space enforceable, rather than relying on prompts such as “don’t use future data.” The leakage-safe design in *What survives honest evaluation?* makes this distinction concrete: the paper’s agent cannot express a feature excluded from its registry. [Validated Tools](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=4)

**Make the evaluation chronological, costed, and point-in-time.** Enforce as-of joins for every field, publication delays for news and fundamentals, survivorship-aware universes, and a fixed signal-to-order lag. Backtest against the actual venue and instrument conventions relevant to each strategy—fees, spread, slippage, market impact, borrow, funding, roll, financing, and rejected or partially filled orders. Use development folds for search, a validation window for selection rules, then an untouched sealed test; test feedback must never return to the agent. Separately run a prospective paper-trading period before any human considers promotion.

**Count the search, not just the winner.** Store every candidate evaluation, including syntax failures, rejected candidates, abandoned branches, and unsuccessful runs. Record its parent(s), hypothesis, prompt/tool trace, model and version, model knowledge-cutoff evidence, code and dependency hashes, data snapshot, point-in-time universe, parameters, random seed, split, cost model, metrics, and selection decision. Keep an append-only run ledger and calculate selection-aware statistics against the full search, not only the survivors. A pre-registered single hypothesis and a thousand-variant search should not face the same evidential bar; the search-aware evaluation paper explicitly ties the correction to recorded trial count. [Search Ledger](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=5)

**Treat model-training contamination as a different failure from code leakage.** A causally correct feature pipeline does not stop a model from having seen the test period, a strategy, or an unusually successful public backtest in pretraining. Pin model snapshots; record provider-documented cutoffs and retrieval sources; disable unlogged browsing; and timestamp or quarantine external text. For decisive evidence, freeze the model and research protocol before collecting a genuinely prospective test window. Keep training-data contamination audits separate from DSR/PBO and temporal code audits: an extreme future-informed signal can survive statistical corrections.

**Use role specialization without making critics the safety boundary.** A practical team might have an idea proposer, an implementation agent, a diagnostic critic, and a portfolio/risk analyst. Let symbolic search or evolutionary operators handle cheap local variations; use LLM calls for hypothesis changes and explanations. Keep a champion and diverse archive rather than automatically replacing the incumbent after each better-looking backtest. Critics can flag semantic drift, suspiciously complex formulas, and weak costs, but deterministic tests—not another model’s judgment—should block invalid data access or execution.

**Make human approval a distinct, signed promotion step.** The person should review an immutable, reproducible candidate package: thesis, code hash, data and split, full search history, net-of-cost results, uncertainty and multiple-testing results, drawdown/exposure, failure modes, and proposed limits. Approval should authorize only that exact artifact and a bounded paper/live configuration; any material change means a new candidate and a new review. A 2026 deployed-system study, [*AllPath*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26662), illustrates an approval queue and versioned strategy files, but it is an operational case study—not evidence that an alpha-discovery system has solved strategy validation. [Human-Gated Revisions](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26662v1?page=5)

For your multi-strategy setup, I’d start with **one read-only research harness, a typed strategy DSL, a deterministic costed backtester, a complete append-only experiment ledger, and a human-signed promotion of a frozen artifact**. Add multi-agent roles or evolutionary search only after that evaluation boundary is working; otherwise, greater search throughput mainly increases the number of ways to select noise.

## Turn 2 (follow-up a: top three harness papers and a minimal harness workflow, model: Smart) - answer

The three most useful papers for building this are [*What survives honest evaluation?* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) for the **evaluation boundary and search ledger**, [*AQuA: Recursively Self-Improving Quantitative Trading Research Agents* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) for **hypothesis-led, memory-bearing research loops**, and [*EvoQuantLab* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17130) for **evolutionary search with staged admission and overfitting diagnosis**. Their reported results are not directly comparable: the tests differ in market, horizon, period, portfolio construction, and in how completely costs and drawdowns are reported.

## 1. *What survives honest evaluation?* — build the boundary before scaling search

**Architecture, step by step.** A researcher sets the task and the permitted feature and strategy registries. The LLM does not write arbitrary code: it calls typed tools to assemble a declarative strategy plan from registered features, signal-graph nodes, and portfolio archetypes. The harness validates each call, returns structured errors for correction, runs a deterministic audit of event-publication delays, and sends valid plans to a deterministic backtester. The agent sees design-window results only; every evaluated candidate goes into a trial ledger. The search stops at its configured budget—100 candidates in the headline discovery experiment—and the selected plan is assessed on a held-out window that is never returned to the agent. The plan is JSON-like and human-readable rather than a Python strategy file. [Validated Tools](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=4) [Discovery Loop](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=5)

**Inputs and output.** Inputs include the task, market data through a controlled interface, registered features and strategy archetypes, and rotating economic hypothesis prompts. The artifact handed to a reviewer is a validated plan, plus its full iteration trace, trial ledger, run manifest, and statistical results—not just a backtest winner. The evaluation reports DSR, probability of backtest overfitting (PBO), bootstrap confidence intervals, and comparisons with benchmarks. [Search Ledger](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=5)

**Results and protections.** On the 453-stock US equity experiment, 102 recorded trials produced a best strategy with design Sharpe 1.69, held-out Sharpe 0.18, and held-out return +4.7% over 2022–25; it did not pass the paper’s DSR certification threshold. On 39 ETFs, the agent discovery returned −21% over the 2017–25 evaluation period with Sharpe −0.23. Costs included commission, spread, square-root market impact, and short-borrow financing. The paper does **not** report maximum drawdown for these discoveries in the results presented here. Its controls include point-in-time liquidity-based universe selection, delayed execution, a registry excluding look-ahead features, and trial-count-adjusted inference. Its strongest warning is that a deliberately leaky oracle still achieved DSR 1.00: statistical corrections do not repair contaminated inputs. [Agent Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=8) [ETF Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=10) [Leakage Test](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=7)

**Where it falls short.** It does not establish that the LLM’s pretraining data could not contain information about the historical test period; model-cutoff or training-corpus contamination is not audited in the retrieved results. The authors disclose survivorship limitations in the stock universe and that DSR’s trial assumptions and PBO’s estimates have limitations. Four-year tests may lack power for modest edges; the paper uses a longer nine-year ETF window for that reason. Compute expense is not quantified in the results summarized here. [Limitations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=13)

## 2. *AQuA* — make research memory useful, but keep the experiment contract fixed

**Architecture, step by step.** A manager reads the research goal, prior run records, and accumulated memory, then routes work through six sequential specialists: Data Steward, Visual Analyst, Idea Miner, Factor Evaluator, Backtest Engineer, and Research Librarian. The agents do not call one another directly; handoffs pass through the manager. The Idea Miner states an economic mechanism, expected direction, and falsification conditions before construction. Factor candidates are composed from a registered operator vocabulary; evaluators check predictive strength, stability, regimes, turnover, complexity, correlation with existing factors, and baseline comparisons. The librarian records successes and failures so later runs can revise hypotheses. A separate system searches model configurations through a restricted DSL. The paper does not specify one universal natural stopping rule; the harness is operated in bounded research runs, and the paper shows 20 factor-research epochs. [Specialist Workflow](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=7) [Memory Loop](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=9)

**Inputs and output.** The factor track receives a research goal, registered market data/features, and research memory; its outputs are factor expressions, a selected/combined crypto signal, and structured experiment records. The model track receives a task and a sealed sandbox, then emits one configuration diff per experiment. The eventual artifact is a factor or model plus its evidence and persistent research record. The paper describes a human operator who sets goals, owns the sandbox, and supervises promotion; it does not give the agent an order-execution or money-moving role.

**Results and protections.** For US equities, the model track reports a held-out 2021–25 dollar-neutral Sharpe of **+2.50** at a two-leg turnover cost of 2 bps, and about **+2.00** under fully causal walk-forward evaluation. The paper does not report maximum drawdown or a headline total return for this strategy in the cited result table, so those cannot be filled in. The crypto factor track reports a combined validation IC of about 0.190; that is not an out-of-sample net return. The model track trains on 2010–19, leaves 2020 as an embargo gap, and tests on 2021–25. A fixed data pipeline, registered DSL, and sealed evaluator restrict what the agents can change. [Equity Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=15) [Temporal Split](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=11)

**Where it falls short.** The paper documents a subtle intraday look-ahead bug that both the generating agent and an LLM reviewer missed: a feature used current-day total volume, including bars after the signal time. Its response—restricting the agent to causal registered operators—is stronger than relying on an LLM reviewer. But final-test isolation is partly governance-based, not a hard technical barrier; the paper calls for better matched-budget comparisons to isolate memory’s contribution. It tests one equity market/horizon in the model track and one crypto setup in the factor track, and the reported cost is a simple 2 bps turnover assumption rather than a full instrument-specific execution model. Model pretraining cutoff exposure to historical test information is not established in the cited results. [Leakage Failure](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=23) [Test Isolation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=15)

## 3. *EvoQuantLab* — use GP for cheap search and freeze each evidence protocol

**Architecture, step by step.** Each research cycle uses a frozen task protocol: data contract, typed terminals and operators, metrics, and search budget. An LLM proposes mechanisms and candidate knowledge; a typed genetic-programming engine explores factor trees with selection, crossover, and mutation. It keeps family-diverse elites, removes duplicates and highly correlated candidates, and injects candidates from underrepresented families. A staged L0–L6 pipeline handles data assembly, factor creation, search, economic falsification, stress tests, admission, and portfolio construction. After a cycle, an outer diagnostic loop checks train-to-development degradation, cross-seed stability, complexity, concentration, leakage, and search multiplicity. It can propose a versioned protocol patch for the **next** cycle only; completed results are not changed. Search is bounded by the cycle’s declared budget, though a universal fixed candidate count is not stated in the main results summarized here. [Factor Search](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17130v2?page=4) [Outer Diagnosis](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17130v2?page=5)

**Inputs and output.** Inputs include a market task, point-in-time data, registered primitives/operators, and economic reference material. The search returns typed factor trees; finalists get falsification and stress tests, then an admitted, versioned artifact with implementation, data version, terminal manifest, metrics configuration, and hashes. The paper’s admission gate is a research-validation gate, not evidence of a human’s final live-capital approval.

**Results and protections.** The authors report execution-aware, costed annualized returns of 18.34% on CSI 500 and 21.30% on S&P 500, with maximum drawdowns −4.78% and −5.82%, respectively. These are short OOS windows—roughly May to mid-August 2026—so annualization magnifies a few months of realized performance. For hourly crypto, the paper reports 37.91% annualized return, 7.17% cumulative return, and −3.23% maximum drawdown over April 1–June 9, 2026. The protocol uses point-in-time data, prefix-replay invariance, development folds, and physically withheld OOS data; it also applies regime, delay, residualization, and permutation checks. The one-year single-factor transfer audit reports 12, 36, and 49 candidates for crypto, CSI 500, and S&P 500, respectively. [Equity Metrics](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17130v2?page=6) [Crypto Metrics](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17130v2?page=7) [Transfer Audit](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17130v2?page=18)

**Where it falls short.** Short OOS windows are the central limitation for the eye-catching annualized numbers. The results are presented as execution-aware and costed, but the precise cost schedule is not in the material available here, so do not assume its costs match your FX, futures, crypto, or stock execution. The trial counts in the transfer audit are not necessarily the full count of every search evaluation, and the paper’s protocol describes a matched-budget null audit rather than the full DSR/PBO-style accounting in *What survives honest evaluation?*. It compares models using a shared Claude Opus 5 foundation model, but does not provide a pretraining-cutoff audit for that model. The inner GP loop reduces LLM calls, but the total compute-cost figures are not available in the results reviewed here.

## What later work adds or challenges

These are mostly contemporaneous 2026 papers, so I would **not** claim a direct citation lineage without evidence. They are useful as independent challenges to how agent results are evaluated:

- [*Quant-Arena: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Quantitative Trading Research* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30113) challenges the inference that iterative refinement reliably produces investable strategies: in its sealed 2024 A-share test, only one of 38 iterative model/effort configurations beat the gross equal-weight market reference, and iteration improved returns for only four of nine models. Its five-candidate cap, sealed year, costed cash-account evaluator, and recorded submissions are useful complements to open-ended search. [Sealed Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.30113v1?page=5) [Iteration Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.30113v1?page=8)
- [*Auditing LLM Alpha-Factor Mining: Metric Provenance and Full-Cohort Reanalysis* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29500) challenges claims based on incomplete candidate retention or inconsistent metric populations. Its audit found that a retained positive-only catalog could not support the original zero-reversal claim; its remedy is to preserve the entire cohort, failures included, and state precisely which candidates each reported metric averages. This is an audit of a preserved project, not a direct refutation of the three selected papers. [Outcome Retention](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.29500v1?page=3)

## Minimal complete harness for your system

For a human-promoted, multi-strategy system, I’d implement the following as one sequential pipeline. The research service should have **no credentials or network route to a broker**; the human promotion service should accept only a frozen, signed candidate artifact.

1. **Register the hypothesis before testing.** The proposer submits a short thesis, expected direction and horizon, target instruments/universe, entry/exit or factor definition, rationale, falsification tests, and intended holding/turnover range. Assign a hypothesis ID and timestamp it before the first backtest.
2. **Freeze the experiment contract.** The harness—not the agent—sets the data snapshot, point-in-time universe, feature and label definitions, venue calendar, decision/execution lag, development folds, costs, risk limits, metrics, candidate budget, and final OOS dates. Pin the model snapshot and log its documented knowledge cutoff. Do not let the agent browse unlogged web sources.
3. **Generate and implement through a constrained interface.** Have the agent emit a typed factor expression or strategy DSL, not unrestricted code with data-file access. If code is necessary, sandbox it with read-only, date-scoped data APIs, no outbound network, no secrets, resource limits, and deterministic package versions. Run static checks and replay-prefix causality tests before any performance evaluation.
4. **Evaluate in stages.** Run unit/synthetic tests, then development folds with realistic fees, spread, slippage/impact, borrow/funding/roll, partial fills, and latency assumptions. Provide the agent only pre-declared development diagnostics. Require robustness across folds, regimes, delay/cost stress, simple baselines, and placebo or block-permutation checks; preserve each failed result.
5. **Refine within a budget and ledger.** Each distinct backtest—including invalid candidates that reach evaluation, code repairs that change logic, branches, and parameter variants—gets an immutable trial ID and counts toward the search budget. Keep a champion and diverse archive; stop at the declared budget, or earlier on no improvement/diversity collapse. Never send final-test metrics to the agent. Compute DSR/PBO or an appropriate multiple-testing correction from the complete ledger.
6. **Freeze and independently validate.** Hash the selected code/DSL, configuration, model ID, data snapshot, dependencies, and full trial ledger. A separate deterministic evaluator runs the sealed chronological OOS window once and reports net return, Sharpe, maximum drawdown, turnover, capacity, costs, exposures, and uncertainty. If you inspect and iterate based on that result, it is no longer untouched OOS; start a new holdout or prospective test.
7. **Run a paper-trading qualification period.** Use the production feed, order simulator, and risk limits, but no live funds. Compare expected and realized fills, slippage, rejected orders, stale signals, and strategy behavior. This tests implementation and execution assumptions that a historical backtest cannot.
8. **Hand the human a promotion packet—not an order.** Show the pre-registered thesis, immutable artifact hash, all tried candidates and failures, fold and OOS results, cost sensitivity, drawdown and exposure, capacity estimate, leakage checks, model cutoff, and paper-trading record. The human explicitly promotes that exact version into a separately controlled deployment system. Any code, data, model, or parameter change creates a new version that must repeat the relevant gates.

That design borrows AQuA’s bounded research memory, EvoQuantLab’s staged diversity and falsification, and *What survives honest evaluation?*’s non-negotiable principle: **the complete search history is part of the experiment, and the system must never be able to hide it.**

## Turn 3 (follow-up b: asset classes, performance, discovered ideas and combinations, model: Smart) - answer

The clearest practical conclusion is to use **asset-specific execution models inside one shared research-governance harness**: the same proposal ledger, leakage boundary, search accounting, and human promotion gate, but different data contracts, costs, calendars, and minimum evidence requirements by market.

## 1. How the harness should differ by asset class

No universal “minimum history” makes a strategy valid. Separate **feature warm-up**—enough past bars to calculate a signal—from **evidence history**—enough independent market conditions to judge whether an edge persists. More frequent observations do not automatically replace exposure to different regimes; overlapping returns and clustered events reduce the effective sample size.

The table gives practical starting requirements for *research design*, not guarantees or regulatory standards. Calculate costs from the actual venue, broker, instrument, order size, and holding horizon; do not infer them from a paper’s generic bps assumption.

| Market | Point-in-time data and signals to allow | Execution and session details to simulate | Main false-discovery traps | Practical evidence floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FX** | Timestamped bid/ask quotes and trades, venue-specific liquidity, swap/roll points, trading holidays, and publication-time macro releases. Permit carry, momentum, volatility and order-flow features only if their source timestamps and release times are known. | Bid/ask—not mid-price—fills; spread by pair, session and volatility; commissions, slippage, market impact, financing/swap, rollover and weekend gaps. Model the Sunday open, daily rollover and holidays. OTC FX has no single consolidated tape, so identify the feed/venue. | Trying many pairs, lookbacks, session filters and event definitions; mistaking macro release revisions or time-zone misalignment for predictive information; overstating fills on stale or mid quotes. | Warm-up: enough bars for the slowest feature. As an initial research gate, seek **multiple years and several policy/volatility regimes**, with **at least 3–5 years of OOS evidence** for slower signals. Intraday systems need many more independent sessions and a prospective latency/slippage check. |
| **Stock indices** | Point-in-time index membership and weights, futures/index prices, dividends, constituents where used, and timestamped macro data. Include overnight futures and cash-market observations as distinct sessions. | Index futures: exchange tick/fee schedule, spread, order-book liquidity, price limits, session breaks, settlement and expiry rolls. ETFs: bid/ask, commissions, premiums/discounts, dividends and cash drag. Simulate overnight gaps and different cash/futures calendars. | Small effective number of independent macro regimes; trying many index, volatility and macro filters; structural changes in monetary policy; treating an ETF or futures result as interchangeable with a cash index. | For daily swing signals, target **10+ years of diverse data where available**, with several distinct volatility/rate regimes. A shorter sample should be treated as preliminary and paired with prospective paper trading. |
| **Metals** | Separate spot, futures and ETF data; timestamped prices, inventory, lease/financing where relevant, currency, and time-stamped supply/demand releases. For futures, use point-in-time contracts rather than a hindsight-adjusted continuous series for P&L. | Futures commissions, exchange spread/impact, expiry and roll mechanics, delivery constraints, contract-specific tick values, margin and overnight gaps. ETFs add tracking error, fees and market-hours mismatch. | Long commodity cycles; structural breaks; small sample sizes for supply shocks; back-adjustment leakage in continuous futures; treating gold, silver and industrial metals as one interchangeable universe. | Prefer **multiple commodity cycles (often a decade or more for daily strategies)**. Rare supply or geopolitical events cannot be “validated” merely by having many intraday bars around a small number of events. |
| **Energy** | Contract-specific futures curves, spot benchmarks, inventories and release timestamps, weather/production data with publication delays, transport/storage constraints, and contract calendars. Maintain separate point-in-time records for each delivery month. | Contract expiry, roll rules, delivery/notice dates, limit moves, exchange halts, seasonality, large gaps, margin changes and realistic impact. Back-adjusted continuous prices are for features only if their construction is causal; P&L must use actual tradable contracts. | Extreme event dependence, seasonality, roll artifacts, limit moves and sparse tail events. A strategy can look excellent by fitting one crisis or a single contract’s behavior. | **A decade-plus and multiple seasonal and shock cycles** is a reasonable daily-strategy starting point, but even this cannot establish reliability for rare crises. Stress and scenario tests are essential; report which crisis types were actually observed. |
| **Crypto** | Exchange-specific trades, quotes, order books, funding, open interest, liquidations, listings/delistings, token migrations and chain events. Timestamp exchange-local data and distinguish spot from perpetual/futures markets. | Venue fees by maker/taker, spread, depth-based impact, funding payments, borrow/margin, liquidation rules, outages, maintenance, latency, 24/7 operation and weekend liquidity. Use exchange-specific executable prices, not a composite close alone. | Short histories; regime and venue changes; token survivorship; leakage from listings and token migrations; repeated tuning on the same bull/bear cycle; leverage and liquidation assumptions. High bar frequency can disguise little independent regime evidence. | For short-horizon signals, require **many thousands of decisions plus several years across different regimes and venues**, and always add a prospective exchange paper phase. Avoid declaring a strategy robust from a short annualized window. |
| **Listed futures** | Actual contract histories, point-in-time rolls, settlement and notice calendars, contract specifications, underlying reference markets, rates and carry. Store raw contract records, not only continuous-series bars. | Exchange commissions, bid/ask, impact by contract and time, tick sizes, price limits, expiry/roll, initial/maintenance margin, overnight gaps, settlement, delivery and forced-liquidation rules. | Roll construction, contract-specific behavior, parameter searches across contracts, leverage, and rare limit/overnight events. Treating roll yield as a free signal is a common error. | Use **multiple full contract/roll cycles and preferably a decade or more** for daily systems; for intraday systems also require many sessions at the actual execution cadence and a realistic order-book replay. |
| **Single stocks** | Point-in-time constituents including delisted names; corporate actions, splits, dividends, mergers, symbol changes, borrow availability, fundamentals/news by publication time, and exchange status. Keep point-in-time sector/index membership. | Commissions, bid/ask, spread and impact by liquidity, borrow/locate and financing, short-sale constraints, auctions, halts, price limits, partial fills, corporate actions, overnight gaps and delisting proceeds. | Survivorship and look-ahead in fundamentals; thousands of correlated stock/date trials; microcap illiquidity; event leakage; multiple comparisons across universes, horizons and factor variants. | Use **a long point-in-time panel, ideally 10–20+ years, across different regimes**, including delisted securities. For a new single-name/event idea, the effective evidence is the count of independent events—not the number of stock-days. |

These differences are why one generic “realistic backtest” is not enough. For example, [*Can AI Make Money in Crypto?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34510) used 10 large-cap crypto assets, exchange paper trading and live trading, 0.10% spot fees and 0.05% perpetual taker fees, and still found a large gap between historical results and real-money execution. Its live phase was only about four weeks, so it demonstrates the value of staged testing—not long-run profitability. [Execution Protocol](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.34510v1?page=4) [Live Outcome](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.34510v1?page=8)

**False discovery is worst where the effective evidence is weakest relative to the search.** In practice, that means rare-event energy strategies, high-dimensional single-stock searches, short crypto histories, and heavily parameterized FX/session filters are particularly exposed. This is not a universal ranking by asset class: trial count, test duration, dependence, survivorship, and the number of regimes matter as much as the label on the asset.

## 2. What the 2025–26 results actually show

“Out of sample” ranges from a sealed historical year to a prospective paper account. It is not a synonym for “proved deployable.” The strongest contrast in recent work is between promising costed retrospective tests, short sealed-window results, and evaluations that continue into prospective or real-money markets.

**Which ideas or factor families are actually evidenced?** Be careful not to turn an attractive hypothesis into a demonstrated strategy:

- [AQuA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) gives a concrete crypto **open-interest unwind → weak price rebound → missing taker-flow confirmation** hypothesis. Example single-factor ICs are about 0.026–0.037, but the paper’s headline ~0.190 is a *combined validation IC*, not a costed OOS crypto portfolio return. In its US-equity track, the outcome is a hybrid price/volume model rather than an isolated factor. [Factor Hypothesis](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841v3?page=9)
- [AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789) emphasizes original, simple, hypothesis-aligned OHLCV factors; its actual end product is a set of factors combined by LightGBM into a top-stock portfolio. Its contribution is the **generation/regularization approach**, not a proof that a named factor family will transfer to your FX, energy or crypto systems.
- [Quant-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30113) shows that an agent may find a cash/exposure filter that reduces drawdown, while still underperforming the market. That is a useful risk overlay hypothesis, not a general-purpose alpha claim.
- [EvoQuantLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17130) reports strong short-window results, but the results reviewed do not provide enough detail to name a specific factor family as a proven strategy.
- [MASS (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10278) is a portfolio-construction rather than alpha-mining design. It simulates heterogeneous investors with distinct feature access and styles, then learns an aggregation over their decisions. It reports 2023 A-share predictive metrics and a Q1 2025 leakage check, but the metrics provided here are not net-of-cost Sharpe and drawdown evidence sufficient to promote its factors directly. Treat it as a candidate **stress/diversity mechanism**, not a return guarantee. [Data and Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.10278v2?page=6)

## 3. Combinations worth building

The most useful combinations separate **discovery**, **statistical authorization**, **current viability**, **robustness testing**, and **human deployment approval**. They are complements, not interchangeable corrections.

1. **[EvoQuantLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17130) + [What survives honest evaluation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) + [Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03384): evolutionary discovery → search-aware historical evidence → anytime-valid forward promotion.** EvoQuantLab supplies economical GP search, family diversity and multi-stage falsification. The leakage-safe/search-aware design supplies typed point-in-time tools and DSR/PBO over the *full* candidate ledger. The new [**Promote**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) paper adds a lifetime testing-wealth ledger for adaptively born candidates: each frozen candidate gets a funded test on returns observed *after* its registration, and promotion is scheduled no earlier than the next period. Its market replay promoted zero of 24 calendar rules; repeated unadjusted inspection falsely crossed in 94.5% of simulated null paths, while the funded procedure had zero crossings in 3,000 paths for that fixed-family experiment. It explicitly warns that promotion evidence does not certify future profitability. [Anytime Tests](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=1) [No Future Guarantee](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=1) **Workflow:** DSR/PBO assess how surprising a *frozen historical winner* is after the search; Promote controls repeated prospective monitoring and proposal births. Do not pretend these answer the same statistical question.

2. **[AQuA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) + [Propose, Don’t Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051) + [AllPath (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26662): research memory → frozen referee → human approval.** Keep AQuA’s manager, falsifiable hypotheses and retained negative results, but let a separate anytime-valid referee—not the generator or an LLM critic—decide whether evidence crosses a pre-registered promotion bar. [*Propose, Don’t Judge*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051) reports that a frozen referee admitted 5–11 times fewer sub-threshold factors than deliberately leaky referees in its scripted comparisons; its ten-year CSI 500 study also illustrates the cost: roughly 500 trading days’ median wait for admission and a certified portfolio whose Sharpe trailed the ungated portfolio. [Referee Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051v1?page=3) [Wait Cost](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051v1?page=3) Then use *AllPath*’s principle—no agent can approve its own proposal; changes are versioned, displayed as a current diff, and approved by a human—to govern the final strategy promotion. It is an operational case study, not evidence of alpha profitability. The human gate should promote a frozen research artifact into **paper trading only**; live money remains a separate, later human decision.

3. **[AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789) + [Propose, Don’t Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27051): originality and simplicity for proposals; post-submission evidence for admission.** AST novelty, semantic hypothesis alignment and complexity penalties help reduce redundant or over-engineered factors, but none proves profitability or controls an adaptive sequence of backtest peeks. Route accepted proposals to a referee that sees only returns realized after the factor’s immutable registration. This makes the LLM useful for proposing and diagnosing while denying it the power to certify its own success.

4. **[EvoQuantLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17130) + [FORESIGHT-9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29372) + [MASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10278): search diversity → counterfactual stress → competing market views.** Preserve diverse factor families, then evaluate frozen candidates under multiple documented regimes, execution shocks and deterministic baselines. FORESIGHT-9’s nine prospective counterfactual worldlines show why: its retrospective winner stayed first in only 1/9 scenarios, and simple equal-weight or inverse-volatility controls outperformed 31 of 36 agent runs. [Worldline Findings](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29372v2?page=1) MASS can supply heterogeneous analyst/portfolio hypotheses for *stress-testing alternatives*, but not truth: its simulations are models, and should not be confused with causal market evidence. FORESIGHT’s worldlines also are **not probability-calibrated forecasts**. Use them for “does this break under this scenario?” rather than “what is the expected return?”

5. **[Promote](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) + a live alpha-decay monitor + conservative uncertain-edge sizing.** Promote controls the chance of ever promoting a null candidate under its declared bounded-score assumptions; it does not promise that a once-valid factor remains profitable. Pair it with a separately registered current-regime revalidation epoch and a decay detector. *Propose, Don’t Judge* proposes an e-detector for factor viability, reporting synthetic-decay detection delays of about 204–322 trading days and simulated false-retirement rates of 0.5–1.5% under its lower-autocorrelation tests; these are not universal operational guarantees. [Decay Detector](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051v1?page=10) Size positions from a conservative lower bound on net edge, not the point estimate of a selected backtest: use a capped fractional-risk rule, volatility and drawdown limits, and shrink toward zero as uncertainty or estimated costs rise. Treat sizing as a risk control, not a second chance to optimize on the holdout. Promote’s paper likewise separates evidence wealth from trading capital and says historical certification does not certify current profitability. [Separate Ledgers](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=3)

### What I would implement first

For your system, the best initial combination is **typed per-market research contracts + full-cohort search ledger/DSR-PBO + anytime-valid forward promotion + no broker access for agents + human-signed version promotion**. Add a prospective paper period with venue-specific execution and a decay/revalidation rule. Add counterfactual multi-agent simulation later as a *stress-test layer*, never as a substitute for real point-in-time data or prospective evidence.

The stress suite should perturb the conditions that most often make a strategy look better than it is: spreads and impact, delayed fills, gaps, funding/borrow, contract rolls, liquidity collapse, outages, halts and regime shifts. The human packet should show the entire candidate lineage and every failed trial, costed OOS and paper results, drawdown and exposure, search-adjusted evidence, current decay status, and the exact artifact hash. The agent proposes and explains; deterministic systems test; a human promotes; no agent touches money.

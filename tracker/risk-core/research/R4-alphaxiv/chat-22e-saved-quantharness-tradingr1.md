## Turn 1 (question with both papers attached via @, model: Smart) - answer

**Neither paper supplies a complete live risk-and-sizing layer.** [QuantHarness](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101) is mainly a governed process for developing and accepting strategies; [Trading-R1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420) is a model that turns mixed financial information into medium-term trade recommendations. For your human-promoted system, QuantHarness is more directly useful as a *research and validation gate*, while Trading-R1 offers candidate signals and a way to structure model outputs—not a ready-made sizing or exit policy.

## What each contributes

**QuantHarness: strategy governance, not portfolio risk management.** It makes the agent turn a request into an explicit task contract and strategy specification; bind each required input to an actual data source, field, and frequency; specify the output interface; and write an implementation plan before generating code. It then validates the candidate against the spec and interface, backtests it, and sends the evidence to a Judge for recovery, rejection, or freeze. Optional candidate optimization is budget-limited; Harness Core controls which information and tools each stage can access, which proposed transitions are allowed, and what artifacts are retained for audit and replay. [Workflow](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=3)[Governance](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=5)

That gives you useful **pre-promotion controls**: strategy-to-request fidelity, causal/data checks, bounded repair and optimization, a frozen candidate identity, and an audit trail. It does **not** specify a universal per-trade risk budget, leverage cap, portfolio-level exposure or correlation limit, stop-loss/take-profit policy, or live drawdown-triggered kill switch. Its validation/backtest stages should not be mistaken for operational monitoring after deployment.

**Trading-R1: model-generated recommendations, not a complete trading system.** It trains a Qwen3-4B model on structured market, fundamental, news, sentiment, and macro inputs, using staged supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement fine-tuning: first for thesis structure, then evidence-grounded claims, then a five-way recommendation (strong sell through strong buy). The action classes are described as mapping to portfolio weights, but the paper does not give a sufficiently specified sizing rule to use as one. It targets roughly week-long holds and describes sell signals as potentially opening shorts. [Training](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=6)[Actions](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=10)[Horizon](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=11)

Its risk contribution is chiefly **volatility-aware signal labeling and evaluation**, not a rule for controlling a live portfolio. It does not report a concrete stop, take-profit, position-sizing formula, or drawdown-control mechanism. The prompt and thesis can provide evidence for a human or downstream policy; they are not themselves a risk limit.

## Strategy ideas to extract

The clearest rule candidate in QuantHarness is the paper’s worked **daily moving-average crossover**: calculate adjusted-close MA20 and MA60; go long eligible names when MA20 exceeds MA60; score them by `max(MA20/MA60 − 1, 0)` if no score formula was specified; rank and hold up to 20 names equally weighted; execute on the next session. This appears as an example trajectory, not evidence that the rule is profitable. [Example Strategy](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=36)

Trading-R1’s most concrete candidate is a **multi-horizon, volatility-normalized momentum signal**. It smooths price with a 3-span EMA, forms returns over 3, 7, and 15 days, divides each by rolling 20-period volatility, combines them with weights 0.3, 0.5, and 0.2, then assigns action labels by percentile. Those forward returns and labels are a **training-target construction**, not a deployable signal as written: forward returns must never enter a live feature. A faithful strategy translation would compute only trailing information at decision time, set thresholds from training data or a rolling past-only window, and test the signal independently. [Signal Labels](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=9)

The broader model idea is to combine technical context with dated news, fundamentals, analyst/insider sentiment, and macro data into evidence-backed directional proposals. That suggests experiments such as news-surprise or sentiment-confirmed momentum, or using a model recommendation as a filter on a separately defined price signal. The paper does not isolate these as proven standalone alphas, so treat them as hypotheses for your strategy queue.

## Performance and what it establishes

**QuantHarness’s headline 90.72% Task Success is a reliability result, not a return.** It means successful completion under the benchmark’s execution, requirements, and runtime-evidence criteria across 97 explicit rule-based A-share tasks. For financial utility, its separate ten daily-score research tasks completed 10/10 but averaged **−3.27% annualized return, −0.219 Sharpe, −13.85% maximum drawdown, and 33.06 cumulative turnover**. The returns and Sharpe were negative; QuantHarness did better than the compared systems on those reported averages, but that is relative performance, not profitability. [Task Success](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=1)[Daily Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=32)

For its two small model-research groups, QuantHarness reported mean final-period total returns of **+1.75%** and **+1.29%**, with maximum drawdowns of **−15.32%** and **−15.93%**; each group had five tasks. The periods were chronological: train **Jan–Aug 2025**, optimize **Sep–Dec 2025**, and final **Jan–May 2026**. The final period was used once, not fed back into candidate selection. [Model Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=33)[Chronological Windows](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=29)

The shared QuantHarness financial evaluator applied costs and execution constraints: 3-basis-point commission (with a minimum), 5-basis-point sell stamp duty, 0.1-basis-point transfer fee, and default 5-basis-point slippage; signals execute after their generation bar, with liquidity, cash, T+1, and market-rule constraints. For daily scores, the default conversion was equal-weight, long-only, up to 100% gross exposure. These are materially better controls than a frictionless backtest, but they are specific to the A-share evaluator—not FX, futures, crypto, or your other venues. [Execution Protocol](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=46)

**Trading-R1 reports stronger short-period results, but on a narrow test.** Across six US equities/ETFs for **June–August 2024**, its tables show positive cumulative returns on all six, ranging from **+2.38% to +8.08%**, Sharpe from **0.86 to 2.72**, and drawdown magnitudes from **1.52% to 4.65%**. The result is not directly comparable to QuantHarness: different assets, dates, strategy task, and evaluation setup. The backtest section says the test is held out and decisions use information available through each day; it does not establish comparable, explicit transaction-cost and slippage treatment in the material reviewed here. [Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=13)[Results Continued](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=14)

There is also a **date-split ambiguity worth resolving before relying on the result**: the paper describes the training corpus as covering January 2024–May 2025, while the June–August 2024 backtest is described as held out. The paper’s text asserts that the test was excluded from training, but the stated corpus span overlaps those test dates; sample-level exclusion and any label-threshold calibration split should be verified. The signal-label algorithm uses percentiles over valid signals, so a live implementation should explicitly fit those thresholds on past/training data only. [Dataset Period](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=2)[Held Out Test](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=12)[Label Algorithm](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=31)

## Fit and gaps for your system

For a multi-strategy, multi-asset short-horizon system, I would use **QuantHarness as the development-control pattern**: strategy contract, explicit data bindings, deterministic validation, cost-aware backtest, small predeclared optimization budget, held-out final evaluation, and immutable candidate/run artifacts. Keep your human promotion step as the live authorization boundary.

Use Trading-R1 more selectively: as an **idea generator or signal component** whose output must be converted into a versioned, machine-readable proposal. Its one-week horizon and long-only/short class assumptions do not map cleanly to scalping or intraday execution; its six-name evaluation does not establish robustness across FX, indices, metals, energy, crypto, futures, or individual stocks. Long text-generation latency, changing news availability, asset-specific calibration, shorting feasibility, and unreported sizing/exits also limit direct deployment. Neither paper demonstrates cross-strategy risk aggregation, portfolio-wide exposure controls, continuous live monitoring, or a reliable drawdown stop.

## Newer related work and challenges

I did not find a meaningful later paper explicitly building on QuantHarness. For Trading-R1, these later works address gaps around its outcome-based learning, sizing, and offline evaluation:

- [Trade-R1: Bridging Verifiable Rewards to Stochastic Environments via Process-Level Reasoning Verification](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.03948) challenges reliance on noisy realized returns alone, adding process-level evidence/reasoning checks to reduce reward hacking.
- [Position Sizing as a Risk-Control Variable: A Closed-Loop GRPO Framework for LLM Trading](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11676654/) makes trade quantity, take-profit, and stop-loss explicit policy outputs. Its reported five-ticker, five-month test highlights sizing as a drawdown-control variable, addressing an important omission in Trading-R1.
- [LiveTradeBench: Seeking Real-World Alpha with Large Language Models](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.03628) argues for live streaming evaluation and portfolio-level allocation, testing concerns that a short static backtest cannot settle.
- [StockR1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.21975) couples an interpretable forecast action to a time-series decoder and rewards consistency between the stated view and numerical forecast—another challenge to treating a language recommendation as a sufficient trading signal.

For your use case, the practical synthesis is: **borrow QuantHarness’s governance, not its benchmark score; borrow Trading-R1’s candidate signal concepts, not its sizing assumptions.** Have every proposed strategy emit explicit entry, exit, size, exposure, and invalidation rules, and promote only after leakage-controlled, costed, asset- and horizon-matched validation.

## QuantHarness: a governed research process

QuantHarness separates **strategy-development reliability** from profitability: it asks whether a candidate implements the stated request, uses admissible data, executes as intended, and leaves evidence to support its acceptance. Its development workflow runs from clarifying the request through data binding, implementation, validation, backtesting, optional optimization, and a final Judge decision. [Workflow](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=3)[Testing and Review](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=4)

**Step by step:**

1. **Turn the request into a contract and specification.** The agent records its interpretation, assumptions, and unresolved details in a task contract, then writes a strategy specification describing the intended rules and constraints. If the meaning of the strategy changes later, that change must first be reflected in the specification; it cannot be smuggled in through a code repair.

2. **Bind the requirements to available data and an output interface.** Data binding maps each required input to a confirmed source, field, and frequency. Unresolved requirements stay unresolved rather than being assigned guessed fields. The output protocol defines whether the strategy emits scores or orders and which fields the backtester expects.

3. **Plan, then build.** An implementation plan states how the bound data becomes decisions. Rule-based strategies are coded to that plan. Model-based candidates use the framework’s predefined model templates, trained on data constructed from the bindings, and must emit the specified output format.

4. **Validate, then backtest.** Validation checks structural completeness, environment compatibility, declared inputs, and output format. Only a passing candidate can be backtested. The reports record failed checks as well as successful ones. A profitable-looking metric cannot override a failed requirement or execution check.

5. **Optionally optimize within a fixed protocol.** This is a bounded search for better financial quality, not an unrestricted rewrite. The task requirements, data binding, and output protocol remain fixed; proposed variants must pass validation and are compared on the optimization period using a preset objective. For the reported QuantHarness runs, the limit was three optimization evaluations and one final submission per task. The selected design is evaluated once on a separate final period; that result is not returned to the optimization loop. [Optimization Protocol](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=31)

6. **Recover, terminate, or freeze.** The Judge can send a candidate back to an earlier stage for a correctable problem, within a fixed attempt budget; terminate the run without acceptance; or recommend freezing a candidate. Harness Core checks that the required artifacts and evidence exist before committing the transition. In other words, the agent can *propose* a change of state, but cannot authorize its own acceptance. [Review and Freeze](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=4)[Lifecycle Guard](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=5)

Three Harness Core boundaries make this more than a prescribed sequence. **Information** gives each stage a tailored Runtime Card rather than the whole run history. **Action** exposes only registered tools allowed at that stage and checks calls when they are made. **Commitment** intercepts proposed lifecycle transitions and commits them only when the route and supporting evidence pass the guard. The run record retains Runtime Cards, tool outcomes, stage outputs—including failed or superseded outputs—and lifecycle decisions. It links the frozen candidate to its supporting evidence; post-run inspection can examine that record but cannot feed new measurements back into the run. [Runtime Boundaries](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=5)

That maps well to **a proposal-only research harness with human promotion**. The contract/specification, data binding, tool permissions, validation gates, fixed optimization budget, candidate identity, and immutable run record can sit upstream of your human decision. The paper’s Judge is an internal workflow reviewer, not the human; for your design, freezing should mean “eligible for human review,” not “approved for live capital.” Neither its backtest nor its artifact store is a live exposure monitor or drawdown kill switch.

### What its results say

On FinRelBench’s 97 explicitly specified rule-based tasks, QuantHarness achieved **90.72% Task Success**, versus 74.23–84.54% for the four comparison systems. Task Success requires both mechanical execution checks and semantic requirement gates; the benchmark is about faithful, evidenced development, not a profitable trading strategy. [Benchmark Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=9)

<!-- Bar Chart: FinRelBench Task Success -->

Paired stress tests support the intended mechanism: with the relevant boundary enabled, the study observed **0/30** cases of invalid information use, **0/30** unauthorized file creations, and **0/30** changed candidates being frozen. In the ablated conditions, those violations occurred in **11/30, 22/30, and 14/30** cases, respectively. For auditability, an organized artifact view recovered the requested evidence in **100%** of cases and replayed **44/44** decisions; unorganized artifacts recovered 43.2% and replayed none, while final artifacts alone recovered 27.6% and replayed none. These are controlled reliability tests, not estimates of live failure rates. [Boundary Stress Tests](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=9)[Audit and Replay](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=10)

The separate financial results underline why “reliable” does not mean “profitable.” On ten daily-score research tasks, QuantHarness completed 10/10, but mean annualized return was **−3.27%**, Sharpe **−0.219**, and maximum drawdown **−13.85%**. In two five-task model groups, its mean final-period returns were **+1.75%** and **+1.29%**, with mean maximum drawdowns of **−15.32%** and **−15.93%**. The evaluation used chronological training, optimization, and final windows and a shared A-share evaluator with explicit commissions, stamp duty, transfer fees, default slippage, and trading constraints. Those results are evidence of utility on that task set—not evidence of reliable alpha across markets. [Daily Research Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=32)[Model Research Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=33)[Market Execution Rules](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=46)

**What it costs versus what it protects:** stages, checks, bounded retries, and retained evidence add engineering and runtime overhead, and a strict optimization budget limits how much historical exploration an agent can do. That can slow candidate throughput or leave a promising variant unexplored. The protection is against a different kind of loss: accepting a strategy that quietly changed its rules, used the wrong data, or was selected after repeated peeking at the final period. For a system in which an agent only proposes strategies, that trade—some speed and search freedom for traceable proposals—is usually valuable. The paper does not show that the workflow prevents drawdowns once a strategy is live.

## Trading-R1: structured reasoning trained toward market labels

Trading-R1 is a Qwen3-4B model trained to produce a structured investment thesis and one of five actions: strong sell, sell, hold, buy, or strong buy. The training corpus contains roughly 100,000 samples across 14 equities over January 2024–May 2025, with technical data, fundamentals, news, sentiment, and macroeconomic inputs. [Data Corpus](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=2)[Backbone and Training](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=10)

Its curriculum alternates supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) across three stages:

- **Structure:** learn a consistent, professional thesis format.
- **Claims:** support analysis with evidence and sources from the supplied context.
- **Decision:** emit an investment recommendation aligned with outcome-derived labels.

For SFT targets, the authors use **reverse reasoning distillation**: a strong external model supplies a recommendation from a dated financial snapshot; other language models infer and assemble a plausible structured rationale connecting the snapshot to that answer. RFT then rewards structure, evidence/claims, and decision quality. The decision stage uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which compares sampled responses to the same input by their rewards and updates the policy toward relatively better responses. [Three Training Stages](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=7)[Reverse Distillation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=8)[GRPO Training](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=11)

### What “volatility-aware” means here—and does not mean

The paper creates five action labels from a composite of EMA-smoothed price returns over **3, 7, and 15 days**, each normalized by rolling 20-period volatility, with weights **0.3, 0.5, and 0.2**. Percentile cutoffs assign the labels. That makes the target signal’s strength comparable across volatility levels and horizons. But it is a **labeling and reward scheme**, not a position-sizing algorithm: the paper says actions map to portfolio weights but does not specify a concrete weight mapping, risk budget, stop-loss, take-profit, or portfolio exposure limit. [Volatility Labels](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=9)[Action Space](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=10)

This distinction matters for leakage. Forward returns are appropriate for constructing *training targets*; they must not be model inputs at the decision time. The label algorithm also computes percentile thresholds from the distribution of valid signals. A production implementation should estimate and freeze those thresholds using training data only, or update them using past data only—not calculate them across a dataset that includes the evaluation future.

### Evaluation and transfer limits

Trading-R1 reports a held-out backtest from **June 1 to August 31, 2024** on six equities/ETFs. In its tables, cumulative returns range from **+2.38% to +8.08%**, Sharpe from **0.86 to 2.72**, and maximum-drawdown magnitudes from **1.52% to 4.65%**. The paper states that inputs are restricted to information available through each trading day and describes time-ordering financial artifacts before they are aligned with prices. However, the backtest description does not establish a clear, comparable schedule of commissions, spread, market impact, borrow costs, funding, or slippage. A short three-month result on six names therefore does not establish that the returns survive realistic costs or later regimes. [Backtest Window](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=12)[Performance Tables](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=13)[Causal Data](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=25)

There is also a split detail to check before trusting the out-of-sample claim: the paper describes the training corpus as spanning January 2024–May 2025, while the stated test period is June–August 2024. It says that the test was excluded from training, but those stated date ranges overlap. That is not proof the test leaked into training; it means sample-level exclusion and how percentile thresholds were calibrated should be documented and verified.

**What it costs versus what it protects:** the multimodal inputs, long structured generations, model fine-tuning, and reliance on dated news and fundamentals cost compute, latency, and engineering effort. A one-week-oriented, equity-trained model is unlikely to be a sensible round-the-clock scalper without substantial redesign. The intended protection is against poorly grounded, incoherent, or volatility-insensitive recommendations—not against execution costs, tail losses, bad sizing, or correlated portfolio exposure. The output is best treated as a human-readable signal proposal or a feature for a separate portfolio/risk policy.

## What would need to change across your markets

These are adaptations for your setting, not results demonstrated by either paper.

| Market | QuantHarness adaptation | Trading-R1 adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| **FX** | Bind pair-specific quote/base conventions, sessions, calendar events, spreads, rollover, pip values, leverage and order constraints; validate at the actual trading frequency. | Replace company fundamentals with rate differentials, central-bank events, macro releases, positioning and cross-pair context. Train and calibrate by pair/regime; define position sizes in account-risk terms, not action-class labels. |
| **Stock indices** | Distinguish cash index, ETF, CFD and futures data; encode sessions, overnight gaps, contract or ETF mechanics, and execution rules. | Train on index-relevant macro, breadth, volatility and constituent context; define how “buy/sell” becomes exposure and how gaps affect exits. |
| **Metals** | Specify spot versus futures, contract units, roll calendars, currency denomination, trading hours and liquidity rules. | Replace firm-level features with rates, dollar, inventory, event and term-structure inputs; recalibrate thresholds for the instrument and horizon. |
| **Energy** | Bind the correct contract and expiry, roll method, delivery/price-limit rules and event calendars. | Add inventory releases, curve shape, seasonality and event-risk context; the equity-focused thesis and labels do not transfer as-is. |
| **Crypto** | Treat the market as 24/7; test venue-specific prices, fees, funding, liquidity, liquidation and outage handling. | Use crypto-native inputs and thresholds. Equity news cadence and a one-week equity label scheme are not evidence for short-horizon crypto signals. |
| **Futures** | Make expiry, roll, multiplier, tick value, margin, session and limit rules explicit; validate that sizing is contract-aware. | Train on futures-specific data and actions; explicitly map recommendations to contracts and risk per trade, including stop distance and gap risk. |
| **Single stocks** | Add point-in-time universe membership, corporate actions, halts, earnings calendars, borrow availability and survivorship checks. | This is the closest fit to the model’s training domain, but each name still needs point-in-time fundamentals, news timing, borrow assumptions, and validation across more than a short test window. |

For **both** systems, cross-asset use means more than swapping a ticker or feed: the data contract, calendar, execution model, costs, action semantics, and validation windows must match the instrument. QuantHarness gives you a template for enforcing those changes and recording the evidence; Trading-R1 gives you candidate reasoning and directional labels, but not the sizing, exits, or portfolio controls required to turn them into a multi-strategy risk system.

## Turn 3 (follow-up b: combinations and what to build first, model: Smart) - answer

**Build the promotion pipeline as [QuantHarness](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101) plus leakage-safe, search-aware evaluation plus an anytime-valid promotion test; put deterministic risk controls between any promoted strategy and capital.** That combination addresses three different failure modes: changing what was requested, mistaking search luck or leakage for alpha, and repeatedly checking results until something passes. It should produce *promotion evidence*, not automatic live authorization.

### Combinations I’d build

**1. QuantHarness × [“What survives honest evaluation?”](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) × [Promote](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384): a search-accountable research gate.**

- QuantHarness turns each research request into a contract, data bindings, implementation plan, and declared output format; it validates the implementation and limits optimization to a fixed budget. The candidate’s requirements and data bindings stay fixed during optimization. [Workflow](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=3)[Optimization Protocol](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26101v1?page=31)
- The leakage-safe search paper adds a tool surface that makes future-dependent features unavailable by construction, a point-in-time data protocol, and a ledger of every evaluated candidate. It uses the ledger’s actual trial count in the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) and estimates backtest-overfitting risk with PBO. It reports that statistical deflation alone did **not** catch a deliberately leaky oracle; the feature boundary and search correction are complementary. [Structural Guardrails](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=2)[Leakage Oracle](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734v1?page=7)
- Promote adds an anytime-valid, cost-aware evidence stream for each registered candidate. It freezes the rule package and costs before scoring, excludes all pre-birth returns, debits a finite testing budget for each new candidate, and begins trading no earlier than the session after a promotion crossing. This is designed for agents that generate candidates and inspect results repeatedly—not just one fixed train/test split. [Promotion Ledger](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=3)

**Combined workflow:** candidate birth → freeze code, data version, sizing and cost assumptions → run causal, point-in-time backtests → record every trial → evaluate net returns and search-adjusted evidence → allow a promotion proposal only if the predeclared gate passes → human reviews the packet. DSR/PBO answer “how much selection and overfit risk is in this completed search?” Promote answers “does repeated monitoring or adaptive candidate arrival inflate the chance of ever promoting a null?” They are related but not interchangeable.

For a multi-strategy system, the ledger should make the strategy identity canonical: not just code, but asset/universe, timing, data fields, sizing, execution assumptions and costs. Editing any of those should create a new candidate identity and a new testing allocation. That prevents a renamed or slightly altered variant from inheriting the previous one’s evidence.

**2. QuantHarness × [AllPath](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26662) × Promote: a human-gated self-modification and deployment path.**

QuantHarness governs research stages and candidate acceptance; AllPath contributes a concrete division of authority for deployment: code enforces mechanical constraints, an agent may make bounded proposals, and a human decides judgment calls. In AllPath, the reflection agent can propose strategy-rule changes but cannot change authorization or monitoring status; the proposal is checked again against the current strategy at application time. Its drawdown breaker demotes autonomous revisions to human confirmation until a human resets it. [Revision Bounds](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26662v1?page=2)[Drawdown Breaker](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26662v1?page=3)

Promote can then be the *statistical* gate for a candidate, while the separate operational gate controls whether and how much it can trade. I’d add a staged ramp that the papers do not themselves test: shadow/paper → small risk allocation → larger capped allocation → normal approved limits. Each step requires current evidence and a fresh human authorization; the agent cannot raise its own caps. Hard limits on order size, gross/net exposure, leverage, liquidity and daily loss belong in deterministic execution code, not in the strategy prompt.

This stack also clarifies what “live monitoring” should mean. A historical promotion certificate is not a certificate that an edge remains profitable now. Promote explicitly warns that old evidence can persist after an edge decays; it proposes expiring authorization and funding each revalidation epoch as a new test. That is a principled basis for periodic revalidation, but it is **not itself an alpha-decay detector**. A rolling net-edge or regime-change alarm would need its own predeclared method and validation; repeatedly resetting a test without accounting for those restarts would void the original guarantee. [Evidence Expiry](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=7)

**3. Trading-R1 × [Position Sizing as a Risk-Control Variable](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11676654/) × AllPath: separate the signal from the risk decision.**

Trading-R1 supplies the evidence-backed thesis and directional action. The position-sizing paper’s central design change is to make **quantity, take-profit and stop-loss** explicit parts of the agent’s structured action, rather than treating a Buy/Sell label as a complete trade. Its abstract reports that removing quantity returned cumulative performance to roughly Buy-and-Hold parity but worsened drawdown by about three percentage points—evidence for sizing as a path-risk control, not proof of a universal sizing rule.

The combination should not let either language model set unconstrained exposure. Treat the signal as a proposal; a deterministic risk service computes or clamps the permitted size from the strategy’s risk budget, stop distance, current volatility, liquidity and portfolio exposure, then checks stop/target and venue rules before order submission. AllPath-style authorization decides whether this is shadow, human-confirmed or pre-approved execution. A drawdown trigger should reduce autonomy or allocation independently of the model’s rationale.

For Trading-R1 specifically, its volatility-aware labels are **not** a quantity policy. The model labels actions using 3-, 7- and 15-day forward returns normalized by rolling volatility, then applies percentile thresholds; the paper says recommendations map to portfolio weights but does not specify a concrete allocation formula. Those forward returns are training targets, never live inputs; thresholds need past-only fitting. [Volatility Labels](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=9)[Action Space](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.11420v1?page=10)

**4. Trading-R1 × the leakage-safe search paper × Promote: test whether its signal adds value after costs.**

Rather than start by reproducing the full reasoning/RL training pipeline, expose a versioned Trading-R1 recommendation as one candidate feature or filter, then compare it to simple price-only and no-model controls. Freeze the model version, prompt, data snapshot, action-to-position mapping and cost model; log every attempted prompt/threshold/strategy variant; apply the same point-in-time constraints and net-cost evaluation to all arms. Promote only on fresh evidence after candidate registration. This directly tests whether the thesis or recommendation improves a trading rule, rather than whether it sounds coherent.

For your multi-asset setup, the cost model must be instrument-specific and set before testing: spread, commissions, impact, borrow and locate for single stocks, funding and liquidation mechanics for crypto perpetuals, rollover and contract multipliers for futures, and session/overnight costs for indices, FX, metals and energy. A candidate with positive gross return but negative expected net edge should fail admission. That last hurdle is an integration recommendation, not a tested rule from either attached paper.

### What the evidence says about strict gates

The gates protect against false promotion, but they can slow discovery or reject real weak signals. In the [injected-truth study of admission gates](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07701), gates reduced false discoveries in weak-signal settings but adopted only about **1–7%** of candidates; above a training-period $t$ statistic of roughly 2.5, the study found they added little over pure search. It also found that applying criteria to absolute rather than excess returns could reject true signals. [Gate Tradeoff](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07701v1?page=7)[Excess Returns](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07701v1?page=8)

Promote makes a similar power-versus-validity tradeoff: in its market replay it promoted **zero of 24** calendar-rule candidates, and at a simulated 15-basis-point component perturbation it detected the target in 70.1% of paths, with a median crossing session of 2,327. The authors explicitly distinguish control of false historical promotion from proof that an earlier edge remains profitable at deployment. [Power Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=5)[Market Replay](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384v1?page=5)

So I would make the gate **risk-tiered**, not universal: require stronger evidence for a large or irreversible allocation; allow small, explicitly experimental paper/shadow candidates to keep collecting evidence; and never silently weaken a gate because it is rejecting too many ideas. Report abstentions and rejection reasons, as well as promotions.

### First build from each attached paper

From **QuantHarness**, build the **immutable candidate-and-evidence packet** first: request/specification, data bindings, code/config hash, validation report, complete trial history, costed backtest, and human decision all tied to one candidate identity. It is the foundation for approval, search accounting, staged rollout, and later incident review.

From **Trading-R1**, build the **causal, multi-horizon volatility-normalized momentum signal as a simple candidate**, not the full RL model: compute trailing features only, set thresholds on training/past data, and test whether they add net value beyond a price-only baseline after realistic costs. It is concrete and cheap to falsify. Only if the signal survives should you invest in the much more expensive structured-reasoning and reinforcement-learning training loop.

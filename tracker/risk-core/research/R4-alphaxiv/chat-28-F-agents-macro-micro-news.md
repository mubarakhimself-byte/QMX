## Turn 1 (topic question, model: Smart) - answer

**Keep LLMs as evidence interpreters, not trading controllers:** have them emit short-lived, typed risk proposals to a deterministic service, which alone computes permitted exposure and enforces limits. The 2025–26 evidence supports testing for incremental signal value, but not assuming it: a news-language study reports net predictive value under costs, while a point-in-time portfolio study finds that gains depend heavily on analyst-signal quality and are not validated alpha.

### What the evidence says about performance

There is some measurable evidence beyond safety. In MFAST’s June 2024–January 2026 U.S.-equity evaluation, its LLaMA-3 sentiment strategy reports a 2.85 long-short Sharpe and 180% cumulative long-short return after 5-basis-point trading costs and a 10%-of-daily-dollar-volume participation cap. Those are results from that paper’s particular data, model, and portfolio design—not evidence that an LLM will add alpha to scalpers across FX, futures, crypto, or other markets. [Portfolio Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=30)

OpenPM’s result is more conditional: with its stronger frozen analyst capture, four of five LLM constructors beat equal-weighting the same candidate pool in the reported single-window comparison; with the weaker capture, none did. The authors characterize returns as single-window, no-market-impact upper bounds rather than validated alpha. [Constructor Comparison](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=5)

Macro agents look more defensible first as a *risk-context* input than as a stand-alone directional signal. LiveMacroEval reports that GPT-5’s aggregate macro score tied the Bloomberg consensus over its live evaluation, but performance varied substantially across indicators. That supports testing indicator-specific release context—not treating a generated macro narrative as a universal market regime. [Macro Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=6)

Strat-LLM likewise reports that constraints can affect performance differently by market regime: for example, GPT-5’s maximum drawdown in its U.S.-stock sample fell from 20.83% in Guided mode to 11.66% in Strict mode. That is a reason to test regime-conditioned risk controls, not to make an LLM the authority that chooses or changes live constraints. [Regime Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.06024?page=4)

For microstructure, ViperQ reports large out-of-sample returns on two stocks, but it is an RL system, not an LLM. Its authors also report substantial drawdowns: −27.5% on TSLA and −47.8% on NVDA. It motivates structured features such as order-flow imbalance and tape velocity; it does **not** show that a language agent should interpret raw book updates or make fast execution decisions. [Test Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13825?page=5)

### Recommended architecture

**Macro and regime agent.** Feed it only point-in-time macro releases, vintages, scheduled-event metadata, and already-computed market features. Ask for a compact state estimate—such as probabilities over `risk_on`, `risk_off`, `inflation_shock`, and `uncertain`—plus cited input IDs and an expiry. Keep any prose narration explanatory; do not let free-form narrative become a risk instruction. LiveMacroEval’s live pre-release setup is a useful evaluation model because historical macro targets may be memorized and revised data can differ from what was known at the time.

**Microstructure agent.** Do not send raw high-rate order-book streams to an LLM in a scalping decision loop. Compute causal, versioned features in conventional code—spread, depth imbalance, trade-flow imbalance, volatility, liquidity gaps, and venue/contract state—and let an agent summarize them only at a cadence where its latency is acceptable. The strategy or execution system should continue to use deterministic, tested rules for price-sensitive actions.

**News and event agent.** Process each item as an event record: source, article/headline ID, instrument/entity mapping, publication time, ingestion/availability time, event class, polarity or impact estimate, confidence, and duplicate/correction status. Separate *when the event happened* from *when the system could know it*. MFAST explicitly treats timestamped text, calibration, execution timing, capacity, and costs as parts of one pipeline. OpenPM gives concrete examples of availability gates: filings by acceptance time, macro series by vintage, and news only after a specified publication lag. Do not let retrieved headlines or article text issue tool instructions to other agents.

**Bounded output to the deterministic risk service.** Require schema-validated proposals such as:

```json
{
  "instrument": "…",
  "as_of": "…",
  "valid_until": "…",
  "evidence_ids": ["…"],
  "regime_probabilities": {"risk_on": 0.20, "risk_off": 0.65, "uncertain": 0.15},
  "event_flags": ["high_impact_release_nearby"],
  "signal_confidence": 0.72,
  "risk_scale_proposal": 0.60
}
```

Make `risk_scale_proposal` **de-risking only**—for example, clamp it to `[0, 1]`; never allow the agent to increase the strategy’s approved risk budget. The risk service should independently check freshness, source provenance, allowed enums, numeric bounds, aggregate portfolio exposure, event calendars, drawdown limits, and instrument/session rules. Stale, malformed, contradictory, or low-confidence outputs should be rejected or mapped to a conservative default. The LLM never supplies an order, position size, leverage, stop override, or broker/API call.

For your human-promotion model, keep this boundary explicit: agents may propose candidate strategies or risk-context outputs; the deterministic service enforces the approved live configuration; only a human promotes a strategy to live. Agent prompts, input snapshots, parsed outputs, rejection reasons, risk-service decisions, and model/version identifiers should be logged for replay.

### Evaluation that can distinguish alpha from safer behavior

1. **Enforce point-in-time data discipline.** Store event time and availability time separately. Gate every feature and text record by availability at the decision timestamp; use macro vintages, point-in-time membership/universe, and causal feature windows. For news, account for actual publication and ingestion latency, corrections, syndication, and duplicates. A headline timestamp alone is not proof that your system could have acted on it then.

2. **Replay the real decision path.** Score signals only after their true availability time, execute no earlier than the next feasible fill, and model spread, fees, slippage, latency, liquidity, and—where material—queue position and market impact. Stratify results by asset class, session, event type, volatility regime, and strategy horizon. Do not infer subsecond value from daily-bar tests.

3. **Isolate incremental contribution.** Hold the strategy, inputs, candidate set, and risk limits fixed; compare (a) no agent, (b) deterministic features only, (c) agent output, and (d) agent output plus risk service. Replay identical evidence for alternative models. Report net returns and risk-adjusted metrics alongside drawdown, tail loss, turnover, cost sensitivity, and constraint violations. Lower drawdown is a safety benefit; it is not, by itself, proof of alpha.

4. **Use forward and sealed tests.** Freeze prompts, model versions, thresholds, and feature definitions before an untouched evaluation period. Use rolling or walk-forward evaluation and a live shadow phase; measure uncertainty across dates and regimes, not just model calls. For macro agents, score against future releases that were genuinely unknown at inference time. For text agents, also test whether performance survives the model’s data-freshness cutoff and alternative execution delays.

5. **Red-team the information path and control plane.** Test fake or conflicting headlines, indirect prompt injection, stale data, wrong-entity matches, missing timestamps, contradictory event flags, and compromised upstream-agent messages. Keep risk limits outside the prompt and outside the agent’s authority: the 2026 poisoning study finds that a compromised terminal decision role can bypass upstream deliberation, so voting or adding more agents is not a substitute for a deterministic enforcement boundary.

**Bottom line:** start with agents as timestamped macro/news interpreters and risk *reducers*, and evaluate their value by paired, cost-aware, point-in-time tests. For book/microstructure features, use deterministic feature extraction and keep LLM inference out of latency-critical execution. The key result that would change this recommendation is repeatable, out-of-sample **net** improvement over the same strategies and risk service across multiple regimes and relevant asset classes.

## Turn 2 (follow-up a: top three papers and a three-agent workflow, model: Smart) - answer

**The three most useful design references are LiveMacroEval for live macro nowcasting, MFAST for timestamped news signals, and OpenPM for the deterministic risk boundary.** None is a ready-made around-the-clock multi-asset agent: the first two study specific signals, while OpenPM studies a slower portfolio workflow. Taken together, they offer a practical pattern: agents interpret evidence and propose bounded, expiring inputs; deterministic code alone decides whether those inputs can affect approved risk.

## 1. Macro: [Can LLMs Take the Pulse of the Economy? A Real-Time Evaluation of LLM Nowcasts on Macroeconomic Indicators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30110) (2026)

**What it does.** LiveMacroEval tracks 16 U.S. indicators across production, demand and inflation, labor, and housing. On an hourly schedule during each indicator’s pre-release window, a web-search-enabled model is prompted to produce numeric nowcasts. It can make multiple web searches per call and decides what to search; the prompts instruct it to gather current official releases, revisions, trackers, consensus, policy and market developments, and news. The prompt fixes each indicator’s definition, unit, target and release month, scale guardrails, and required output format. The result is a line of numeric `key=value` pairs—not a trade recommendation or a risk multiplier. It is compared with Bloomberg’s economist consensus, regional Federal Reserve nowcasts where available, and an auto-ARIMA baseline. ([Prompt and workflow details](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30110); [system prompt and template](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30110))

**Evaluation and results.** The main evaluation is live: target releases run from November 2025 through March 2026, with release dates through May 5, 2026. That prevents the *not-yet-released target value* from being retrieved during the forecast window. GPT-5’s aggregate LiveMacro Score was +0.004, statistically indistinguishable from the Bloomberg consensus. Its magnitude-weighted directional score was 0.572; that is a weighted sign-accuracy measure, not an ordinary hit rate. Results varied by indicator: the paper says all evaluated LLMs trailed the leading professional or institutional forecasters on CPI betting performance, while several were competitive on GDP and unemployment. The betting metric is a sequence of simulated $1 hourly bets on prediction-market buckets, not a conventional portfolio backtest; it does **not** provide one comparable LLM portfolio Sharpe or net trading return. [Live Score](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=6) [Betting Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=7) [Directional Score](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=39)

A later same-paper comparison used Claude-sonnet-4.5 in a live July–August 2026 window. A plain-prompt control scored −0.080; adding a financial-analysis plug-in scored −0.055, but the paired difference was not significant. An orchestrated researcher-plus-independent-verifier team scored +0.020 and got the direction right on 54% of indicator releases, versus 38% for the plain prompt. These are macro-release scores, not returns or Sharpe; the experiment does not establish that the multi-agent setup improves trading performance. [Agent Comparison](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=9)

**What it does not establish.** This is a relatively clean test against memorizing the *future official release*, but it does not prove that every retrieved item was accurate, that every inferred event caused a forecast revision, or that a macro estimate predicts tradable returns after execution costs. The authors discuss information-event examples and shared misses, including sharp month-over-month reversals that multiple models got wrong in the same direction. I found no reported latency or inference-cost measurement in the results available here. Treat its estimates as context about macro conditions—not verified event facts, and not a high-frequency trading signal. [Shared Errors](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30110?page=43)

## 2. News: [Financial Language Models as Applied Artificial Intelligence Systems for News-Based Trading under Market Frictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23703) (2026)

**What it does.** MFAST is a news-to-signal evaluation pipeline, not an autonomous web-browsing agent. It takes timestamped, firm-specific text; matches articles to firms; removes redundant same-firm coverage; runs language models as sentiment classifiers; calibrates their probabilities; aggregates article-level scores into firm-day signals; and ranks securities for a long-short portfolio. It evaluates LLaMA-3, OPT, RoBERTa, BERT, FinBERT, and a dictionary baseline. Inference text includes the headline, lead paragraph, and article body, but not prices, returns, trading volume, or timestamps. Its structured model output is a probability of a return-relevant label; after calibration that probability becomes a ranked signal. That is a much narrower and more testable role than asking a model to explain headlines and choose trades. [MFAST Pipeline](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=15)

**Evaluation and results.** The primary out-of-sample window is June 2024–January 2026, chosen to be after the cited LLaMA-3 data-freshness cutoff and release-date buffer. Articles published before market open can enter that day’s portfolio; intraday, after-close, weekend, and holiday articles are delayed to the next eligible trading day. The portfolio evaluation uses a 5-basis-point one-way cost assumption, a 10%-of-daily-dollar-volume participation cap, liquidity screens, and additional robustness and statistical checks. Its reported LLaMA-3 long-short portfolio has a Sharpe of 2.85 and 180% cumulative return; the Sharpe’s block-bootstrap 95% interval is [2.31, 3.34]. **No hit rate is reported in the results available here.** These are paper-reported results for its particular U.S.-equity portfolio construction, not evidence that the same sentiment model improves a separate multi-strategy system. [Portfolio Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=30) [Statistical Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=32)

**Operating costs and failure modes.** The paper measures compute as well as financial outcomes. Its reported LLaMA-3 setup uses 54.2 GB of peak GPU memory and processes 8.9 articles per second at 112 ms per article; the faster, smaller baselines process more articles per second. Those throughput numbers do not guarantee acceptable end-to-end latency for a live feed: ingestion, entity resolution, vendor delay, queues, and risk-service handoff still matter. The paper also says its execution model is not a full order-book simulator—it omits queue position, hidden liquidity, and strategic interaction. Its post-cutoff design reduces training-data contamination risk but does not prove an absence of all pretraining exposure. Finally, even correctly interpreted news may arrive after prices have moved; the relevant test is whether the signal survives the next feasible execution time and costs, including a deliberately delayed-signal test. [Compute Metrics](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=32) [Limitations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=36)

## 3. Risk boundary: [OpenPM: Auditable Point-in-Time Evaluation for LLM Portfolio-Management Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988) (2026)

**What it does.** OpenPM’s reference allocator follows a clear sequence: compile a mandate into typed constraints; screen to at most 50 liquid names; run six channel-specific analysts for technical, regime, event, news, 8-K item codes, and retrieved 10-K/10-Q narrative; aggregate their scores and confidence; ask a constructor LLM to propose weights; project those weights through a deterministic critic; then simulate fills and log the run. Analysts return a score in \([-1,1]\) plus confidence. The constructor proposes long-only portfolio weights. The deterministic critic enforces long-only exposure, name count, per-name and liquidity caps, cash/gross limits, and turnover scaling. It—not the constructor—guarantees feasibility. [Allocator Design](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=4)

For timing, each visible record must satisfy `ts_available ≤ decision_time`. The paper distinguishes availability time from event time and bar-end time: for example, it gates filings by SEC acceptance time, macro series by FRED/ALFRED vintage, bar features until the bar closes, and GDELT news with a publication lag. It records gate logs, proposals, projected weights, fills, contamination status, and constraint adherence. [Point-in-Time Gate](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=3) [Evidence Timestamps](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=10)

**Evaluation and results.** OpenPM’s controlled window is March 2–May 1, 2026: 44 trading days and 3,432 five-minute bars for a point-in-time S&P 500 universe. In its once-then-held comparison, constructor performance depended on the frozen analyst evidence: with the stronger GPT-5 analyst capture, four of five constructors beat equal-weighting the same candidate pool at \(K=50\); with the weaker DeepSeek-V3.2 capture, none did. In a separate 44-rebalance daily sweep with DeepSeek analysts, Opus-4.7 returned 13.7% net with Sharpe 4.32; DeepSeek-V3.2 returned 9.7% net with Sharpe 3.01. They are **single-run point estimates** from one short window, and the authors label results no-market-impact upper bounds, not validated alpha. The table does not report a hit rate. [Constructor Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=5) [Daily Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=6)

The main safety result is architectural: feasible positions are guaranteed by deterministic projection rather than trusting the LLM’s instruction-following. The critique matters too: fills use single-venue IEX top-of-book, not consolidated NBBO, and omit market impact and queue dynamics. The window is short, not a test across independent regimes. OpenPM provides point-in-time controls for visible data, but its own contamination certificate is not proof that all possible leakage has been eliminated. It does not measure a production trading agent’s latency budget or API cost. [Limitations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09988?page=7)

## Newer challenges to the optimistic interpretation

These are relevant follow-on or adversarial results, not all direct replications of the three papers:

- [The Price of Thought: Does Test-Time Reasoning Pay in LLM Trading?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30705) (2026) challenges the assumption that more elaborate reasoning improves economic outcomes. On a 2024 U.S.-equity ranking task with 241 formation dates, three models and numerical, identifiable-news, and masked-news inputs, none of nine primary low-versus-baseline-reasoning comparisons established a reliable improvement in net returns at 10 bps one-way costs. The paper also reports $415.05 total inference cost across its evaluated tasks. It is a caution against paying for longer reasoning without measuring the resulting portfolio change. [Primary Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.30705?page=7) [Inference Costs](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.30705?page=9)

- [Fake News, Real Crashes: Information Injection Attacks on LLM Financial Trading Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26438) (2026) challenges the premise that an agent can safely treat incoming news as trusted evidence. In a sandboxed study over roughly 10,000 decisions and 15 U.S. stocks, the authors report 62% mean attack success for fabricated information versus 14% for prompt-injection controls; the attack changes are not equivalent to proven realized losses, and the study is not a live-market trial. The key design lesson is to authenticate and cross-check the data plane, and cap how much any single unverified item can change a position. [Attack Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26438?page=1)

- [Measurement Boundaries in LLM Financial Agent Evaluation: Fixed-Tape Execution and Multi-Defect Auditing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32379) (2026) is a useful challenge to overconfident execution comparisons and safety-audit scores. It finds that independent runs can change both model responses and portfolio state, so replaying a fixed response tape is needed to isolate a narrower execution effect; it also shows why audit recall alone can miss false positives and incomplete findings. The paper’s results are synthetic-harness diagnostics, not evidence about which trading model is best. [Fixed-Tape Result](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32379?page=5) [Audit Limit](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32379?page=11)

## Concrete three-agent workflow

Keep the agents asynchronous and give each a separate evidence contract. Use LLMs for slower interpretation and deterministic feature extraction for latency-critical inputs. Every message should contain `as_of`, `valid_until`, provenance, and an uncertainty field. If the risk service sees stale, malformed, unsupported, or contradictory output, it ignores the proposal and uses its configured conservative default.

**Macro agent:** reads only point-in-time macro releases and revisions, vintages, scheduled-release calendars, and approved market-derived context. It emits a regime probability vector and flags upcoming high-impact events. Its expiry should be short—such as until the next relevant release or a conservative maximum TTL—and a scheduled event should trigger expiry/re-evaluation rather than relying on an old narrative.

**Microstructure agent:** receives causal, computed features rather than raw LOB text: spread, depth imbalance, trade-flow imbalance, volatility, liquidity gaps, and feature timestamps. It emits a short-lived condition such as `liquidity_thin` or `flow_dislocation`, plus confidence and a proposed *risk reduction*. A stale or late result expires before it can affect a fast strategy.

**News agent:** consumes timestamped, entity-resolved headlines and event records from allow-listed sources. It emits an event class, polarity/impact estimate, confidence, provenance IDs, and duplicate/correction status. It must distinguish publication time from ingestion/availability time. Headlines are evidence—not instructions—and one uncorroborated item should not be able to force a trade.

For example, each agent could submit a record like:

```json
{
  "agent": "news",
  "instrument": "…",
  "as_of": "…",
  "valid_until": "…",
  "evidence_ids": ["…"],
  "event_flags": ["unconfirmed_regulatory_headline"],
  "confidence": 0.62,
  "risk_scale_proposal": 0.70
}
```

The deterministic risk service—not the agents—then validates schema, timestamps, source allow-list, and instrument mapping; aggregates valid signals; clamps any proposed risk scale to **de-risking only** (for example, `[0,1]`); and applies hard portfolio, drawdown, liquidity, event, and session limits. It outputs an approved risk multiplier to the existing strategy layer, never an order or broker instruction. Failed validation, expired inputs, or loss of the service means no agent-driven change to risk. The human promotion gate remains responsible for moving strategies into live operation.

To test whether these agents add value, compare the same strategies and deterministic risk service with and without each signal source, using point-in-time replay, next-feasible execution, realistic costs, and a forward shadow period. Report net returns and risk-adjusted measures alongside drawdown, turnover, tail loss, signal expiry/rejection rates, and constraint violations. Improvement in safety counts as safety improvement; call it added alpha only if the incremental net result repeats out of sample across relevant assets and regimes.

## 1. Make the agents asset-specific

The same agent design should not be copied unchanged across these markets: **the evidence sources, event clock, and useful lifetime of a signal differ materially by asset class.** The notes below are implementation guidance; the empirical studies cited later are mostly U.S. equities or crypto, not comparative trials across all seven classes.

| Asset class | Macro agent: what to read and emit | Microstructure agent: what to read and how fast | News/event agent: useful sources and event clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FX** | Track central-bank decisions, rate paths, inflation and labor releases, and relative—not just absolute—surprises between the two currencies in a pair. Examples of source families include central-bank releases and statistical agencies; FRED can provide U.S. series, but use each jurisdiction’s official releases for the other leg. Emit pair-relative rate/inflation regime and expiry through the next relevant release. | Use the traded venue’s bid/ask, depth, spread, trade flow, volatility, and session/time-of-day features. Spot FX is fragmented and largely OTC: a single broker’s book is not the whole market. Keep broker-specific signals labeled as such; don’t imply they represent the global FX order book. | Prioritize scheduled central-bank statements, minutes, speeches, CPI/jobs/PMI releases, interventions, and geopolitical developments. A timestamped headline about one currency may affect several pairs, so map entities to currencies and pair exposures before applying risk. |
| **Stock indices** | Track policy/rates, inflation, growth, labor, and cross-market stress; make the regime relative to the index’s own constituents and country. Macro is usually a **market-wide** input, while index construction and sector composition determine exposure. | Use index-futures or ETF spread, depth, imbalance, volatility, basis, and session features. Futures and cash-index signals have different trading hours and execution venues; align timestamps and expiry to the actual traded instrument. | Scheduled macro releases and central-bank events are typically more directly relevant than individual-company headlines, though mega-cap earnings and index rebalances can matter. For event studies, compare release values to the consensus available immediately beforehand—not to the final revised series. |
| **Metals** | Emphasize real rates, dollar strength, inflation expectations, central-bank policy, and relevant physical-market context. Gold and industrial metals need different narratives: a single “metals regime” is too coarse. | For exchange-traded contracts, use contract-specific book and trade features, spread, depth, volume, and roll/basis state. Separate the active contract from the continuous series used for research. | Gold: central-bank, rates, currency, geopolitical and ETF-flow news. Industrial metals: inventory, mine/smelter supply, trade policy, and demand data. Check exchange calendars and official releases; some physical-market benchmarks and detailed data feeds are licensed. |
| **Energy** | Give inventory, production, demand, weather, refinery and policy data greater weight. EIA’s scheduled petroleum and natural-gas releases are important U.S. examples; OPEC and other official producer sources add context. Separate crude, refined products, and gas rather than treating “energy” as one regime. | Contract-specific order flow, spread, depth, volatility, calendar spreads, and delivery/roll state are important. Storage, delivery, weather, and expiry can dominate generic price trends. Use the actual venue’s timestamps and contract specs. | Schedule EIA releases, OPEC communications, weather alerts, outages, sanctions, and pipeline/refinery events. Price-impact timing can be very short; a story arriving after a report or price move should not be treated as a fresh signal. |
| **Crypto** | Many traditional macro inputs remain relevant, but add funding, basis, stablecoin/liquidity conditions, exchange-specific flows, and liquidation risk. Macro releases have no market close to anchor to. | A venue-specific, streaming agent can observe trades, book changes, spread/depth, funding, open interest, liquidations, and outages continuously. It must identify the venue and product: spot and perpetual-futures books are not interchangeable. | News flow is **24/7**, and source authenticity is a core risk. Track exchange/status notices, protocol governance, security incidents, token unlocks, regulatory announcements, and corroborated issuer statements. Deduplicate syndication and preserve first-public, ingestion, and correction times. A fabricated or manipulated post is a documented failure mode for financial agents. |
| **Futures** | Treat macro context as an input to the specific contract, not an asset class by itself: rates futures, equity-index futures, energy, and agriculture have different calendars, fundamentals, and expiry behavior. | Use contract-level order book, trades, spread, depth, queue/volume, basis, and roll information. Respect exchange session breaks, contract multipliers, expiry, delivery, and limit rules. Don’t build a continuous contract that accidentally makes future roll information available earlier. | Use the relevant underlying’s official scheduled releases, exchange notices, and event calendar. Keep the signal linked to the actual expiry and contract month; event relevance can vary across the curve. |
| **Single stocks** | Macro is usually a common risk context; company-specific earnings, guidance, filings, and corporate actions often matter more to the name-level signal. Model sector and market exposure separately from idiosyncratic evidence. | Use venue and consolidated quote data where licensed, plus spread/depth/imbalance, trade flow, volatility, and auction/session state. For a scalper, an LLM should not be in the order-book decision loop; compute features deterministically. | SEC EDGAR provides public filings, including acceptance timestamps; earnings releases, company guidance, regulatory actions, analyst revisions, and verified news are relevant. Article-to-ticker mapping, duplicate coverage, and whether a headline was available before the next feasible trade are critical. |

**Low-cost and legal data approach:** start with official macro/statistical releases and calendars, public filing APIs such as SEC EDGAR, and exchange or broker APIs for the specific venue you trade. Public access does **not** automatically grant redistribution or commercial-use rights; market-data display, storage, derived-data, and automated-trading terms vary by venue, vendor, and jurisdiction. For order-book research, free or delayed data may be inadequate for the live latency being claimed. Use vendor timestamps and entitlement checks, and retain a record of the applicable license and data version.

A useful common envelope across classes is: `instrument`, `venue`, `event_time`, `available_time`, `as_of`, `valid_until`, `source_ids`, `confidence`, and bounded `risk_scale_proposal`. Add class-specific fields—such as FX currency pair and relative surprise, futures contract month, crypto venue/product, or stock ticker and filing accession number—rather than forcing all agents to narrate into a generic “market mood.”

## 2. What the 2025–26 evidence says about performance

The most persuasive positive result is for **news classification converted into a tested equity signal**; macro studies show forecast skill but not yet a comparable, costed trading strategy. Several other papers measure risk control or live execution rather than incremental alpha.

| Evidence | What improved—and what did not | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| [Financial Language Models as Applied Artificial Intelligence Systems for News-Based Trading under Market Frictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23703) (2026) | Its LLaMA-3 news strategy reports **180% cumulative long-short return and Sharpe 2.85**, net of the paper’s 5-bp one-way cost and subject to a 10%-of-daily-dollar-volume cap. The reported block-bootstrap Sharpe interval is [2.31, 3.34]. This is the clearest reported example here of language-based news features contributing to a return strategy, not merely lowering risk. The paper acknowledges no full order-book/queue simulation and that pretraining exposure cannot be ruled out completely. Its LLaMA-3 inference is reported at 112 ms/article, or 8.9 articles/s, with 54.2 GB peak GPU memory. [Compute Metrics](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23703?page=32) |
| [OpenPM: Auditable Point-in-Time Evaluation for LLM Portfolio-Management Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988) (2026) | Some constructors improved returns **conditional on good analyst evidence**. In a separate daily sweep, Opus-4.7 returned 13.7% net with Sharpe 4.32 and DeepSeek-V3.2 returned 9.7% with Sharpe 3.01. Equal-weighting the same candidate pool remained a demanding baseline in the controlled comparison. ### Strategy and signal ideas worth testing—not assuming

- **Macro surprise and revision signal:** LiveMacroEval’s hourly pre-release nowcasts suggest testing surprise magnitude and direction relative to a timestamped consensus, particularly around scheduled releases. Keep event-window timing explicit; don’t infer from the paper’s market score that a tradeable edge exists. Candidate style: low-frequency event-driven risk adjustment or a small, separately tested release strategy. Data: official release calendar and vintages, consensus snapshot, instrument prices/quotes before and after release.
- **News novelty and calibrated sentiment:** MFAST’s pipeline suggests a firm-day signal built from calibrated probabilities, duplicate/novelty filtering, and next-feasible execution. Candidate style: slower news-reaction/swing signal in liquid single names, tested with delay, turnover and capacity sweeps. Data: licensed or permitted timestamped text, entity mapping, prices, quotes, volume, costs, and point-in-time universe.
- **Order-flow regime features:** [ViperQ: Order Flow Pattern Recognition via Auction Market Theory for Reinforcement Learning Trading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13825) reports features such as CVD slope/divergence, tape velocity, volume-area position, and imbalance, with results on TSLA and NVDA. This is an RL paper, **not** evidence for an LLM microstructure agent. Candidate style: deterministic short-horizon regime filter or scalper feature model, with order-book replay and realistic fees/impact before promotion. Its reported drawdowns were large, including −47.8% for NVDA. [Test Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13825?page=5)
- **Risk scaling from predictive uncertainty:** [CAST: A Cross-Asset State-Space Trading System for Drawdown Control in Stock Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14205) combines online forecast uncertainty with an MPC risk penalty. It is not an LLM or an all-asset system, but it is a useful template for deterministic sizing: more uncertain forecasts can produce smaller allowed exposures. In its stock-market study, removing the variance-weighted risk penalty caused drawdowns as high as 80% in two reported markets. [Risk Penalty Ablation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14205?page=8)

The main practical distinction: **MFAST is the strongest performance claim; LiveMacroEval is a promising but not yet portfolio-level macro result; OpenPM is strongest on enforceable constraints; and the crypto/live papers warn that latency, fills, fees, funding, and deployment can erase apparent advantage.**

## 3. Useful combinations with complementary research

Here are combinations that fit your human-promotion and deterministic-risk design. The combinations are engineering proposals, not claims that these papers have been tested together.

1. **Macro event context + risk constraints + uncertainty-aware sizing**  
   Pair [LiveMacroEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30110), [OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988), and [CAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14205). The macro agent emits a point-in-time, expiry-bound release surprise/regime estimate. The risk service joins it to strategy exposure and deterministic forecast uncertainty, then reduces permitted risk around designated event windows or uncertain states. The strategy still decides whether it has a trade. OpenPM supplies the typed hard-constraint boundary; CAST supplies the useful principle of separating predictor output from an uncertainty-sensitive controller. CAST’s empirical scope is stocks, so validate anew for each asset class.

2. **News signal + provenance defense + deterministic position cap**  
   Pair [MFAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23703), [Fake News, Real Crashes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26438), and [OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988). MFAST-style news processing emits a calibrated score, event type, timestamp, entity mapping, and novelty. A provenance/corroboration layer checks source and independent confirmation; OpenPM-style deterministic projection caps the effect on any one strategy or instrument. Expire the signal quickly, and reject it if the source is unverified or the processing timestamp is stale. The adversarial study is a reason not to equate confident wording with verified evidence: its fabricated-news attacks were substantially more successful than its prompt-injection controls. [Attack Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26438?page=1)

3. **Research-agent promotion + search-aware multiple testing + human approval**  
   Pair [OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988) with [What survives honest evaluation? Leakage-safe, search-aware assessment of LLM-driven trading strategy discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) and [Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Adaptive Financial Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03384). OpenPM governs point-in-time inputs and feasible portfolio constraints. The search-aware framework records every generated strategy and adjusts for how many trials were tried. Promote adds an anytime-valid, cost-aware evidence ledger for sequential strategy promotion. In practice, an agent may propose and backtest in research; a candidate becomes eligible for human review only after the frozen rule passes leakage checks, search-adjusted validation, and a forward shadow period. Promotion is a human decision, not an agent-generated order. Promote’s guarantee is specifically about a declared bounded score under its assumptions; it does not certify that a historical edge still exists in the current regime. [Promotion Protocol](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384?page=3) [Scope of Guarantee](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384?page=7)

4. **Agent proposals + enforceable control layer + staged crypto deployment**  
   Pair [OpenPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09988), [The Control Layer Behind Rule-Following Agents Trading Across Market Conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26324), and [Can AI Make Money in Crypto?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34510). The agent can propose a trade thesis or a risk reduction; a separate service enforces stops, exposure and concurrent-position ceilings. Then move from historical replay to exchange paper trading, shadow operation, and only after human approval a limited live pilot. Record intended versus actual execution time/price, since the crypto study shows substantial gaps across those stages and lengthy LLM/agent decision latency. The control paper also shows why “rules were followed” and “the system made money” must be separate scorecards.

5. **Selective abstention + news uncertainty + human escalation**  
   [FinAbstain: Uncertainty-Calibrated Multimodal RAG for Selective Financial Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24875) proposes a useful pattern: specialist agents provide cited evidence and probabilities; disagreement, contradiction, retrieval quality, and calibration contribute to uncertainty; high uncertainty triggers abstention, reduced exposure, more evidence, or human review. **Its reported performance tables are explicitly simulated, not empirical**, so borrow the architecture as a testable design idea, not as proof of risk reduction. Use it with MFAST-style timestamping and OpenPM-style deterministic enforcement.

For **live alpha-decay detection**, use the promotion-ledger pairing rather than trusting a permanent “approved” label: monitor net, cost-adjusted outcomes; expire authorization; and require a freshly funded revalidation epoch or another predeclared forward test before reinstating risk. A conventional rolling Sharpe alarm can still be a fast operational kill switch, but it is a monitoring heuristic—not the same thing as statistically valid promotion evidence.

For **stress scenarios and risk-off detection**, test the same frozen signals under separately specified shocks: scheduled macro surprises, spread/depth deterioration, venue outage, price gaps, cross-asset correlation changes, and conflicting or fabricated headlines. Don’t let an LLM invent the stress path or approve its own result. The agent may narrate what evidence it sees; deterministic scenario generators and risk limits decide exposure.

In all combinations, I’d keep the order of authority as: **human-approved strategy configuration → deterministic risk service and kill switches → strategy execution → agents’ advisory signals.** Agents can propose context or a bounded reduction; they cannot loosen limits, revive expired signals, change their own deployment status, or send broker instructions.

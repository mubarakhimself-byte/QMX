## Aurelle

For machine-generated strategies, validate the **entire search process**, not just the winner: an agent’s proposals, revisions, repeated inspections, and abandoned ideas all create chances to get lucky.

There is no universal conversion from a stricter gate to “X true winners lost, Y false strategies avoided.” That trade-off depends on the strategies’ real net edge, sample size, serial dependence, similarity, and the chosen threshold. Several methods are diagnostics rather than formal promotion tests. A recent simulation gives a concrete example of the price of a strict sequential gate, but it does not establish a general rate for your system.

### What each method is for—and what it can reject

| Method | What it tells you | False strategies it can help avoid | True winners it can discard or delay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)** | Whether a measured Sharpe remains impressive after accounting for non-normal returns and an estimated number and dispersion of trials. | Reduces promotion of the luckiest result from a large search. The key inputs must cover the *actual* search; the correction does not repair leakage or a bad cost model. | Raises the bar, so weak, noisy, or short-lived real edges may fail. It is not a winner-preserving test, and its result depends on how trials are counted and modeled. |
| **Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) / CSCV** | Whether the in-sample winner tends to rank poorly against other candidates across combinatorial train/test partitions. A high PBO warns that “best in sample” is unstable. | Useful for identifying fragile selection among many candidates before promoting the top-ranked one. | A real but regime-specific strategy can rank poorly in some partitions. PBO is a rank-instability diagnostic—not the probability that a particular strategy will lose live, and not a calibrated false-promotion guarantee. |
| **Purged and embargoed cross-validation / CPCV** | Whether results are sensitive across multiple time partitions while reducing leakage from overlapping labels or trade horizons. | Purging removes training observations whose label intervals overlap a test interval; embargo excludes a buffer after test intervals. This reduces a common source of optimistic validation. | More folds do **not** create more independent market history. Genuine effects concentrated in a few regimes may fail some splits; treating the folds as independent evidence can make the gate overconfident. |
| **Multiple-testing correction / Harvey–Liu haircuts** | Whether a claimed result clears a higher evidential bar after accounting for the number of hypotheses tried. | Directly targets false discoveries from broad factor or strategy searches. Harvey, Liu and Zhu argued that the conventional “$t>2$” standard is too weak for heavily mined discoveries; their work is a useful warning, not a universal trading threshold. | The higher cutoff increases false negatives, especially for modest edges and correlated or short histories. The correction is only as credible as the recorded search count and dependence assumptions. |
| **Walk-forward testing** | Whether a frozen procedure works when trained only on past data and tested on the next chronological window. | Exposes some forms of regime sensitivity and look-ahead that random splits can hide. | Regime-specific genuine edges may fail in a particular window. Repeatedly adjusting rules after each window and then reporting the best sequence turns walk-forward into another adaptive search; it does not, by itself, control that selection. |
| **Synthetic data and simulations** | Whether a pipeline catches known leakage, null strategies, and planted edges under specified market-like scenarios. | Can reveal broken code, excessive false promotions under a chosen null, or sensitivity to costs and dependence. | A realistic-looking generator can still omit the feature that makes the true edge work—or miss a real market failure mode. Synthetic success is not evidence of tradable alpha; a synthetic gate that is too severe can reject candidates that would work in reality. |
| **Minimum track record length (MTRL)** | How much evidence is needed to distinguish the observed Sharpe from a benchmark at a chosen confidence level. | Prevents short, noisy track records from being treated as decisive. The required history grows quickly as the target edge shrinks and must account for non-normality, serial dependence, and selection. | It deliberately delays or withholds promotion when evidence is insufficient. A real strategy can miss the required record length before its edge decays. It is a sample-size/evidence requirement, not a test that the strategy is false. |

### Newer work on large strategy searches

The most directly relevant recent work I found is [What survives honest evaluation? ### A promotion pipeline I’d use

1. **Freeze and log every candidate.** Give each strategy a deterministic fingerprint, executable rule, market and timeframe, data vintage, feature availability times, cost assumptions, and birth time. Record every evaluated variant—including failures, retries, parameter changes, and strategies the agent abandons. A changed rule is a new candidate. Without a complete ledger, DSR and multiplicity adjustments can undercount the search.

2. **Apply structural and economic checks before statistical promotion.** Enforce point-in-time data and executable timing in code, not by prompt or researcher assurance. Replay with spreads, fees, slippage/impact, funding, borrow, and instrument-specific trading calendars. Stress costs and execution assumptions. Keep the agent outside the money-moving path, as in your setup.

3. **Use purged, embargoed temporal validation for research robustness—not as a final untouched test.** Use chronological training and evaluation, and purge overlapping trade/label intervals with an appropriate embargo. CPCV can show whether performance is highly split-dependent; CSCV/PBO can show whether the selected candidate’s rank is fragile. Don’t count overlapping folds or paths as independent replications, and don’t tune on their results without logging that as further search.

4. **Correct for the actual search at the promotion gate.** Use DSR and a family-level multiple-testing procedure as complementary summaries, with the full trial ledger and defensible dependence assumptions. For agents that inspect results continuously, generate adaptively, or stop when a result looks good, prefer a prospectively registered sequential gate with an explicit lifetime error budget; ordinary end-of-backtest corrections do not automatically handle optional stopping and adaptive candidate births. Decide in advance whether your priority is low family-wise false promotion or higher discovery yield with controlled false-discovery rate.

5. **Protect one final chronological holdout.** Do not expose it to the agent, humans selecting candidates, or repeated “one last” tests. Evaluate only the frozen shortlist and frozen promotion rule there. If anyone uses the holdout to change strategy, costs, thresholds, or universe, it has become development data; start a new prospective test instead.

6. **Require enough observations for the intended edge, then promote in stages.** Set the target net Sharpe or return hurdle and confidence level before seeing results; compute the corresponding track-record requirement with dependence and selection in mind. Sparse intraday or overnight opportunities are not made well-powered just by having many timestamps. After statistical promotion, run shadow or tightly capped live trading for execution and current-regime checks before a human increases risk. A backtest certificate is not a claim that the edge will persist.

**My practical choice:** use hard leakage and execution controls, a complete strategy ledger, purged temporal splits plus CSCV/PBO as diagnostics, and a search-aware multiplicity gate; for a continuously adaptive agent, make that last gate sequential and explicitly budgeted. Reserve the truly untouched forward period for final confirmation. That combination makes false promotion visible and auditable while keeping “missed winner” risk measurable through predeclared power scenarios, rather than pretending any single statistic can eliminate it.

## Aurelle

The three most useful tools before promotion are **DSR**, **PBO/CSCV**, and an **anytime-valid, funded promotion test**; they answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one score.

A key distinction: “rejected by a gate” does not mean “proven false.” Except for simulations with injected effects, the literature generally does not know which strategies are genuine winners. So it cannot give a universal count of true winners lost versus false strategies caught. The strongest concrete trade-off I found is from a 2026 simulation of a sequential gate: it caught no null strategies in 3,000 fixed-family null paths, while detecting 70.1% of one injected signal strength and none of a weaker one. Those rates are specific to that simulation, not forecasts for your system.

## Three methods that matter most

### 1. Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)

The foundational paper is [The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094) (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014).

**What it computes, step by step**

1. For each candidate, calculate its Sharpe ratio over the evaluation returns, along with the sample size, skewness, and kurtosis.
2. Estimate the Sharpe threshold expected from selecting the best of $N$ trials, using the variance of Sharpe ratios across trials and an expected-maximum approximation.
3. Calculate the probabilistic Sharpe ratio (PSR) against that raised threshold. The result, the DSR, is a probability-like score: evidence that the observed Sharpe exceeds the selection-adjusted threshold. A commonly used hurdle is DSR ≥ 0.95, chosen in advance.
4. Keep the count of all searches that could have influenced the winner—not merely the final shortlist—as the trial count.

In the 2026 LLM evaluation described below, DSR uses the number of trials and their Sharpe-ratio dispersion, and PSR adjusts for non-normal returns using skewness and kurtosis. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="DSR" page="5" first="Let be the" last="honest arms.">

**Inputs and outputs.** It takes periodic returns, return count $T$, estimated skew and kurtosis, number of trials $N$, and variance of Sharpe ratios across trials. It outputs a DSR, not a PBO, fold score, or estimated probability that this particular strategy will fail live. It does **not** take an explicit trial-correlation matrix. Correlated strategies therefore make the meaning of $N$ and the trial-dispersion estimate important; simply counting every near-duplicate as an independent draw can make the correction miscalibrated.

**What the evidence says about winners and false promotions.** DSR does not come with a universal empirical “false strategies caught / real strategies discarded” rate. In Gençay’s 2026 study, the agent’s best strategy from 102 recorded trials had an in-sample Sharpe of 1.69 and DSR 0.86—below the paper’s 0.95 certification bar—and its held-out Sharpe was 0.18. That is a rejection consistent with caution, not a known true-negative: the study cannot establish that every rejected strategy was truly false. The same study’s leaky oracle had DSR 1.00, showing the important failure boundary: DSR cannot correct contaminated inputs or look-ahead. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="Agent Results" page="8" first="The result is" last="and the">

**Where it fails.** DSR depends on an honest trial count and a defensible model of the trial Sharpe distribution; it does not fix leakage, costs, survivorship bias, or regime change. A short track record makes both Sharpe and tail-moment estimates noisy. Intraday returns with heavy tails, volatility clustering, and serial dependence are especially awkward: skew and kurtosis do not by themselves make observations independent. For clusters of highly correlated strategies, report the raw count, family structure, and a correlation-aware sensitivity analysis rather than claiming the count is an exact number of independent trials.

### 2. Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) via CSCV

The original paper is [The Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322) (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, and Zhu, 2017).

**What it computes, step by step**

1. Build a time-by-candidate matrix of returns for **all** tested strategies.
2. Divide the history into an even number of blocks. For each way to choose half the blocks as in-sample, use the other half as out-of-sample; reverse the roles through the combinatorial choices.
3. In each split, pick the best in-sample candidate.
4. Rank that candidate against the other candidates on the complementary out-of-sample blocks. Convert its rank to a logit, $\lambda=\log(\omega/(1-\omega))$.
5. Report PBO as the fraction of splits with $\lambda \leq 0$: the in-sample winner ranked at or below the out-of-sample median.

A concrete implementation in Gençay’s 2026 evaluation uses 16 blocks and all ${16 \choose 8}=12{,}870$ half-block choices. The candidate-return matrix contains every logged trial. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="CSCV" page="5" first="PBO is estimated" last="trial count.">

**Inputs and outputs.** It takes the return matrix, the candidate columns, and a chosen even block count. Its output is a probability-like **rank-instability diagnostic**. It does not directly output a strategy-level p-value, the probability of a live loss, or a pass/fail guarantee. Correlations among candidates show up in their ranks, but PBO does not estimate an “effective number of independent trials” the way a multiple-testing correction might.

**What the evidence says about winners and false promotions.** No general false-positive or true-winner-detection rate is intrinsic to PBO. The 2026 study illustrates why it is a complement, not a substitute: one agent’s strategy had PBO 0.01—low rank-instability by this measure—yet its absolute out-of-sample Sharpe was only 0.18. It remained comparatively well ranked among mostly weak candidates while failing to show a strong absolute edge. In another ETF experiment, PBO was 0.80 and correctly warned of likely rank degradation, but that result still does not establish a universal cutoff. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="PBO Limit" page="9" first="Notably, PBO is" last="held-out confidence interval caught.">

**Where it fails.** Results depend on the candidate set and block construction; a small family can make the estimate coarse—the 2026 paper explicitly notes that its PBO estimate with roughly 20 candidates is coarse. CSCV is symmetric, not a strict forward-in-time deployment simulation. It cannot prove persistence through a future regime, and a high PBO can reflect genuine regime dependence as well as selection instability. Most importantly for this system, CSCV/PBO is **not** a cure for overlapping-label leakage. For labels or trades spanning multiple bars, purge overlapping training observations and apply an embargo around test periods; use CPCV or walk-forward splits as separate leakage/time-order diagnostics. These choices reduce leakage risk but do not turn folds into independent market histories.

A useful later evaluation that applies and stress-tests both DSR and CSCV is [What survives honest evaluation? Leakage-safe, search-aware assessment of LLM-driven trading strategy discovery](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) (Gençay, 2026). It also challenges any attempt to rely on either statistic alone: low PBO can coexist with weak absolute evidence, while a leaky oracle can pass statistical deflation. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="Three Instruments" page="12" first="Three failure modes" last="caught.">

### 3. Anytime-valid, funded promotion test

The newest directly relevant proposal is [Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Adaptive Financial Research Agents](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) (Benhamou et al., 2026; preprint dated November 2).

**What it computes, step by step**

1. At a predeclared candidate birth, freeze and fingerprint the strategy, its score definition, costs, and testing rule; assign it testing wealth $\pi_h$.
2. Require that lifetime allocations sum to at most one: $\sum_h \pi_h \leq 1$. A new or materially changed candidate consumes new budget.
3. From **post-birth** returns only, calculate a bounded net score $Y_{h,t}$ and update a predeclared betting e-process using bets chosen from past information.
4. Promote when its evidence reaches $1/(\alpha\pi_h)$. Under the paper’s conditional nonpositive-mean null and bounded-score assumptions, the weighted test controls the chance of ever promoting a null candidate at the chosen family-wise level $\alpha$—even with dependent strategies, adaptive births, and continuous monitoring.
5. Start economic exposure no earlier than the next executable interval; keep the testing-evidence ledger separate from the actual trading P&L.

The paper’s stated assumptions, threshold, and no-inherited-evidence rule are central to the guarantee. A changed strategy, restart, or fresh-regime certification is a newly funded test, not a free continuation. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="Protocol" page="3" first="1. Before outcome" last="first executable session.">

**Inputs and outputs.** The inputs are not a fixed number of trials or CV folds. They are each candidate’s birth time, allocated mass, bounded cost-adjusted score stream, and predictable betting rule. The output is an anytime-valid crossing decision, with its family-wise error budget. Candidate dependence is allowed under the assumptions; the method does not need an independence correction based on a guessed effective trial count. But it does need a valid score bound and the stated conditional-mean null.

**What it reports about catching false strategies and discarding winners.** In one fixed-family audit, unadjusted repeated tests had a 94.5% family-wise crossing rate across 3,000 null paths; the funded gate had 0% observed crossings (95% interval upper endpoint 0.128%). At the 15-basis-point injected alternative, it detected 70.1%, meaning it missed 29.9% in that experiment; at 5 bp, it detected none. At 20 bp, detection was 99.27%. The median crossing at 15 bp was session 2,327 of a 2,868-session test. For adaptive births under a separate exact mean-zero simulation, the funded gate’s observed family-wise crossing rate was 1.77% across 3,000 programs. In its historical market replay, it promoted 0 of 24 candidates—**not** a measured 24 false rejections, because true profitable alternatives were not planted or known in that market replay. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="Power Results" page="5" first="Target power rises" last="15 bp."> <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="Adaptive Audit" page="5" first="The selected identity" last="3,000 programs."> <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="Market Replay" page="5" first="The pseudo-out-of-sample market" last="retrospectively.">

**Where it fails.** The control applies to the declared bounded score and conditional-mean null, not to all definitions of profitability. Clipping, cost misspecification, contaminated data, nonstationarity, and a mismatch between score and actual economic risk can undermine relevance. The paper says its market replay was designed retrospectively and does not establish that market returns satisfy the null assumptions. Its simulations are not a market microstructure model. This method can be statistically conservative: its power and delay results make that visible.

The method builds on the broader anytime-valid inference and e-process literature rather than replacing DSR/PBO. For related foundations, see [Safe Testing](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae011) (Grünwald, de Heide, and Koolen, 2024) and [E-backtesting](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.01659) (Wang, Wang, and Ziegel; published online 2025). The latter is about backtesting risk forecasts, not a plug-in alpha-selection method. The recent agent-focused paper [Propose, Don’t Judge: An Anytime-Valid Referee for LLM Agents That Mine Investment Factors](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051) is a complementary line of work; I would not treat it as established evidence of an optimal gate.

## A staged promotion pipeline

These are operating recommendations, not thresholds proven optimal across FX, futures, crypto, equities, and different holding periods. For this many-agent setting, I would use the following sequence:

1. **Freeze the research object and make leakage fail closed.** Before any backtest, register a deterministic strategy fingerprint; code/config hash; data vintage and availability timestamps; instruments and calendar; feature definitions; holding period; order timing; and borrow, fee, spread, financing, and impact model. Reject look-ahead, revised-history joins, same-bar fills unsupported by the execution model, and ambiguous missing data. No statistical score compensates for an invalid information set. The recent agent study planted a look-ahead oracle with DSR 1.00, demonstrating that separation. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="Leakage" page="7" first="Table 2 shows the" last="out of sample by construction.">

2. **Log the whole search before selection.** Log every proposal, backtest, parameter or rule change, failed implementation, rerun, and human-directed variant—not just strategies the agent calls “candidates.” Keep family IDs and similarity links, but do not silently deduplicate near-identical tests out of the audit trail. Record prompt/model version, data version, costs, return series, and every selection decision.

3. **Use purged temporal validation as a robustness screen.** Run walk-forward splits and CPCV with purge intervals covering the maximum label/trade overlap, plus a predeclared embargo. For a practical diagnostic, require net performance to be positive in a clear majority of economically distinct test periods and investigate any fold with large losses; do **not** treat a particular percentage of positive folds as a calibrated p-value. Do not tune to CPCV/PBO output and then report it as untouched evidence.

4. **Apply search-adjusted and selection diagnostics to the frozen development search.** Require DSR ≥ 0.95 as an initial research hurdle, using the complete ledger and a sensitivity analysis for correlated variants. Use PBO as a warning light: for example, PBO > 0.20 triggers redesign or stronger forward evidence, but PBO ≤ 0.20 is not a pass by itself. Those are practical triage thresholds; neither paper establishes them as universal boundaries. A high DSR does not override poor execution robustness, leakage concerns, or a strategy whose profits come from one fragile regime.

5. **Use a genuinely untouched chronological confirmation period.** Open it only after the strategy, costs, sizing, candidate family, and test have been frozen. Require positive net evidence versus a declared benchmark, with a confidence interval or sequential test that accounts for dependence. If the team sees the holdout and retunes, it becomes development data; it cannot remain the promotion holdout.

6. **For ongoing adaptive research, add a sequential family-level budget.** I would use an anytime-valid funded test with a predeclared program error budget—for example, family-wise $\alpha=0.01$ for actual live authorization—and candidate allocation rules fixed before the candidates’ post-birth outcomes are observed. This is deliberately stringent; model its power against simulated effect sizes that matter to your strategies before adopting it. Shadow trade first, then permit the human promotion decision and risk limits to govern capital.

7. **Separate statistical authorization from portfolio approval.** A candidate may pass a return-evidence gate and still fail liquidity, capacity, concentration, correlation-to-existing-strategies, venue, borrow, or drawdown limits. Keep those checks as distinct vetoes and log them separately.

### When a strategy family keeps being retuned

Treat each material retuning as a new hypothesis, but preserve the lineage: parent hash, parameter/rule delta, reason, timestamp, and which prior results informed it. Never carry forward the parent’s validation evidence as if the child were pre-registered. Do not let repeated near-duplicates evade the family-level error budget.

For an actively explored family, perform tuning only inside development data; select the family-level rule there; then test the **selection procedure**, not just its final favorite, on fresh forward data. If the system continues retuning after seeing that evidence, register a new version and fund a new sequential test or wait for a new untouched period. Under an anytime-valid scheme, a new birth gets post-birth evidence only; a failed or abandoned candidate does not refund its spent testing allocation. The 2026 Promote paper explicitly prohibits importing pre-birth evidence and treats a changed rule or restart as a newly funded candidate. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="No Free Restarts" page="3" first="Conditioning is what" last="hidden near-duplicate tests.">

## Aurelle

**The main cross-asset rule is to count independent market opportunities, not bars:** a year of minute data is not automatically a year’s worth of independent evidence. Validation should reflect each market’s trading calendar, data history, execution mechanics, and regime coverage—and should distinguish “not enough evidence yet” from “evidence against the strategy.”

## 1. How validation should differ by asset class

There is no defensible universal minimum history for “FX,” “crypto,” or any other class. Set the needed history from the strategy’s target net edge, turnover, dependence, and promotion error budget; then ask whether the available history covers enough distinct regimes and independent-ish opportunities. Keep the full time series, but estimate uncertainty with methods that respect dependence, such as HAC errors, block bootstrap, or a predeclared sequential test.

For an idealized AR(1) return sequence, the effective sample size for estimating a mean is roughly

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx N\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho},
$$

where $N$ is the nominal count and $\rho$ is lag-one autocorrelation. For example, $\rho=0.9$ leaves roughly 5% of the nominal count by this approximation. Real intraday returns are not generally stationary AR(1) data; clustering, overlapping holding periods, shared market shocks, and volatility regimes make a “number of independent trades” even harder to summarize. A count of trades is not an independence count.

Stress large shocks and price-limit/market-disruption periods. | One front-month continuous chart is not an adequate history of a tradable strategy. Roll treatment can dominate a purported edge; run contract-level replay with explicit roll and margin assumptions. |
| **Crypto** | Include venue-specific fees, spread, depth, funding, borrow, liquidation, outages, and changing market access. Model 24/7 calendars, exchange fragmentation, token listings/delistings, and major structural regime changes. | Round-the-clock bars create an especially misleading nominal sample size. Multiple coins and venues can be highly correlated during market-wide moves. Short history or many bars cannot substitute for distinct bull, bear, volatility, funding, and liquidity regimes. |
| **Futures** | Use point-in-time contract specifications, individual contracts, actual roll decisions, multiplier/tick changes, margin, commissions, and market-specific session rules. | Back-adjusted continuous contracts are useful for some signals but are not executable return series. Preserve raw contract prices and separately test roll logic; do not let a synthetic roll adjustment create P&L. |
| **Single stocks** | Point-in-time universe membership; delisted names; splits, dividends, mergers, halts, borrow availability/fees, corporate actions, and realistic market impact. | Survivorship and selection bias are central. Test across point-in-time constituents, not today’s survivors; control for sector and market exposure. Many stocks do not supply independent observations because of common factors and event shocks. |

For every asset class, I would report **both** nominal observations and uncertainty at the relevant time scale: number of trading days, non-overlapping trade episodes, independent time blocks under the chosen bootstrap, and the number of distinct regimes represented. If a strategy holds for $h$ bars, adjacent $h$-bar outcomes overlap; they should not be treated as separate evidence. For intraday strategies, estimate dependence at the strategy’s actual decision/holding horizon, and check whether apparent sample size is mostly repeated exposure to the same session or volatility episode.

A recent synthetic execution study reinforces why execution must be part of validation, not a final haircut: on its compressed two-asset, high-volatility board, the ranking agreement between near-ideal and default-stress execution was Kendall $\tau_b=0.21$; the study also reports wide intervals and higher agreement with broader universes, so this is a warning about benchmark sensitivity, not a universal effect size. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2610.05077" title="Execution Ranking" page="3" first="The largest rank change" last="we tested.">

## 2. What strict gates cost—and what the papers actually measure

The literature does **not** establish one general number of true winners discarded or false strategies saved. In historical market studies, the true status of most rejected candidates is unknown. The cleanest winner-discard versus false-alarm trade-off comes from simulations where truth is injected; those results depend on the injected effect and the assumed return process.

- **Sequential, anytime-valid promotion:** [Can We Stop the Backtest Yet?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) reports 94.5% family-wise false crossings for repeated unadjusted tests versus 0% observed in 3,000 fixed-family null paths for its funded gate. At its 15-basis-point injected alternative, the gate detected 70.1%—so it missed 29.9% in that particular experiment—and the median crossing was session 2,327 of 2,868. It detected none of the 5-basis-point alternatives. This makes the cost concrete: strong error control can mean long waits and low power against weak effects. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2611.03384" title="Power Results" page="5" first="Target power rises" last="15 bp.">

- **Anytime-valid referee for factor mining:** [Propose, Don’t Judge](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051) reports that, on its synthetic world with planted truth, its frozen referee admitted no false factor in any seed, while three deliberately leaky referees admitted 0.26–0.85 false factors per submission. In its CSI 500 walk-forward, the frozen referee admitted 11.7 false factors per campaign versus 86–196 for leaky comparators under the script proposer. But a valid referee did not make true signals arrive quickly: a true factor waited about 500 trading days; its comparison desk waited 500 days by design. In one library, the anytime-referee portfolio Sharpe also trailed a fixed-horizon comparator by 0.12–0.24. This is evidence of a delay and selection-composition cost in that setting, not proof that anytime tests always underperform fixed-horizon tests. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2609.27051" title="Real Results" page="17" first="On the four-family" last="against 9.8."> <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2609.27051" title="Portfolio Cost" page="21" first="The comparator’s net Sharpe" last="in every start year.">

- **DSR and PBO in generated-strategy evaluation:** [What survives honest evaluation?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) finds that its LLM-discovered strategies do not clear its search-aware DSR hurdle, across the study’s tested settings. That does **not** mean all were known false winners. One candidate had DSR 0.86 and held-out Sharpe 0.18; the paper argues that PBO can miss an absolute-edge failure when the winner retains rank among mostly poor candidates. Conversely, its planted look-ahead oracle achieved DSR 1.00: selection corrections do not repair contaminated data. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="Agent Results" page="8" first="The result is" last="and the"> <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.27734" title="PBO Limit" page="9" first="Notably, PBO is" last="held-out confidence interval caught.">

- **Execution assumptions:** [How Execution Assumptions Change Short-Horizon Sharpe Rankings](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.05077) does not estimate true strategies wrongly rejected. It demonstrates a different cost: evaluation rankings can change when fills, latency, spreads, or impact change, particularly in short, high-volatility tests. Thus, a gate based on idealized execution may “reject” or promote the wrong strategy for the wrong reason.

### Strategy ideas that appear in these papers

These are **candidate hypotheses mentioned or tested**, not validated recommendations. Their presence in a paper is not evidence of a live edge.

| Candidate idea | Style / instruments | Data needed to test it |
|---|---|---|
| RSI plus volume-z-score contrarian signal, monthly re-ranking | Mean-reversion; liquid equities in the cited LLM-search experiment | Point-in-time prices and volume, universe membership, corporate actions, executable rebalance prices, spread/impact, and borrow if short |
| SMA(50,200) crossover | Trend-following; tested as a daily rule on liquid ETFs | Adjusted and executable prices, dividends, turnover and costs; test across instruments and regimes |
| Calendar effects: turn-of-month, weekday, January, quarter-end | Short-horizon calendar rules; tested on equity ETF/industry panels | Correct exchange calendar, point-in-time dates, next-executable prices, turnover and costs; do not infer an effect from reconstructed calendars alone |
| 126-day momentum, low-volatility, short-term reversal | Cross-sectional long/short equity factor hypotheses | Point-in-time prices and constituents, delistings, borrow, rebalancing costs, factor/sector exposures |
| Time-series momentum / trend across asset classes | Slower trend strategy; referenced in a 39-ETF study | Point-in-time instrument history, roll/funding/financing, volatility scaling and realistic costs |
| Rank-IC factor sleeves | Quintile long-short factors such as value, liquidity, volatility or momentum | Point-in-time features and forward returns; enforce publication lags, purge overlapping forward labels, calculate break-even edge from turnover and costs |

The agent-audit paper [Auditing LLM Alpha-Factor Mining](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.29500) is a useful warning about evidence retention: its reanalysis kept the full 1,047-expression cohort, including non-scorable outcomes, while it found that an earlier outcome-filtered catalog omitted negative OOS rows. “Keep every outcome, including errors and failures” is part of validation integrity, not bookkeeping overhead.

## 3. Combinations that would make a stronger validation system

These pairings serve distinct roles; applying several overlapping significance corrections to the same fixed test can make the gate needlessly conservative. I would use diagnostics to find fragility, a predeclared inferential gate to authorize testing, and separate forward/live rules to manage changing economics.

1. **[DSR](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094) + [PBO/CSCV](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322) + [search-aware LLM evaluation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734).**  
   Use a complete search ledger to calculate DSR on all recorded variants; use CSCV/PBO to diagnose selection-rank instability; then require an untouched chronological confirmation of absolute net performance. That catches different failure modes: DSR raises the hurdle for search intensity, PBO asks whether the winner’s relative rank is fragile, and forward confirmation checks whether the edge itself persists. Do not let the agent see the final holdout.

2. **[Can We Stop the Backtest Yet?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) + [Propose, Don’t Judge](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051).**  
   Let agents propose and document strategies, but a frozen referee—not the proposing agent—computes post-birth evidence and controls the growing candidate family. Use funded per-candidate anytime tests for a family-wise authorization objective, or a valid online e-BH-style procedure if the explicit objective is discovery with controlled FDR. Separately register a break-even net-edge threshold; statistical evidence of positive gross prediction is not enough if costs exceed the edge. The referee paper’s important warning is that what gets certified (daily IC versus the actual traded horizon) affects both waiting time and economics.

3. **[Propose, Don’t Judge](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.27051) + [E-Detectors](https://doi.org/10.51387/23-NEJSDS51) + [Understanding Alpha Decay](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4353).**  
   Use a sequential detector on live, cost-adjusted evidence to flag deterioration after promotion, with a viability null tied to that family’s break-even. Define an investigation / scale-down / retirement ladder prospectively. Do not reuse the initial promotion certificate as evidence that the edge remains alive: admission and ongoing viability are different hypotheses. E-detectors provide a nonparametric sequential change-detection framework; their threshold and detector design still need calibration to each strategy’s stream and loss tolerance.

4. **[FIDES](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.23308) + [search-aware LLM evaluation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734) + [LLM alpha-factor audit](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.29500).**  
   Before statistical testing, compare the strategy description, executable code, and actual backtest behavior; then bind every run to immutable code/data hashes and preserve the complete candidate cohort, including failures and negative outcomes. FIDES found that only 2 of its 40 ETF strategies beat buy-and-hold, and that a model’s self-reported claim to beat it held for 1 of 32 such claims. These are small-sample protocol results, not a market-wide base rate, but they make a compelling case not to accept the agent’s description or summary as the artifact of record. <alphaxiv-paper-citation paper="2608.23308" title="FIDES Findings" page="2" first="Across 8 liquid" last="a 3.1% hit rate.">

5. **[Synthetic execution benchmark](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.05077) + [anytime-valid promotion](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) + stress scenarios built from actual instrument mechanics.**  
   Test the whole promotion procedure—not just each candidate—against null paths, injected edges of different strengths, realistic cost/latency/roll scenarios, fat tails, and regime changes. Measure both false family crossings and the fraction/delay of injected winners detected. Use synthetic runs to falsify a broken gate and expose its operating curve; require market data or forward evidence for economic promotion. Do not interpret “passed a simulator” as “has alpha.”

6. **[Certified Alpha Capacity](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01115) + [anytime-valid promotion](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03384) + a predeclared live ramp.**  
   Estimate whether the expected life of the opportunity is long enough to accumulate the evidence required by the chosen gate. The CAC paper’s Gaussian exponential-decay example says that with family-wise false-deployment level 0.05 and power 0.90, a 100-candidate Bonferroni search and instantaneous Sharpe 2 requires a half-life of about 7.24 years. That is a model-based feasibility illustration, not a universal half-life estimate. If a strategy’s plausible decay horizon is shorter than its validation horizon, test a faster, properly controlled design—or decline promotion because evidence will arrive too late. After authorization, ramp through shadow trading and capped exposure; increase only when observed fills, costs, and risk remain within predeclared bounds. CAC itself is not a live ramp-up rule.

### Practical overall design

I would make the order of operations:

**Artifact and point-in-time audit → realistic instrument-specific execution replay → purged temporal robustness splits → DSR and PBO diagnostics on the full search ledger → funded sequential or online family gate → untouched forward confirmation → human authorization → shadow / capped live ramp → live decay and risk monitoring.**

The human retains promotion authority; the agent never handles capital. Every tuning step creates a new logged version, and no retuned child inherits its parent’s confirmation evidence. A family that keeps changing should be treated as an adaptive research stream: use development data for iteration, a frozen referee and post-birth evidence for authorization, and fresh evidence after material changes or regime resets.

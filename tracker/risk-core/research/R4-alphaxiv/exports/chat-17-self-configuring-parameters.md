### A parameter architecture that fits many instruments

Separate **what the market contract dictates** from **what must be learned**.

- **Instrument-specific facts:** tick size, contract multiplier, trading hours, minimum order size, and fee schedule should be exact instrument/venue configuration—not statistically pooled guesses. Treat spread and slippage as measured, time-varying execution-cost estimates, with venue and session context.
- **Shared priors with instrument-level posteriors:** volatility dynamics, typical costs, normalized stop distance, signal threshold, and edge estimates can have group-level distributions with instrument deviations. Pool first by meaningful structure—asset class, venue, liquidity/volatility tier, strategy family, and possibly session—rather than forcing FX, crypto, and equities into one undifferentiated group.
- **Use comparable units:** express stops in volatility units, costs relative to spread or volatility, and thresholds on a common signal scale. This lets related instruments share information without assuming their raw price scales are comparable.
- **Shrink edge estimates toward no edge.** For an instrument with little independent evidence, its estimated edge should be pulled toward zero (or a conservative group prior), not toward an optimistic average. Let the data earn a positive override.

Empirical Bayes is a practical way to estimate group-level prior parameters from historical data, then update each instrument’s estimate using its own evidence. It is attractive at this scale because you need not hand-set a separate prior for every instrument. But estimate those group priors inside the training portion of each chronological evaluation; otherwise information from the evaluation period leaks into the parameters being tested.

### Defaults, overrides, and when to split

Keep a **versioned group default plus an explicit override layer**. Every override should record its evidence window, effective sample size, posterior uncertainty, last update, and expiry or review condition. “No override” should be a valid and common outcome—not a missing configuration that triggers an unreviewed optimizer.

Create an instrument- or session-specific parameter only when all of these hold:

1. **There is a plausible mechanism.** For example, a session has consistently wider spreads, different volatility, or a distinct market microstructure—not just a historically high score.
2. **There is enough independent evidence.** Count independent days, sessions, and market regimes, not only trade count: correlated trades from one episode are not many separate experiments.
3. **The difference is material and persistent.** The posterior difference from the group default should be large enough to matter after costs, and persist across rolling forward windows. A noisy point-estimate advantage is not enough.
4. **The override survives a fair comparison.** Compare the group default and candidate override on identical, untouched forward periods with the same cost model and risk constraints. Account for the fact that many instruments, sessions, and settings were tried.
5. **The benefit pays for the extra degrees of freedom.** If the override adds parameters but only marginally improves the objective—or increases drawdown, turnover, or fragility—keep the pooled default.

A good rule is: **pool unless a pre-registered forward test shows a repeatable, economically meaningful gain that remains under cost and regime stress**. Prefer a wider stable region of good settings over a single sharp optimum; a narrow peak is evidence of sensitivity, not necessarily precision.

### Auto-tuning without turning noise into edge

Bayesian optimization is best used for **expensive, bounded offline searches**—for example, a small number of signal thresholds, normalized stop parameters, or model settings. The 2012 work shows why it can be more evaluation-efficient than grid search, while the 2025 portfolio study suggests explicitly penalizing instability can improve the search trajectory. Neither supports repeatedly optimizing recent realized P&L and deploying the winner.

For continual adaptation, use a **Bayesian state update or bandit over a small, pre-approved set of configurations**, not unconstrained exploration over every parameter. Update only after enough new evidence; discount old data or change the prior only when a pre-specified drift/regime diagnostic fires. Optimize a risk-aware utility—net returns after realistic costs, with drawdown and exposure constraints—not raw profit alone. Keep risk limits such as maximum exposure, loss limits, and stop protections outside the optimizer’s authority.

Evaluate the entire selection process chronologically: tune on past data, validate over rolling forward windows, then hold out a final period with parameters locked. Stress spreads, slippage, and volatility; report fold-level results and sensitivity around chosen parameters. The 2026 framework is useful as a process checklist, but its own case study does not establish execution robustness because it lacks cost/slippage stress tests. [Execution Limitations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09219?page=15)

Finally, keep the governance boundary you described: agents may propose configurations and evidence, but they should not promote them or change live risk controls. A human should review the forward-test record, override rationale, operational limits, and rollback plan before promotion.

## Aurelle

The three pieces I’d put at the center are **hierarchical empirical-Bayes shrinkage**, **Bayesian optimization**, and **strict forward validation**—but with an important distinction: the trading studies below test tuning or validation, not a complete hierarchical parameter system across hundreds of instruments. ### 1. Hierarchical empirical-Bayes shrinkage: pool first, specialize when evidence supports it

This is a statistical method rather than a trading paper with a directly comparable backtest result. It is the part that makes self-configuration across many instruments sensible when some strategies have much less data than others.

**What it does, step by step:** For a parameter or statistic—say net edge, normalized stop distance, or session cost—estimate each instrument’s value and uncertainty from its own data. Estimate a group mean and between-instrument variation from the related instruments. Then pull noisy instrument estimates toward that group mean, more when their estimates are uncertain. For a simple normal model, the shrinkage is:

$$
\tilde{\theta}_i
=
w_i\hat{\theta}_i+(1-w_i)\mu_g,
\qquad
w_i=\frac{\tau_g^2}{\tau_g^2+s_i^2}.
$$

Here, $\hat{\theta}_i$ is instrument $i$’s estimate, $s_i^2$ its estimation variance, $\mu_g$ the group mean, and $\tau_g^2$ the group’s between-instrument variance. Empirical Bayes estimates the group quantities from the observed instruments; the system returns a shrunken estimate, uncertainty, and weight—not just a single “optimal” value.

**Inputs and outputs:** Inputs should be trade-level, time-stamped net P&L and exposure, plus the relevant market measurements: realized volatility, spread and slippage, signal values, and session labels. Use independent days or sessions as well as trades to estimate uncertainty, because many trades from one market episode are correlated. Outputs can include shrunken estimates of edge, costs, volatility, and normalized stop or threshold settings, with posterior intervals and the instrument’s shrinkage weight. Do not shrink hard contract facts such as tick size or minimum order size; those should come from instrument configuration.

**Performance and robustness evidence:** Shrinkage reduces the variance of estimates when instruments are genuinely related and individual samples are noisy. That is a statistical benefit, not evidence that a particular strategy will earn more or draw down less. The retrieved trading studies do not report a multi-instrument, hierarchical-shrinkage performance comparison, so I would not attach return or drawdown claims to it.

**Where it fails:** Partial pooling can be worse than no pooling if the group is wrong—for example, if one “energy” group combines instruments with materially different trading hours, liquidity, or execution costs. Regime shifts can also make historical group estimates stale. Use economically meaningful groups, allow group membership to change only under a declared rule, and shrink edge estimates toward zero or a conservative prior rather than toward an optimistic group average. A useful later complement—not a direct extension of shrinkage—is [AlgoXpert Alpha Research Framework](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09219) (2026), which puts stability regions and chronological validation around parameter choices.

### 2. Bayesian optimization: spend evaluations efficiently, but treat its winner as a candidate

[Practical Bayesian Optimization of Machine Learning Algorithms](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1206.2944) (2012) is a foundational method paper, not a trading-performance study.

**What it does, step by step:** Specify a bounded set of parameters and an objective that evaluates a configuration. Fit a probabilistic surrogate to the configurations tested so far and their observed scores. Use an acquisition rule to choose the next configuration, balancing promising predicted performance against uncertainty. Run the evaluation, add its result, and repeat until the budget is used. The method can also account for unequal evaluation costs and parallel trials.

**Inputs and outputs:** Inputs are the parameters being varied and the result of a consistent evaluation for each trial. In this system, that evaluation should use net returns after execution costs, with a risk-aware objective and explicit constraints—not raw backtest profit. The output is a proposed parameter vector (and, in Bayesian optimization, an uncertainty estimate about the objective); it does **not** inherently return an empirical-Bayes shrinkage weight or prove an instrument merits its own setting.

**Performance and robustness evidence:** The paper reports more efficient optimization than grid search on several machine-learning problems and, for CIFAR-10, a test error over 3 percentage points better than the expert-tuned baseline. That supports evaluation efficiency in those tasks, not trading edge or drawdown control. [CIFAR-10 Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1206.2944?page=11)

Later trading applications illustrate both the appeal and the gap. [Unlocking Profit Potential: Maximizing Returns with Bayesian Optimization of Supertrend Indicator Parameters](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2405.14262) (2024) reports higher optimized maximum profits for several stocks, but lower maximum profit than default parameters for Infosys; the paper does not establish durable forward or cost-stressed gains. [Stock Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2405.14262?page=12) [Improving Bayesian Optimization for Portfolio Management with an Adaptive Scheduling](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529) (2025) changes the search objective to account for variability as well as performance; details follow below.

**Where it fails:** BO efficiently searches the objective it is given, including an overfit objective. Repeatedly tuning against the same periods, instruments, and score creates selection bias even if each individual search is well behaved. A winning setting may also be a fragile peak. Use BO on training data, prefer broad stable regions over isolated maxima, and validate the frozen candidate on later data. Don’t give it authority to widen risk limits.

### 3. Adaptive Bayesian optimization plus forward gates: search stability is not live robustness

[Improving Bayesian Optimization for Portfolio Management with an Adaptive Scheduling](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529) (2025) is the most directly relevant empirical study here for tuning portfolio-model parameters under a limited evaluation budget. It is **not** a hierarchical multi-instrument shrinkage study.

**What it does, step by step:** It evaluates a configuration of a black-box portfolio model, initially emphasizes expected performance, then increases a penalty on variability in observed performance as the search budget is used up. It uses a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE), aimed at mixed and relatively high-dimensional spaces, and returns the next proposed configuration. In that paper the tested configuration includes at least 25 hyperparameters, including lookbacks, buy/sell thresholds, and risk or sizing settings.

**Inputs and outputs:** The input is a configuration and the backtest model’s observed score—annualized Sharpe in the experiments. The output is a next trial proposal and a more stable search trajectory. It does not return instrument-level volatility/cost estimates or a pooling weight, and the paper’s described experiments do not expose the black-box models’ detailed configurations.

**Performance and robustness evidence:** With an evaluation budget of 500, TPE-AS reports the highest Sharpe in 8 of 12 scenarios and the lowest optimization variance in all 12. In the high-volatility, threshold-hybrid scenario, it reports Sharpe 3.480 versus 3.070 for BNN-UCB. In a separate ablation, standard TPE reached 2.980 versus 3.288 for TPE-AS; the reported optimization variance was 1.367 versus 0.309. The authors describe these as portfolio-model and **optimization-stability** results. They are not proof of lower live drawdowns or robustness to unseen markets. [Scenario Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529?page=4)[Ablation](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529?page=4)

**Where it fails:** The models and backtest settings are undisclosed, limiting independent audit. Low variance across optimization trials is not the same as stable forward returns: all trials could still be overfit to the same backtest. Treat the result as a reason to penalize unstable search outcomes, not as permission to deploy a winner automatically. The later [AlgoXpert Alpha Research Framework](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09219) (2026) is a useful validation complement: select from a stable region, run rolling forward tests, lock parameters before final out-of-sample evaluation, and stress execution costs. Its illustrative USDJPY study reports OOS Sharpe 2.34 and MaxDD 4.21%, but uses simplified execution and explicitly does not establish cost/slippage-stress robustness. [OOS Results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09219?page=14)[Execution Limitations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.09219?page=15)

### Concrete defaults-and-overrides policy

I would implement this as a **hierarchical default, then a gated override**, not as an optimizer independently fitting every instrument.

**Defaults:** Maintain a global strategy-family default, then defaults for well-supported groups such as venue, asset class, liquidity tier, and trading session. Use normalized parameters where possible: stops in volatility units, costs relative to spread or volatility, thresholds on a comparable signal scale. Keep contract specifications as exact instrument-level configuration. For an instrument without an approved override, use its group estimate, with uncertain edge shrunk toward zero.

**How an instrument earns an override:** Require a plausible market or execution reason, not just a better historical score. As initial governance thresholds—not universal statistical constants—I’d require at least **100 effective independent trades across 30 or more distinct sessions** for an instrument-level strategy override, plus evidence across at least two materially different volatility or market conditions. For a session-specific override, require observations across at least **60 distinct instances of that session**; trades within one session are not independent evidence. Then compare the proposed override with the group default on identical, untouched forward windows. Require a material improvement in the pre-chosen net, risk-adjusted objective, with no breach of drawdown, cost, or exposure limits, and a benefit that survives realistic spread/slippage stress. If the override does not beat the default reliably—or requires tuning many extra knobs—it stays pooled.

**Re-estimation cadence:** Update execution-state estimates such as volatility and spread continuously or daily, but apply strategy parameters only at a scheduled boundary. Refit instrument posteriors and eligible overrides weekly from a rolling history; revisit group-level priors monthly. Revisit higher-level group definitions quarterly, not every time recent performance wiggles. Any detected regime change may trigger a review or a *risk-reducing* response, but should not silently authorize fresh alpha tuning.

**Rate limits and risk boundary:** Limit an approved strategy-parameter change to **at most 5% of its normalized value per weekly update**; for bounded signal thresholds, cap the move at **0.1 group standard deviations** per update. Require the candidate to pass validation again before applying further changes. Keep hard portfolio and instrument risk limits outside the optimizer: it may not increase maximum exposure, leverage, or allowable loss. Automatic changes should be confined to conservative state estimates or tightening risk; loosening risk or promoting a new instrument/session override remains a human decision.

In short: **shrink estimates across genuinely comparable instruments, let forward evidence earn an override, and rate-limit changes so the system cannot chase a short-lived backtest win.**

## 1. What to pool by asset class

Pool **estimation information**, not every raw parameter value. A useful hierarchy is instrument → liquidity/venue/session subgroup → asset class → global strategy-family default. The parameters most suited to pooling are those expressible on comparable scales: volatility forecasts, costs relative to volatility or spread, stop distances in volatility units, signal thresholds on a normalized score, and estimates of signal decay. For edge, pool uncertainty more readily than the edge itself: an instrument with weak evidence should generally have its estimated edge shrunk toward zero, not toward an optimistic group average.

| Parameter | Pooling policy |
|---|---|
| Volatility scaling and risk targets | Pool model structure and prior information within comparable products; update instrument estimates locally. Do not pool raw volatility levels across unlike products. |
| Spread, slippage, impact, borrow/funding | Estimate by instrument, venue, session, and trade size. A class-level model can be a prior, but execution costs should not be assumed equal across instruments or venues. |
| Stops and signal thresholds | Pool normalized parameters within the same strategy family and reasonably similar market microstructure; allow overrides when forward results support them. |
| Session windows | Do not pool clock-time windows indiscriminately. Exchange hours, liquidity peaks, and trading interruptions differ. Share session labels or features only after aligning them to each market’s calendar and timezone. |
| Contract rolls, expiries, corporate actions, funding | Do not pool the *rules*. These are instrument- or product-specific mechanics. Models may share how they account for them, but must use the correct local contract and event schedule. |

**The data do not arrive in equally independent units.** FX and stock-index futures offer many intraday observations, but serially correlated bars and repeated trades in the same session are not independent evidence; FX also varies by broker and venue. Index products have long price histories, though session boundaries, holidays, and contract changes matter. Metals have substantial history, but precious and industrial metals need not share the same liquidity, session, or volatility behavior. Energy can have rich futures histories, but seasonality, expiry, delivery, and roll effects make crude, gas, and refined products poor candidates for one undifferentiated pool. Crypto supplies continuous, abundant bars, but 24/7 trading, venue fragmentation, funding, and changing market structure make apparent sample size easy to overstate. Futures can have deep histories, but a continuous series depends on correct roll and adjustment choices. Single stocks may have many observations for liquid names, but histories differ in length and must handle delistings, corporate actions, and changing liquidity. **In all these markets, count distinct days or sessions and regimes, not just trades or bars.** That is a practical data-quality distinction, not a quantified ranking from the cited papers.

A good default is therefore hierarchical, but conservative: exact instrument configuration for market mechanics; local estimates for execution conditions; and partial pooling for noisy statistical quantities. The studies reviewed here do **not** provide a direct empirical comparison proving that hierarchical partial pooling raises net out-of-sample trading returns across these asset classes.

## 3. Combinations worth building

These combinations are **design recommendations**, not results already validated as a package.

1. **[TPE-AS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529) + [Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282):** use TPE-AS offline to propose a small set of stable parameter configurations; treat each approved configuration as an arm in a conservative bandit online, with the current group default as the baseline arm. Conservative UCB only explores a new arm when a lower-confidence estimate says the cumulative reward-budget constraint can still be met, maintaining the configured baseline-relative floor with high probability under its assumptions. This joins sample-efficient tuning with bounded live experimentation; it does **not** guarantee trading safety under market jumps, execution failures, or nonstationarity. [Conservative UCB](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=5)

2. **[TPE-AS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13529) + [AQuA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) + [What survives honest evaluation?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734):** let a constrained agent propose typed parameter diffs or candidate signals; keep the evaluator, data splits, costs, and risk limits fixed; record every trial; select a stable region rather than the single best result; then apply DSR/PBO-style selection accounting and a final locked forward test. This directly targets the failure modes the papers expose: tuners can be unstable, agents can search many variants, and leakage correction and multiple-testing correction are different safeguards. Gençay’s counterexample is particularly important: its planted look-ahead oracle still passed statistical deflation, so a trial ledger does not replace point-in-time data controls. [Oracle Result](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734?page=7)

3. **[AQuA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) + [Post Selection Estimation of Sharpe Ratios](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.01650):** after choosing a strategy from many candidates, estimate its post-selection Sharpe rather than treating the winning sample Sharpe as an unbiased edge estimate; feed the more conservative edge distribution into position sizing. In implementation, size from posterior or post-selection uncertainty, with exposure capped by conservative downside estimates, rather than from the raw point estimate of Sharpe. This is a proposed link between post-selection inference and sizing; I have not found a result in these sources proving that this exact combination increases realized returns.

4. **[Conservative Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282) + [Rotting Bandits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1702.07274):** use a conservative baseline-relative reward budget, but discount stale observations or monitor for declining arm rewards so that the currently favored parameter is not kept forever after its edge decays. Permit rapid de-risking under a pre-defined deterioration signal, but make re-risking slower and evidence-gated. This addresses alpha decay and safe exploration conceptually; bandit guarantees depend on their reward assumptions and do not model all market regime changes. The LLM trading evaluation study offers a concrete complementary safeguard: fully record trials and correct for the selection process before interpreting a “discovery” as an edge. [Uniform Constraint](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1602.04282?page=3)

5. **[AQuA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12841) + [What survives honest evaluation?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27734):** allow an LLM to propose *research hypotheses or bounded parameter changes*, not trades or risk-limit changes. Compile proposals through typed, registered features and parameter ranges; evaluate them on a frozen, cost-aware process; log every experiment; and keep promotion human-controlled. AQuA reports positive held-out results for one equity setup, while Gençay rejects all discovered strategies in its different evaluation despite some profitable out-of-sample runs. Together they argue for testing the agent under strict governance—not assuming either its gains or its failures generalize universally.

The main practical distinction is **what gets automated**: use pooled estimates to stabilize noisy per-instrument statistics; let Bayesian or bandit methods nominate bounded configurations; use sequential monitoring to spot decay; and require selection-aware, cost-aware, forward evidence before granting an instrument or session an override. A system can be good at finding promising parameters and still be bad at knowing when those parameters are no longer trustworthy.

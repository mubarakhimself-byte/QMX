Conformal Kelly: When Marginal Coverage Transfers
and the Economics Do Not∗
Robert Jacob Ryan

Abstract
We size portfolio positions from conformal prediction intervals, reading the interval half-width as the
scale in a capped fractional-Kelly rule (Conformal Kelly), and evaluate the rule under a pre-registered
protocol. Its development record is an autonomous LLM-agent search over roughly 200 configurations
on a single six-year window (2016–2021) of eight US-listed ETFs. All later data was sealed before
the search began, and two final configurations, nine comparison books, ten metrics, a written growth
prediction and the mapping from outcomes to conclusions were registered before unsealing.
On the sealed 2022–2024 window, realized conformal coverage was 0.745 against a 0.750 target,
but the two configurations’ annualised net log growth was 8.5% and 7.0% (28.5% and 25.8% in
development),belowanunleveredequal-weightbook(9.5%),theleveragedpassivebooks(15.6–16.8%)
and a registered hindsight four-equity book (10.8%, at a 46% drawdown). Both configurations ranked
last of eleven entries on Sharpe and Calmar, and the drawdown control failed its registered criterion.
A post-hoc split, outside the registered protocol, shows the passing coverage averaging 0.592 in 2022
and 0.842 afterwards, with the entire loss in the under-covered year. The split does not show that
under-coverage caused the loss: the leverage cap bound on every 2022 trading day, so narrow intervals
could not enlarge the book, and a long-equity book in an equity bear market is the simpler account.
But the registered acceptance test passed a sixteen-point conditional failure, which motivates, without
establishing, conditional coverage as an endpoint for rules that read interval width as a scale.
On the development window, a slow, unweighted, per-asset conformal quantile out-grew every
locallyadaptivealternativewetested(adaptiveconformalinference,recency-weightedandasymmetric
intervals, pooled and Mondrian calibration) and a plug-in standard deviation by 2.1 percentage points
a year at matched leverage. That evidence is exploratory and cap-conditional: it was measured under a
gross-leverage cap binding on 97.7% of days, and dependence-aware block-bootstrap intervals include
zero for 11 of the 13 contrasts we could re-derive.

that maximises expected log wealth (Kelly, 1956). The first produces something close to what the second
needs: a calibrated statement of how wrong a forecast tends to be. The match is inexact, because Kelly

Kelly needs ascale: a𝜎 such that𝑓★≈𝜇/𝜎 2 is the right fraction of wealth to commit. Reading𝜎 off
a conformal half-width, the rule we callConformal Kelly, is trivial to write down, and it changes what a
good interval is. A forecaster wants an interval sharply adapted to local conditions; a sizing rule may not,
because a noisy𝜎 enters the sizing map nonlinearly and its estimation variance is charged to compounded
wealth. And a marginal guarantee constrains how often an interval fails on average, not when it fails.
This paper reports a negative result obtained under a pre-registered protocol, and it orders its claims
by the strength of the evidence behind them. The empirical base is one universe of eight exchange-traded
funds (ETFs), one six-year development window and one 2.7-year sealed window; every general statement
below should be read against that single experiment.
(i)Confirmatory: apre-registrationprotocolforagenticsearch,anditsoutcome.Theconfiguration
space was searched by an autonomous LLM agent against an immutable evaluation harness (Section 9,
Appendix A), a large multiple-comparisons exposure (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014; Bailey et al.,
2017) that we report rather than omit. The mitigation is structural: a window sealed before the search
began, and a registration, committed before unsealing, that fixed the two final configurations by commit
hash, ten metrics, nine comparison books including the one most likely to beat ours, a sample-size caveat,
a written prediction, and the mapping from outcomes to conclusions. Under that mapping the outcome is
a partial refutation: coverage transferred, growth did not, the registered risk-adjusted defence failed, and
so did the drawdown dial’s registered criterion (Section 10).
(ii) Post hoc: a conditional-coverage diagnostic.Splitting the sealed window at the year boundary
shows realized coverage of 0.592 in 2022 and 0.842 in 2023–24: the passing marginal figure averages
two large errors of opposite sign, and the under-covered year holds the entire loss. The split was not
registered, so the paper’s most informative observation lies outside its confirmatory protocol; we state that
tension wherever the observation is used and do not resolve it. Nor does the split identify a mechanism.
The leverage cap bound on every trading day of 2022, and a common cause—a volatility regime that
outran a deliberately slow estimator while equities fell—fits the data at least as well as a causal link
(Section 11). What the split does show is that the registration’s own acceptance test passed a window
containing a sixteen-point conditional failure. That marginal validity does not imply conditional validity
is known (Vovk, 2012; Barber et al., 2021); one documented instance of the gap in a sizing rule motivates,
but does not establish, conditional coverage as a registered endpoint for rules that read an interval width as
a scale.
(iii)Exploratoryandcap-conditional: evidenceonconformalscaleestimation.Onthedevelopment
window, under a gross-leverage cap that bound on 97.7% of days, every device we tested that made the
interval adapt faster lost growth, and the conformal quantile out-grew a plug-in standard deviation by 2.1
percentage points (pp) a year at matched leverage (Sections 5 and 6). A drawdown control built from the
same intervals cut development-window drawdown at better Sharpe, surviving a constant-leverage control
and a timing placebo (Section 7). The direction of the scale evidence is consistent across devices, but the
entries were selected from a searched grid, block-bootstrap intervals include zero for 11 of the 13 contrasts
we could re-derive, and off the cap the ordering is untested (Section 12).

Conformalprediction.Thesplit-conformalconstructionanditsfinite-samplemarginalcoverageguarantee

intervals in the spirit of conformalized quantile regression (CQR; Romano et al., 2019). EnbPI (Xu
and Xie, 2021), the ensemble-bootstrap construction for dependent series, is related but not tested here.
Mondrian conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2003) supplies the within-taxonomy pooling of Section 6.
Conformal predictive systems (Vovk et al., 2019) and their decision-theoretic use (Vovk and Bendtsen,
2018)—expected-utility maximisation over a predictive distribution, which under log utilityisKelly—are
the theoretically correct version of what we do. We implement the decision rule (expected-log-utility
maximisation over the empirical calibration-residual distribution) rather than a full conformal predictive
system, and it loses, for two measurable reasons (Section 6.3). Exact conditional coverage cannot be
guaranteed distribution-free (Vovk, 2012; Barber et al., 2021); recent methods guarantee coverage over
prespecified subgroups or classes of covariate shift (Gibbs et al., 2025), the natural tool for the conditional
endpoint that Section 11 proposes. Noguer i Alonso (2024b) surveys conformal prediction in finance.
Growth-optimal betting.Kelly (1956) and the fractional- and drawdown-constrained Kelly literature
(MacLean et al., 1992; Grossman and Zhou, 1993; Browne, 1997; MacLean et al., 2010, 2011) supply the
sizing map and the reason to use afractionof full Kelly: its drawdown behaviour is intolerable and its
sensitivity to estimation error in𝜇 and𝜎 severe. Our object is orthogonal: not a new sizing map, but an
estimator for its denominator.

Backtest overfitting.The deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014) corrects one
statistic for a known number of trials, and the probability of backtest overfitting (Bailey et al., 2017)
assesses a selection procedure. A registration is complementary: it protects theinterpretation—which
comparators may be dropped, which outcomes promoted (Section 10).
Autonomous research loops.The development methodology is the fixed-harness agentic pattern
of Karpathy’sautoresearch (Karpathy, 2026): an immutableprepare.pydefining data, splits, costs
and the metric; a mutabletrain.py that is the agent’s entire action space; and a keep-or-revert loop on a

2024). We are not aware of an earlier quantitative-finance study that discloses an agentic search in its
method section rather than omitting it, though we have not surveyed for this systematically (Section 9).

6 What does not work on the development window
The search’s second and third legs tested nine structural hypotheses and rejected eight. The rejections
share a sign and, we conjecture, a mechanism; the intervals below say how much weight each can bear.
6.1 Locally adaptive conformal prediction
Every one of the first four devices in Table 9 makes the interval adapt faster, and every one loses growth in
point estimate; none of those losses is individually distinguishable from zero. Our candidate explanation
is that the sizing map integrates the interval and so charges thevarianceof𝑞 rather than rewarding
its local sharpness. We measured that mechanism directly only for the holding-period device, which
calibrates and sizes on the𝐻𝑠-day residual the staggered book is actually exposed to, instead of the 21-day
forecast residual. There the estimator’s smoothness is analytically transparent: an overlapping 21-day
residual is itself a 21-day moving average of daily shocks, so its trailing quantile is smoother, with the
standard deviation of daily log-changes in𝑞 at 0.00343 for𝐻𝑠 =21 against 0.00391 at𝐻𝑠 =5 and lag-1
autocorrelation 0.53 against 0.36, and growth is monotone in𝐻𝑠. For the other devices the mechanism is
conjectured, not measured. The pooled rows fail for a different reason, taken up below.
One correction to the development notes: what fails isfastadaptivity, not cross-sectional adaptivity—
Section 5.2 shows that flattening the cross-section of𝜎 costs 6.6pp. On this evidence it is the speed of
the scale estimator, not its cross-sectional content, that should be slow. Mondrian calibration sharpens
this: pooling within asset class (0.2584) recovers only 0.1pp over all-asset pooling through the identical
code path (0.2573), so the original diagnosis (“score shapes differ across assets”) was wrong. The−4.2pp
figure is a different leg-1 construction and is not the right comparator. Pooling fails, we think, because it
replaces each asset’s own quantile with (its trailing mean absolute deviation)× (a shape factor shared
across the cell), discarding the per-asset tail behaviour that Section 5.1 suggests matters.

9 The agentic search process, disclosed
The configuration space was searched by an autonomous LLM agent across three relay sessions (“legs”)
against an immutable harness. Approximately200 configurations were scored on DEV;results.tsv
records 47 rows—33 keep/discard experiment decisions(18 keeps, 15 discards), 2 paper-configuration
rows, 10 ablation blocks and 2 robustness blocks—with the rest scratch sweeps inside an experiment.
17
Roughly 25 structurally distinct hypotheses were tested; legs 2 and 3 rejected eight of nine between them.
The same workflow extended to this manuscript: the text was drafted with LLM assistance, then reviewed,
verified line by line against the committed experimental record, and edited by the author, who takes sole
responsibility for every claim in it.
Wedisclosethisforthreereasons, anditisastrengthratherthanaconfessiononlybecauseofthethird.
First, it is a multiple-comparisons exposure.Two hundred configurations on 1,511 days of a single
universe is a large search. The dominant gain (+7.5pp from the forecast horizon) is mechanistic; the final
≈1 pp is tuning and should be expected to shrink out of sample (Section 5.3), as the registration predicted.
The bootstrap intervals of Appendix B are not adjusted for this search.
Second, the search produced akindof result a smaller one would not.The value is not the+11pp
of accumulated metric but the rejected structural hypotheses, each with a controlled ablation. Testing
conformal predictive systems, portfolio-level conformalisation, Mondrian calibration, holding-period
residuals, per-asset regime dials, epistemic disagreement terms, drift shrinkage and water-filled clipping,
each with its own control, is roughly a person-month of work. The negative results are a large part of the
paper.
Third, and only because of this: the sealed window.An agentic search is only as credible as the
evaluation it cannot see. Before unsealing,paper/prereg.mdfixed the two commit SHAs, the metrics,
the nine comparison books including the adversarial one, the sample-size caveat, the mapping from
outcomes to conclusions, a written prediction that Config A would land materially below 0.2845, and a
commitment to report both configurations and bothpredict_tail variants. A large disclosed search
plus a small sealed pre-registered test is a stronger epistemic position than an undisclosed human search of
the same size, which is the field’s status quo.
A limitation of the registration itself: the project repository is private, so the registration and its
ordering relative to the unsealing are verifiable through the committed history, which we can provide
on request, rather than through a public archive or timestamp. Readers should weight the registration
accordingly.

12 Limitations
1. The development-window evidence is cap-conditional and untested off the cap.Every row of
Tables6and9—includingthe2.1pp/yrtheconformalquantiletakesfromtheplug-instandarddeviation,
and with it the tail-sensitivity ordering—was measured with the gross cap binding on 97.7% of days
at the harness’sMAX_GROSS = 2.0 . That is not a side condition: Section 4 derives the candidate
mechanism from it. Below the Kelly optimum, an estimator that understates𝜎 on the fat-tailed assets
keeps weight on them, and the book is rewarded for it only because the cap holds it on the rising
part of the growth-versus-exposure curve. With the cap slack—at gross 1.0, or at 3.0 and 4.5, above
the pre-cap average of 4.28, where the book would sit near or past its own Kelly peak—that reward
weakens and, past the peak, reverses sign. The inertness of𝜅, the empirical flatness of𝛼, the failure of
inverse-volatility tilting, the irrelevance of correlation and the impotence of constant de-levering all
follow from the same fact. Whether the ordering compresses, survives or inverts is unknown, and it

13 Conclusion
In one pre-registered eight-ETF experiment, marginal calibration transferred out of sample and the
economic value of the sizing rule did not: the capped Kelly-inspired rule earned less than the passive
benchmarks and ranked last among them on Sharpe and Calmar, while its slowly varying conformal
quantile held near-nominalmarginalcoverage. A post-hoc split shows that the marginal figure averaged a
sixteen-point under-coverage in 2022 with a later over-coverage. The under-covered year held the entire
loss, but the binding cap rules out the simple oversizing story, and a regime that moved both coverage and
equity returns explains the co-location at least as well. The lesson we draw is narrower than a causal one:
an acceptance test on marginal coverage can pass an interval-as-scale rule through a large conditional
failure, so conditional coverage should be registered alongside it, a recommendation this experiment
motivates rather than establishes.
The development-window evidence runs against the usual advice for conformal prediction on time
series: slow, unweighted, per-asset, tail-robust quantiles out-grew every locally adaptive alternative tested,
including the methods designed for non-exchangeability, and a plug-in standard deviation by 2.1pp/yr at
matched leverage; the same intervals, read as a pooled downside break rate, cut development drawdown at
better Sharpe and survived a constant-leverage control and a timing placebo. That evidence is exploratory,
mostly within bootstrap noise contrast by contrast, and measured only under a binding gross cap of 2.0; on
the sealed window the drawdown control failed its registered criterion. The candidate explanation—a
scale estimator consumed by a nonlinear sizing map is charged for its own estimation variance, while
under a binding cap its lag in level costs little—is a hypothesis for the follow-up registration, not a finding
of this one.

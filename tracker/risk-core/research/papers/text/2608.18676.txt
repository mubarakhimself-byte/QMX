Abstract.We study a long-run risk-sensitive portfolio problem with propor-
tional transaction costs in a continuous-time market whose log-prices are given
as a L´ evy process, and rebalancing is possible only at random moments of in-
vestment opportunities. Using a Schauder fixed-point argument, we solve the
ergodic Bellman equation without any mixing assumptions. Under additional
full-support and growth conditions, the solution to the Bellman equation is
unique, with the unique continuous maximiser. We also prove vanishing risk-
aversion asymptotics towards the risk-neutral (Kelly) problem and convergence
of a dyadic-time-grid approximation of the random intervention moments. Nu-
merical examples illustrate the results.
Keywords:risk-sensitive portfolio optimisation; proportional transaction costs;
Kelly criterion; ergodic Bellman equation; L´ evy process

The market model considered here has two features that jointly determine the
mathematical structure of the problem. First, the asset prices evolve incontinuous
time: we assume that the process lnS(t),t≥0, has stationary and independent
increments, which covers, in particular, the exponential L´ evy dynamics, see Apple-
baum (2009). Second, the investor isnotallowed to trade continuously. Instead,
the portfolio can be rebalanced only at the momentsτ 0 = 0< τ 1 < τ2 < . . .of
an exogenously given sequence of investment opportunities, with the waiting times
ηi :=τ i −τ i−1 satisfyingP[η i >0] = 1 andE[η i]<∞. This reflects a number
of practically relevant frictions: illiquidity, limited access to the market, execution
delays, or simply the fact that trading decisions are made at discrete (and possi-
bly irregular) inspection times. Together with the proportional transaction costs
paid at each rebalancing, this makes the problem a long-run impulse-type control
problem for the portfolio weight process.
Risk-sensitive portfolio optimisation has been extensively studied in the litera-
ture; we refer to Davis and Lleo (2014) and Prigent (2007) for a general overview.
In the continuous-time frictionless setting, the seminal contributions are due to Bi-
elecki and Pliska (1999) and Fleming and Sheu (2000), and further developments,
including problems with factors and with jumps, can be found e.g. in Nagai (2003,
2006); Pitera and Stettner (2016); Bo et al. (2022). On the abstract level, the crite-
rion (1.1) belongs to the class of ergodic (average cost per unit time) risk-sensitive
control problems; see Biswas and Borkar (2023) for a recent survey and Di Masi
and Stettner (1999, 2007); Ja´ skiewicz (2007); Borkar and Meyn (2002); Guasoni
and Robertson (2012); B¨ auerle and Rieder (2014) for a selection of general results
in the Markov decision process framework; see also the monograph B¨ auerle and
Rieder (2011). On the technical side, three approaches dominate the literature: the
vanishing discountapproach, see e.g. Cavazos-Cadena and Hern´ andez-Hern´ andez
(2009, 2017); thespan-contractionapproach, see e.g. Pitera and Stettner (2016);
Shen et al. (2013), which typically relies on a uniform ergodicity or minorisation
condition of Doeblin type, cf. Hairer and Mattingly (2011); andKrein–Rutman-
type or Collatz–Wielandt-type arguments applied to the multiplicative form of the
Bellman equation, see Bonsall (1962) and Arapostathis et al. (2016). Each of these
techniques requires some form of ergodic behaviour of the controlled process, an
assumption that is restrictive in the transaction cost setting, where the controlled
weight process may be pushed towards the boundary of the simplex.
Portfolio problems with proportional transaction costs are, by now, classical,
but the combination with the risk-sensitive long-run criterion is considerably less

explored. In the continuous-time setting we refer to Bielecki and Pliska (2000)

In Pitera and Stettner (2023), the authors consider a discrete-time market with
i.i.d. log-returns, proportional transaction costs, and no short selling, and they
solve the associated risk-sensitive Bellman equation under the minimal integrabil-
ity condition requiring finite mean and finite entropy of the log-returns. For the
risk-seeking caseγ >0, the Bellman equation is solved directly via the vanishing
discount approach. For the risk-averse caseγ <0, which is the focus of the present
paper, the vanishing discount argument yields only arecursive(iterated) family
of Bellman equations, and in order to solve a single ergodic Bellman equation the
authors additionally invoke a span-contraction argument, which requires an extra
ergodicity (mixing) assumption on the returns as well as an auxiliary diversifica-
tion constraint that is subsequently relaxed. The present paper complements and
extends Pitera and Stettner (2023) in the following four directions.
(1)Continuous time and random intervention moments.We work with
a continuous-time price process with stationary independent increments and
allow the portfolio to be rebalanced only at random moments (τn) of investment
opportunities. This is a genuine generalisation of the discrete-time framework
of Pitera and Stettner (2023), which corresponds toη i ≡1; in particular,
it covers exponential L´ evy dynamics observed at renewal-type or externally
driven inspection times. The resulting Bellman equation (2.10) is stated at the
(subordinated) growth vectorR(η 1) and its normalisation is expressed per unit
of time.
(2)No ergodicity condition.We show in Theorem 3.10 that the ergodic Bellman
equation admits a solution under the increments condition (A1), the waiting
time condition (A2), and the integrability condition (A3) alone. In particular,
no mixing, minorisation, or full-support assumption is required, no auxiliary
diversification constraint is introduced, and no recursive scheme is needed: the
Bellman equation is solved directly. Moreover, the solution produced is regular,
in the sense that its span seminorm and its Lipschitz constant are bounded
by explicit constants depending only on the transaction cost rates, and it is
structurally distinguished: the associated positively homogeneous lift on the
cone of capital vectors is concave, equivalently,e¯vγ is concave on the simplex. To
the best of our knowledge, this concavity property is new even in the discrete-
time i.i.d. setting of Pitera and Stettner (2023).
(3)Uniqueness of the maximiser and the solution to the Bellman equa-
tion.We prove in Theorem 3.14 that, for a solution with a concave lift, the
supremum on the right-hand side of the Bellman equation is attained atexactly
onepoint, for every value of the pre-rebalancing weights, and that the resulting
map is continuous. Consequently, for a fixed solution to the Bellman equation,
the optimal rebalancing is unambiguously defined and the optimal stationary

a simpler risk-neutral one when the risk-aversion parameter is small. Further-
more, in Theorem 5.1 we show that the optimal value obtained when the inter-
vention times are restricted to a dyadic time grid converges, as the mesh tends
to zero, to the optimal value of the original problem. This is important from
the numerical perspective, as it requires simulating the controlled trajectory
only at finitely many grid points.
The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the market model,
state the standing assumptions, formulate the associated Bellman equation, and
prove the verification theorem which links its solutions to the optimal value and to
the optimal strategy of the underlying control problem. Section 3 contains the main
results of the paper: in Section 3.1 we develop the capital coordinates and study the
self-financing cone, in Section 3.2 we collect the properties of the entropic utility
and of the one-period reward functional, in Section 3.3 we prove the existence of a
solution with a concave lift, in Section 3.4 we show the uniqueness and the continuity
of the maximiser, and in Section 3.5 we prove the uniqueness of the solution to the
Bellman equation. Section 4 is devoted to the vanishing risk-aversion asymptotics,
while in Section 5 we discuss the discrete time grid approximation. Finally, in
Section 6 we present exemplary setting where all the assumptions are satisfied and
illustrate the theoretical results with numerical examples.

3.Solution to the Bellman equation
In this section, we show the existence of a solution to the Bellman equation (2.10)
and study the maximisers on its right-hand side. The argument is based on a fixed-
point theorem of Schauder type applied to a suitable compact convex family of func-
tions onS. In contrast to a Krein–Rutman-type approach or a span-contraction
argument, no auxiliary diversification constraint is needed. Also, the solution ob-
tained is shown to have a concave exponential lift, which is the key structural
property used in Section 3.4 to prove that the optimal rebalancing is unique.
Throughout this section we assume (A1), (A2), and (A3); the full-support con-
dition (A4) is usedonlyin Section 3.4 and is invoked explicitly there. In particular,
neither the existence of a solution nor any of the bounds below depend on (A4).

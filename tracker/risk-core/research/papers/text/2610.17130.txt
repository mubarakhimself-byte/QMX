ABSTRACT
Automated alpha factor discovery is critical for modern quantitative research. While
large language models (LLMs) have increasingly been integrated into quantitative
workflows, existing agent-based approaches remain highly susceptible to severe
backtest overfitting. To address this challenge, we propose EVOQUANTLAB, a
training-free, dual-loop self-evolving framework for robust factor discovery. In-
stead of relying entirely on LLM generation, EVOQUANTLABdeploys an inner
genetic-programming (GP) loop that dramatically improves token efficiency while
evolving typed factor expressions under structural and diversity constraints. An
outer diagnosis loop then systematically audits evidence across cycles to drive
protocol-level improvements. Furthermore, our evaluation pipeline applies multi-
metric validation to rigorously filter out spurious factors before deployment. Exten-
sive out-of-sample experiments across both daily and hourly frequencies—covering
equities and cryptocurrency—demonstrate the robustness of EVOQUANTLAB.
Specifically, on daily CSI 500 and S&P 500 tasks, EVOQUANTLABachieves lead-
ing annualized returns of 18.34% and 21.30%, consistently outperforming existing
agent baselines. On high-turnover hourly cryptocurrency data, EVOQUANTLAB
delivers a 37.91% annualized return while effectively avoiding execution collapse.
1 INTRODUCTION
Automated alpha discovery has become an increasingly important direction in quantitative re-
search (Gong et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2024; Lopez-Lira & Tang, 2026). Recent large language
model (LLM) agents can formulate economic hypotheses, generate factor implementations, interpret
backtest feedback, and iteratively refine candidate signals, enabling substantially larger research
spaces than traditional manual workflows (Li et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2025; Han et al., 2026; Wang
et al., 2026). Systems such as R&D-Agent-Quant, AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha, and FactorMiner
demonstrate that LLMs can automate significant portions of factor and model discovery across
heterogeneous financial data.
However, scaling automated research also amplifies a fundamental problem:backtest overfitting.
An agent may repeatedly generate and evaluate large numbers of factors, transformations, windows,
and model variants, making it increasingly likely that apparently strong signals arise from noise or
adaptive selection rather than persistent predictive structure (Harvey et al., 2016; Bailey & L´opez de
Prado, 2014). This issue is particularly severe for iterative LLM agents, since development results
are repeatedly fed back into subsequent generations. Existing systems mainly focus on improving
candidate generation, memory, reflection, or S&Psearch (Yang et al., 2024), but stronger search
alone can further increase the effective number of trials (Kou et al., 2025). Meanwhile, relying on
LLM calls for every candidate is unnecessarily expensive when many factor variations are symbolic
transformations that can be explored more efficiently (Shi et al., 2025; Romera-Paredes et al., 2023).
To address these challenges, we introduce EVOQUANTLAB, a training-free dual-loop self-evolving
framework for robust alpha discovery. At the inner level, EVOQUANTLABcombines LLM-generated

At the outer level, the framework audits completed research cycles for evidence of overfitting, in-

an efficient mechanism for large-scale factor exploration, while outer-cycle diagnosis improves
robustness across successive research cycles.
Our contributions are:
• We introduce EVOQUANTLAB, a training-free dual-loop framework that jointly evolves factor
expressions and the quantitative research process to address adaptive backtest overfitting.
• We combine LLM-guided hypothesis generation with diversity-preserving genetic programming,
enabling efficient exploration of large symbolic factor spaces with substantially reduced reliance
on repeated LLM inference.
• We develop a diagnosis-driven outer evolution mechanism and rigorous multi-metric validation
pipeline, and demonstrate strong out-of-sample performance across daily and hourly equity and
cryptocurrency tasks.
2 RELATEDWORK
Automated alpha factor discovery.Formulaic alpha discovery seeks interpretable expressions
that predict future asset returns. Classical approaches rely on human-designed signals or search
over symbolic expressions, with genetic programming (GP) providing a natural mechanism for
evolving executable factor trees through crossover and mutation (Koza, 1992; Kronberger et al.,
2026). Evolutionary search has been applied to trading-rule discovery (Neely et al., 1997; Allen &
Karjalainen, 1999), while AutoAlpha extends this paradigm with hierarchical evolutionary search for
formulaic alphas (Zhang et al., 2020). Learning-based approaches instead optimize the generation
process directly; for example, deep symbolic regression frameworks (Petersen et al., 2019)and
AlphaGen (Yu et al., 2023) formulate formulaic alpha discovery as reinforcement learning. Despite
their effectiveness, these methods typically operate within a predefined search space whose operators
and search procedure remain fixed. EVOQUANTLABretains an explicit GP search engine but allows
validated research knowledge and the search vocabulary to evolve across cycles.

LLM agents for quantitative research.Recent work uses large language models to automate
broader parts of quantitative research, connecting hypothesis generation, factor implementation,
empirical evaluation, and iterative refinement(Wang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2026). R&D-Agent(Q)
coordinates research and development agents for joint factor–model optimization (Li et al., 2025);
AlphaAgent combines LLM-driven factor generation with regularization against redundancy, com-
plexity, and alpha decay (Tang et al., 2025); and systems such as QuantaAlpha and FactorMiner
further explore iterative factor discovery with evolutionary search and accumulated research experi-
ence (Han et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Li et al., 2023). These systems demonstrate the potential of
LLMs as autonomous quantitative researchers, but also expose the model repeatedly to noisy backtest
feedback in open-ended trial-and-error loops, where context noise readily misguides planning deci-
sions(Lu et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2026). In contrast, EVOQUANTLABseparates semantic research
evolution from candidate-level optimization: the LLM updates reusable research knowledge, while a
deterministic, budget-accounted GP engine performs factor search.
Self-evolving agents.Beyond finance, self-evolving agents aim to improve their own reasoning
procedures, workflows, architectures, or procedural skills through accumulated execution feedback
and experiential memory (Shinn et al., 2023; He et al., 2026a; Zhao et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2026).
Pioneering efforts search over executable programs and algorithmic heuristics using LLMs in an
evolutionary loop(Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023; He et al., 2026b). Other methods
automate agent designs and workflows via iterative feedback(Hu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a;b),
or recombine modular components like planning and memory(Shang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026).
This line of work shows that adaptation can occur at the system level rather than solely through
model parameters, extending from agent workflows to broader research automation that co-evolves
skills, memory, and policies (Xu et al., 2026). EVOQUANTLABbrings this perspective to quantitative
research, but evolves research knowledge and symbolic search across cycles under a frozen evidence
protocol, preventing held-out outcomes from influencing subsequent evolution.
3 EVOQUANTLAB
Automated quantitative discovery faces two intertwined challenges: efficiently exploring vast sym-
bolic spaces, and preventing adaptive backtest overfitting caused by repeated experimentation. EVO-
QUANTLABaddresses this by decoupling exploration across two distinct timescales: aninner fast
loopevolving factor expressions via genetic programming (GP) within a frozen protocol, and an
outer slow loopauditing research evidence across cycles to update protocol rules.

3.1 FORMALSETUP& IMMUTABLETASKCONTRACT
Let D= (D train,D dev,D oos) be chronologically ordered market data. A research cyclec is governed
by a frozen protocol Πc =⟨S c,T c,O c,M c,B c⟩, where Sc specifies the task and data contract, Tc
denotes the active terminal set, Oc the typed operator set, Mc the evaluation rules, and Bc the search
and trial budgets. To eliminate lookahead bias,Πc enforces point-in-time availability andprefix replay
invariance: for any τ < τ′, features computed on D1:τ must agree with those computed on D1:τ ′
over the shared prefix. Within cycle c, EVOQUANTLABmaintains a population of typed factor trees
Pc ={g 1, . . . , gK}, where K denotes the population size and each candidate g∈ G(Tc,O c) belongs
to the valid expression space induced by the active terminal and operator sets. When evaluated
on an asset–time observation, g produces a scalar factor score. Candidates are evaluated across F
development folds via a robust worst-fold objective penalized by structural tree complexity:
fdev(g; Πc) = min1≤k≤F Metrick(g)−λComplexity(g),(1)
where Metrick(g) denotes the task-specific utility on development fold k, defined such that larger
values indicate better performance; Complexity(g) is a pre-registered scalar complexity penalty
derived from tree size and depth, and λ >0 controls its strength. Equation 1 operates strictly on
Ddev; the holdout split Doos is physically inaccessible during search. Training data Dtrain are used
only for task-specific parameter estimation when required (e.g., fitting normalization or baselines),
whereas candidate selection and protocol evolution are driven entirely by development evidence.
3.2 THEFROZENL0–L6 EVIDENCEPIPELINE
Candidates are sequentially validated through a cycle-frozen seven-layer pipeline (Fig. 2), advancing
only upon passing preceding gates. The pipeline begins atL0 (Data), which assembles canonical
panels, tradable universes, and purged fold masks under strict prefix invariance. Next,L1 (Factory)
instantiates typed, economically grounded terminals spanning order flow, funding, microstructure, and
momentum, whileL2 (Tree GP)searches typed symbolic expression trees guided by the worst-fold
fitness objective in Equation 1.
Surviving expressions undergo economic falsification atL3 (Falsify)through only-arm/drop-arm ab-
lations, sign-flip and participant-swap tests, and cross-sectional residualization against pre-registered
factor carriers. A candidate survives only if its hypothesized core mechanism remains directionally
consistent and the full expression provides reproducible incremental value. At each timestamp, we
cross-sectionally residualize candidate scores against a pre-registered carrier matrix and re-evaluate
the residual signal across development folds. Candidates whose predictive power vanishes after
residualization are rejected as redundant with known factor families.
Subsequently,L4 (Stress Battery)stress-tests signal stability across regime splits (market trend ×
volatility), execution delay curves, asset clusters, and matched-budget block permutations. Candidates
that clear all development hurdles reachL5 (Admit), where their implementation, terminal manifest,
data version, and metric configuration are frozen and recorded by content hashes before OOS access.
Finally,L6 (Portfolio)maps admitted signals into trading strategies under fixed volatility targeting,
risk constraints, and transaction costs.
3.3 LOOPA: INNERGP SEARCH& KNOWLEDGEINGESTION
L1 Knowledge Ingestion.Instead of expanding features blindly, L1 extracts economic mechanisms
from academic papers, financial disclosures, and analyst reports into structuredReference Cards.
These proposals enter an isolatedL1 Proposal Queue. A candidate primitive must clear both
a pointwise reference check and a prefix causality test before becoming eligible for admission
across cycle boundaries. Each active terminal j∈ Tc maintains a development evidence tuple
ej = (u j, sj, rj, dj), tracking utilization uj, candidate survival sj, residual contribution rj, and
cross-fold stability dj. Primitives with weak standalone power but sustained interaction gains are
retained asinteraction-onlyto preserve diversity.
Tree-Based Factor Evolution.Within cycle c, population Pc evolves across generations via tourna-
ment selection. Offspring are generated via typed subtree crossover and point mutation over valid
grammar nodes. Syntactic duplicates and expressions with pairwise correlation ρ >0.95 are replaced
with targeted immigrants from underrepresented families. Elites are preserved in a family-stratified
archive instead of a monolithic top-Klist, guaranteeing structural diversity within the trial budget.

3.4 LOOPB: DIAGNOSIS-DRIVENPROTOCOLEVOLUTION
The outer loop activates only after an entire cycle crosses theCycle Barrier(Fig. 2). Unlike the inner
GP loop, Loop B does not modify candidates within the current cycle. Instead, it audits accumulated
training and development evidence and proposes bounded protocol changes that take effect only in
the next cycle. Importantly, both diagnosis and protocol evolution are performed without access to
the held-out test set.

4 MAINEXPERIMENTS
We evaluate whether dual evolution improves out-of-sample performance under a reproducible
evaluation protocol. We benchmark EVOQUANTLABagainst four representative agent-based alpha
discovery baselines:AlphaAgent(Tang et al., 2025), which regularizes LLM factor exploration
against redundancy and signal decay;QuantaAlpha(Han et al., 2026), an evolutionary framework
optimizing formulaic alphas through LLM-driven operators;FactorMiner(Wang et al., 2026), a
self-evolving agent guided by accumulated skill and experience memory; andRD-Agent-Quant(Li
et al., 2025), a multi-agent framework coordinating data-centric factor mining and model optimization.
For a fair comparison, all methods execute their native workflows using the identical foundation
model (Claude Opus 5) while sharing the exact same point-in-time data, temporal splits, transaction-
cost assumptions, and evaluation suite. The out-of-sample (OOS) data remain strictly frozen and
unobserved during the factor discovery process, accessed solely during final validation.

5.2 EVOLUTION ACROSSCYCLES
We further trace how the system evolves across outer cycles rather than comparing only the initial
and final states. Figure 5 tracks a representative Crypto run, depicting key search events and the
cumulative equal-weight portfolio of L5-approved factors to contrast genuine search-space expansion
with redundant family refinement.
The trajectory exhibits a clear transition from exploration to exploitation. Initialized with four factors
at Cycle 0 (IR 1.57), early evolution targets momentum- and spread-based structures, elevating the
cumulative IR to 2.93 by Cycle 2. While Cycle 3 finds extra candidates, their highly overlapping
risk profile (ρ= 0.881) yields negligible gain, showing that factor quantity alone cannot bolster the
portfolio.
A larger improvement occurs in the middle of the run when evolution expands into a previously
underexplored factor family. After the rejected Cycle 4 proposals, Cycle 5 introduces coverage-tail
signals combined with CCI and contributes three new L5-approved factors, increasing the cumulative
IR from 2.67 to 3.21. Continued refinement of this family reaches the peak portfolio IR of 3.62 at
Cycle 6, together with an arithmetic return of 8.52%. These transitions suggest that the main benefit
of iterative evolution comes from discovering new predictive axes rather than merely recombining
candidates within an already saturated region of the search space.
Later cycles show diminishing returns and increasing redundancy. By Cycle 7, exploitation begins
to dominate novelty: four portfolio seats are proposed but only two correspond to unique additions,
while the cumulative IR slightly decreases from 3.62 to 3.59. The final recombination stage exhibits
a similar pattern. Although Cycle 10 continues to construct candidates from coverage, RSI, and CLV
signals, the audit identifies substantial overlap with earlier discoveries; deduplication reduces the
corresponding IR from 3.39 to 3.08. Overall, the trajectory shows that the evolution process is most
effective while it continues to open new factor families, whereas later cycles increasingly revisit
correlated structures and approach a performance plateau.

In this paper, we introduced EVOQUANTLAB, a training-free, dual-loop self-evolving framework
designed to tackle the critical challenge of backtest overfitting and trial multiplicity in automated quan-
titative research. By decoupling token-efficient symbolic exploration in an inner genetic-programming
loop from evidence-driven protocol evolution in an outer diagnostic loop, EVOQUANTLABestab-

Comprehensive evaluations across daily equities (CSI 500 and S&P 500) and high-frequency dig-

A DUAL-LOOPEXECUTIONPROCEDURE
For completeness, the outer-cycle procedure is given below. All state transitions are deterministic
except candidate and patch proposals made by the frozen API model.
1. Load protocol Πc and verify its content hash. Instantiate the task adapter from the immutable task
specification.
2. Run the unchanged L0–L6 GP search engine on train/dev data. Freeze the complete cycle artifact
bundle. Abort outer review if the inner run is incomplete.
3. Construct per-terminal evidence from use, survival, ablation, residual, fold, and redundancy
artifacts. Loop A emits lifecycle and budget decisions. Optional reference search emits proposal
cards only.
4. Construct a CandidateAuditInput for every finalist. Gate 1 emits an MLOverfitReport
and a priority-ordered list of target layers.
5. Gate 2 reviews layer evidence in that order. It emits typed local patches with evidence paths,
expected benefit, risks, and a next-cycle validation plan.
6. Reject patches that touch an immutable field, lack evidence, lack a regression test, or attempt to
change the completed cycle. Apply admitted patches to createΠ c+1 and compute its hash.
7. Atomically write the Loop A review, Gate 1 reports, Gate 2 review, next manifest, and outer
checkpoint. Resume only from the last atomic checkpoint.

C.2 TRADINGSTRATEGY
We implement execution-aware portfolio simulations tailored to the characteristics of equities and
digital assets.
Daily Equities (CSI 500 and S&P 500).For equity markets, all methods utilize a daily-bar
LightGBM paradigm based on pre-screened feature pools. The trading pipeline is simulated as
follows:
• Pre-OOS Feature Selection: Candidate factors undergo strict pre-screening using only
development data (train and validation sets). We remove exact duplicates, discard factors
with negative validation IC or IR, and greedily prune redundant factors based on the Pearson
correlation of their daily portfolio weights (candidates with an absolute correlation ≥0.70
against previously accepted factors are excluded). This retains 9 factors for CSI 500 and 20
factors for S&P 500.
• Model Training and Target: Features are rank-normalized and mean-centered cross-
sectionally each day. The LightGBM model is trained to predict the next-day cross-sectional
return ranking, with hyperparameters and early stopping (patience of 50) determined strictly
on the validation set without OOS lookahead (CSI 500: max depth 4, 15 leaves, learning
rate 0.02; S&P 500: max depth 5, 31 leaves, learning rate 0.03).
• Portfolio Construction: At the close of trading day t, the model generates score si,t for
stocki. The dollar-neutral portfolio weightw i,t is constructed as:

expression had a combination lift of −0.0096 over its strongest root arm, below the required 0.25.

not because its raw performance was weak, but because the additional structure failed to provide
incremental evidence.

D.3 CASE3: MODEL-SUGGESTEDKNOWLEDGEISTESTED, NOTTRUSTED
This is an example of how EVOQUANTLABhandles external knowledge. During reference ex-
traction, the model-assisted L1 pathway identified a signed-volume mechanism motivated by per-
sistent imbalanced order flow. The resulting terminal, book dimb524, entered the system as
PROPOSED NOT ACTIVE; it was not directly injected into the active search space.
The proposal was subsequently evaluated through a pre-registered paired-seed counterfactual com-
paring the aligned terminal against a deterministic time-shifted control under the same expression
and seed. For seeds 17 and 27, the improvements in minimum-fold score were respectively 0.206
and 0.243. Their median improvement,∆paired = 0.225, fell below the required 0.25 threshold. The
proposal was therefore assignedREJECTED PAIRED SEED.
This rejection propagated to the next-cycle protocol rather than remaining only as an analysis record.
The compiled policy assigned the terminal zero next-cycle budget and did not inject its formula.
Cycle 3 subsequently loaded the policy with book dimb524 included in both the cross-layer
banned and removed sets. Thus, the complete trace is: reference proposal→provisional terminal

This case illustrates an important distinction in EVOQUANTLAB: model-generated research knowl-

changes the search space only after surviving empirical validation under the frozen development
protocol.

G TASK-SPECIFICCONTRACTS
G.1 HOURLYHONGKONG LIMIT ORDER BOOK
The HK LOB adapter constructs hourly, point-in-time states from valid two-sided order-book obser-
vations. Its primary label is the next-hour return. Within-hour aggregation uses only observations
available before the bar closes; rolling state resets at the midday break and end of day, and no target
may cross a session boundary. Tick size follows the price-dependent market schedule rather than a
constant. Reports use IC, ICIR, IR, annualized return, total return, and pre-registered factor decay,
with coverage, turnover, cost sensitivity, and active/quiet regimes as supporting diagnostics.
G.2 DAILY CROSS-SECTIONAL EQUITIES
Daily adapters form point-in-time time–asset panels from OHLCV , apply all rolling operations with
past data only, and fit cross-sectional transformations within fold. The primary label is next-day return
with a pre-registered cross-sectional normalization. Reports include daily IC, RankIC, cross-sectional
R2, worst-fold score, direction consistency, liquidity/sector/size concentration, and a costed top-K
portfolio as a secondary diagnostic. CSI 500 and S&P 500 are judged separately.
G.3 HOURLY CRYPTO CROSS-SECTION
The crypto adapter uses an hourly asset panel, a historical liquidity universe, and participant-flow
terminals whose daily inputs are lagged before hourly broadcast. Finalist gates require phase stability,
turnover and cost checks, story ablation, residual survival against registered carriers, limited finalist
correlation, and a sign-reversal control. The OOS interval is declared in the task contract and
physically withheld.

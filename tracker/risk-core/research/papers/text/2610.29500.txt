Auditing LLM Alpha-Factor Mining:
Metric Provenance and Full-Cohort Reanalysis
Yufeng He
The University of Hong Kong
he-yufeng@connect.hku.hk
Abstract
Reliable evaluation of LLM-based alpha min-
ing requires more than positive backtest sum-
maries. We audit a preserved project at three
levels: historical run counters, outcome re-
tention, and a separately scoped public re-
analysis. Three historical runs report 67, 20,
and 11 keep decisions, but the comparison
mixes metric populations: a mean over kept
candidates is compared with a mean of per-
round maxima, and successful evaluations with
all returned outcomes. These artifacts can-
not identify an architecture advantage. A re-
leased 1,837-expression catalog also excludes
non-positive OOS outcomes, making its zero-
reversal rate non-informative. We then account
for all 1,047 unique expressions in a frozen, IS-
screened preserved pool using forty A-shares
and 2024–2025 rank associations. A versioned
public-operator correction yields 923 scorable
records, 99 with insufficient valid dates, and 25
unsupported, without modifying the other 990
prior diagnostics. Among scorable records, 671
have positive and 252 negative signed mean
rank-IC. Simple untrained references also show
positive associations, so this distribution does
not establish incremental agent skill or prof-
itability. The case study motivates explicit met-
ric grains, immutable cohort accounting, and
provenance-aware interpretation rather than ar-
chitectural rankings from incomplete records.
1 Introduction
LLM-based factor mining combines expression
generation, a backtester, and selection rules. Its
appeal is practical: models can propose candidate
signals, use feedback, and revise a search direc-
tion. Yet a pipeline can produce plausible summary
tables without preserving the evidence needed to
interpret them. A field namedSharpedoes not spec-
ify which population was averaged; a count named
backtesteddoes not establish whether failures are
included; and a positive-only artifact cannot esti-
mate the frequency of negative outcomes.
We study these problems through a pre-
served project containing a three-role planning–
generation–review pipeline and a twenty-specialist
evolutionary workflow. The historical reports ini-
tially suggested an architectural difference. Our
audit instead asks:which conclusions do the coun-
ters, source paths, and retained artifacts support?
We do not introduce a new mining algorithm or
claim superiority over prior systems.
The study contributes (1) a source-and-report
audit exposing incompatible metric grains and in-
complete configuration/state provenance (§4); (2)
an outcome-retention audit of a 1,837-expression
catalog (§5); and (3) a full-cohort public reanalysis
of 1,047 frozen expressions, retaining non-scorable
records and a versioned implementation correction
(§6). These are related project artifacts, not inter-
changeable experimental populations.
2 Related Work
Agentic generate–evaluate systems and evolution-
ary expression search are active approaches to al-
pha mining [1, 2, 3]. They motivate careful evalua-
tion of search outcomes, but our contribution is an
artifact-level case study rather than a new search
method or a benchmark against those systems. The
findings do not imply that other systems lack ad-
equate controls. They identify concrete ways in
which this project’s reporting and retained artifacts
fail to support its initial interpretation.
3 Workflow and Evidence Scope
The preserved three-role workflow has a Re-
searcher that plans a direction, an Implementor that
generates and tests expressions, and a Reviewer.
In the inspected source, keep/discard decisions
are programmatic. The reviewer then attempts an
LLM-generated next-step suggestion, with a rule
fallback on failure. A role name or model config-
uration is not a verified successful model request,

4 Historical Run-Report Audit
4.1 Recoverable counters
Each report contains twenty rounds. Table 1 pre-
serves their recoverable counters without present-
ing unlike stages as one metric. Keep decisions are
not proved counts of distinct factors surviving in
the final pool. Missing cells are unavailable at a
harmonized stage, not zero values.
The contemporaneous narrative labels the
pipeline generator as Claude Opus 4.6, S-A spe-
cialists as Claude Sonnet 4.5, and S-B specialists
as Claude Opus 4.6. Some report labels are hard-
coded in the template rather than recovered from
requests. We retain them only as reported configu-
ration labels, not a verified model-quality ranking
or backend identity.
4.2 Metric populations do not align
The earlier mean-Sharpe comparison is not a
common-population mean. The pipeline collec-
tor appends kept-candidate Sharpes, while the
swarm collector appends positive per-round max-
ima. Three-decimal report entries reproduce aver-
ages of approximately 2.227 over 67 kept rows and
2.433 over 20 round maxima, matching the earlier
rounded 2.23 versus 2.43 display. We do not retain
this as a performance comparison.
The maximum also changes population: the
pipeline’s kept-candidate maximum is 2.449,
whereas its evaluated round summaries reach 3.171;
the swarm’s 4.206 is an evaluated round maximum
and need not be kept. These values cannot establish
superior outlier discovery.
Likewise, 234 is the pipeline’s reported
successful-outcome count, but 816 is the swarm’s
returned-outcome count, including possible fail-
ures and additional sign-flip evaluations. Six S-
A rounds have more outcome records than newly
generated proposals. The thin report does not re-
cover a success-only denominator, so 67/234 ver-
sus 20/816 is not a verified common-denominator
acceptance-rate comparison.
4.3 Categories and state provenance
The pipeline’s four category labels cover all round
directives; the swarm’s two productive labels cover
mapped specialists with keep decisions. These are
different populations. S-A’s agent leaderboard at-
tributes 8 keep decisions while the run counter is
20; S-B attributes 9 while its counter is 11. The
source’s sign-flip route can fall outside configured-
specialist attribution, but the missing per-event at-
tribution has not been reconstructed.
The comparison code runs the pipeline before
the swarm without an explicit initial-pool snap-
shot/reset binding. Together with missing exact
executed versions and request-level provenance,
this prevents treating the reports as a controlled
experiment. More seeds alone would not repair
incompatible counters: future work must align ac-
counting stages, selection policies, initial state and
resource budgets before using independent runs for
inference.

6 Full-Cohort Public Reanalysis
We separately evaluate a frozen list of 1,047 unique
expressions selected from 3,114 preserved input
rows using the stated IS screen. This does not es-
tablish a census of all generated candidates or a
historically blind holdout. The broader preserved
pool is not a matched replay of the three historical
keep totals. All IDs are retained regardless of sub-
sequent public-evaluator outcome; no expression
is chosen by its new score.

and provider-adjusted daily views through BaoS-

6.1 Fixed reference-signal context
After the cohort analysis, we specify six fixed ref-
erence signals before computing their outcomes.
All are reported in Table 4; signs and horizons are
not selected based on these scores. They use the
same snapshot and scoring convention. Reversal
references negate nominal trailing close changes;
the twenty-day reference retains the positive orien-
tation.
V olume and recent-change references also have
positive associations. Therefore a positive IC alone
does not establish incremental agent skill; we have
not estimated incremental prediction beyond these
references. Factors can have different eligible dates
and stocks, so their cohort median and the stan-
dalone reference means do not establish matched-
sample outperformance. No reference is selected
for a profitability or significance claim.
7 Implications and Limitations
The defensible contribution is an evaluation-audit
case study, not an architectural ranking. Histor-
ical reports preserve different keep counters but
not verified common-grain Sharpe, rate, or cate-
gory comparisons. Single runs, unequal budgets,
unbound starting state and uncertain executed con-
figurations further limit interpretation. The public
cohort comes from a broader preserved pool and
cannot be assigned back to the three run conditions
from the available records.
The reanalysis completes data collection and as-
signs an explicit outcome to every frozen expres-
sion. It still covers one market and a convenience
stock universe. It uses public rather than native
semantics; factor coverage varies and five-day la-
bels overlap. The original proprietary evaluator
and full historical request traces are not released.
The reference checks were planned after the cohort
analysis, not as part of its original primary protocol.
A stronger study would fix common masks and
comparators, retain generation and run identities,
and test repeated systems under matched budgets
and a suitable temporal design.
8 Conclusion
Preserved artifacts can support an audit even
when they cannot support a performance rank-
ing. This case reveals metric-population mis-
matches, outcome-conditioned retention, and a pub-
lic conditional-operator defect. A complete, ver-
sioned reanalysis retains all 1,047 IDs, including
124 non-scorable records, without treating positive
associations as profitability or architecture effects.
Reliable future comparisons require aligned defi-
nitions and provenance before additional scale or
significance testing.
Ethics Statement
The new analysis uses publicly accessible histori-
cal market data for research, makes no live-trading
or financial-advice claim, and sends no trading or-
ders. Failed and non-scorable records are preserved.
Proprietary components and raw provider data are
not represented as publicly redistributable artifacts;
the SDK license is not a data-redistribution grant.
Model labels are reported with provenance limits
rather than as verified backend identities.
AI-assistant disclosure.AI assistants supported
literature review, audit design, implementation,
statistical checking and manuscript preparation.
Numerical findings are computed from preserved
source records and versioned reanalysis outputs,
not generated as substitutes for observations. No
human-annotation study or independent human re-
view is claimed. The author remains responsible
for the content.

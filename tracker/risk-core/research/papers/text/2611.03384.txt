Abstract
Financial research agents can generate and monitor trading rules
faster than a human can document the resulting selection process.
Candidates appear at data-dependent times, weak rules are aban-
doned, and promising ones are watched until they cross a reporting
threshold. We introducePromote, a protocol that assigns every
candidate predictable testing wealth at birth and accumulates a
cost-aware e-process only from subsequent returns. If lifetime al-
locations sum to at most one, a weighted Ville argument bounds
the probability of ever promoting a rule whose declared bounded
net score has always had nonpositive conditional mean. The re-
sult permits dependent candidates, adaptive births, and indefinite
monitoring. We audit the implementation in two settings. A ret-
rospective pseudo-out-of-sample replay contains 24 panel–rule
hypotheses, 3,000 shared-sign null paths, injected alternatives, and
public ETF and industry data. Repeated unadjusted tests produce a
family crossing in 94.5% of null paths, versus 0.0% forPromote; at
a 15-bp aligned ETF-component perturbation,Promotehas 70.1%
target power and median crossing session 2,327. A separate exact
mean-zero experiment lets an agent select candidate identities and
birth times from past scores; the funded gate has 1.77% false-family
crossings across 3,000 programs. The market replay produces zero
promotions after costs. The certificate controls historical false pro-
motion under the declared null; it is not a certificate that an earlier
edge remains profitable at deployment.

1 Introduction
A conventional backtest has a nominal end date. Research rarely
does. An analyst looks at interim results, changes the universe, adds
a seasonal interaction, drops an unpromising rule, and continues
when the evidence is close. An autonomous research agent can
perform the same sequence at machine speed. Treating the final
rule as if it came from one fixed test understates the number of
opportunities the system had to be lucky.
This failure is familiar in empirical finance. Data-snooping cor-
rections compare many strategies on a common terminal sample
[9, 18, 27], while empirical-finance protocols quantify selection and
backtest overfitting [1, 2, 10]. Neither device, by itself, authorizes
continuous inspection of a library whose membership is decided
from the observed history. The relevant error event is operational:
did the research process ever promote a strategy whose declared net
score has no positive conditional mean?
Test martingales and e-values are well suited to this question
because they remain valid at stopping times [16, 19, 22, 23]. Their
use still requires a protocol. A newly proposed rule must not inherit
evidence from before it was specified; adding near-duplicates must
consume a finite research budget; transaction costs must be known
before the return used to update the test; and trading must begin
after, rather than on, the boundary-crossing observation. Those
details are where an apparently safe backtest can leak.
Promoteturns them into a ledger. At a predictable birth time,
candidateℎ receives initial testing wealth𝜋ℎ. Subsequent bounded,
cost-adjusted payoffs update an e-process with bets chosen from
the past. Promotion occurs at the first crossing of1/(𝛼𝜋ℎ). If the
total wealth assigned over the life of the research program is at
most one, a weighted Ville argument controls the probability of any
false promotion by𝛼. The library may be dependent, monitored at
different frequencies, and expanded in response to old data.
Contributions.The statistical ingredients are established; our
contribution is to make them operational for an adaptive financial-
research workflow:

Conditioning is what permits adaptive research: pre-birth ob-
servations may determine the rule’s code, identity, and mass, but
cannot enter its post-birth product. Abandonment can set future
bets to zero but does not replenish the account; the program already
enjoyed a crossing opportunity. Revival, rescaling, or a nearby pa-
rameterization requires another debit. Zero-mass candidates are
ineligible. These rules block free restarts and hidden near-duplicate
tests.
This theorem controls familywise error, a demanding criterion
appropriate to an automated promotion gate. If the objective is
discovery rather than certification, stopped e-values can instead be
passed to e-BH, which controls FDR under arbitrary dependence
[25]. That is a different operating point and is not used for the
primary experiment.
3.3 Promotion is not same-period trading
The crossing observation belongs to the test, not to post-promotion
performance. If𝜏ℎ <∞ , live or holdout exposure starts at𝜏ℎ+1. This
one-period separation prevents the return that triggers promotion
from being counted as evidence of deployed profitability. Statistical
wealth is also not trading capital:𝐸ℎ,𝑡 may be highly volatile even
when the economic position is small.
For a promoted rule, we therefore maintain two ledgers. The ev-
idence ledger continues to update (3); the economic ledger records
the actual net return from the next executable interval. Demotion
can be governed by a separately registered rule, but optional re-
moval does not retroactively change the false-promotion guarantee.
Production use therefore requires a distinct current-regime or for-
ward deployment check. A predictable epoch restart can provide
one, but it must be funded as a new test; deleting old factors from
the original product would break anytime validity.
3.4 State, corrections, and recovery
Each update reads the committed state at𝑡− 1, joins one point-in-
time outcome, and writes one state for𝑡. Its unique key combines
the candidate fingerprint, event time, and data-version fingerprint.
Replaying a key after a crash returns the stored row instead of
multiplying an e-factor twice; partial writes are not evidence.
Event time and ingestion time remain separate. Revised data or
code cannot silently overwrite an old factor: the ledger retains the
original row, flags the certificate, and computes a versioned diagnos-
tic. Any corrected series used for authorization starts prospectively
as a newly funded stream. Thus a reviewer can reconstruct what
the gate actually knew when it acted.
Automated checks enforce idempotent replay, no influence from
post-𝑡 inputs, and cumulative birth debits that never decrease or
exceed one. These target double counting, look-ahead joins, and
budget recycling.

4 Experimental Design
4.1 Retrospective pseudo-out-of-sample library
The implementation crosses three asset panels with eight calen-
dar definitions, giving 24 panel–rule hypotheses. This is the fixed-
family special case of Theorem 1: within the replay, all candidates
are frozen before the 2015 segment is processed and receive equal
wealth. Adaptive births and data-dependent allocations are not ex-

exact mean-zero score law. The panels are six equity ETFs (SPY,
QQQ, IWM, EFA, EEM, and VNQ), five defensive or diversifying

Fama–French industries. The rules are turn of month, first three
and last three trading days, Monday, Friday, last three trading days
of a quarter, January, and the third Friday of a month. The common

4.4 Public-market replay
The market study uses a July 31, 2026 frozen snapshot: Yahoo
adjusted closes for the ETFs and the Ken French Data Library’s
value-weighted ten-industry file. Source and derived-file SHA-256
hashes accompany the artifact; the replay itself performs no net-
work retrieval. Returns, candidate indicators, costs, and evidence
are computed from information available at each decision. The
study reports the entire promotion ledger, including candidates
that never cross. The split and library were designed retrospec-
tively, and trading-day ranks are reconstructed from the realized
exchange calendar. No test return calibrates direction, scale, bet
grid, or prior wealth, but one historical path cannot establish the
conditional null in (2).
For any promoted strategy, exposure begins one interval after
crossing and pays exact one-way turnover under the declared cost
convention. Program-level wealth includes cash before activation;
the complete daily ledger permits a separate live-only analysis
when a crossing occurs.

5.4 What the artifacts establish
The rowwise replay verifies three deterministic invariants: the 24
fixed-family weights sum to one, every test e-process restarts at one,
and no promoted return is credited before the interval following a
crossing. The adaptive simulation checks variable birth times, iden-
tities, masses, and family sizes under an exact mean-zero law. Seeds
20260731 and 20262731, the complete hypothesis ledger, summary

These checks do not establish a stable positive conditional mean

validation data or testing wealth.

6 Relation to Existing Work
Backtest overfitting has been studied through data-snooping tests,
superior-predictive-ability tests, multiple-testing adjustments, and
selection diagnostics [2, 9, 10, 18, 27]. These methods address closely
related selection problems, usually for a collection evaluated at
a terminal sample size. Our target is the event generated by an
ongoing research system: a false strategy is promoted at some
random time after a data-dependent proposal.
Repeated interaction with a holdout set is adaptive data analysis;
the reusable-holdout framework gives a privacy-based route to
valid reuse [6]. Asynchronous online testing handles overlapping
sequential experiments [29]. Neither is a cost-aware familywise
promotion ledger for financial strategies.
Online multiple-testing methods allocate error across a sequence
of hypotheses, including alpha-investing and adaptive FDR rules
[7, 13, 17]. Our pathwise masses are deliberately simpler: they are a
weighted online Bonferroni allocation for familywise control, with
no claim of wealth recycling. That conservative choice makes the
audit rule transparent when candidate identities and birth times
are selected from the past.
E-values provide the underlying evidence currency [ 23], and
safe testing, betting-based inference, and confidence sequences ex-
plain their optional-stopping behavior [ 8, 12, 16, 26]. The e-BH
procedure extends the framework to false-discovery control un-
der arbitrary dependence [25]. Sequential e-values have also been
used to evaluate probability forecasts and backtest financial risk
measures [11, 24]; those applications test forecasts, whereas our
unit is a strategy-promotion workflow. We use the conservative
wealth-weighted crossing rule because one false launch can matter
operationally. Our contribution is its implementation as a strategy-
level gate, an exact adaptive-birth audit, and a reproducible market
ledger with costs and delayed deployment.
Calendar and technical trading rules provide a demanding case
study because their design space is easy to expand and many re-
ported effects are sample-sensitive [3, 14, 20, 21]. More broadly, the
attenuation of published return predictability and the prevalence
of false anomaly rejections motivate a strict separation between
discovery and deployment [5, 15]. The candidate generator may
instead be a language model, feature search, or a human, provided
its executable definition and wealth are fixed before scoring. ICAIF
studies multiscale evaluation of financial time-series foundation
models [4], and AAAI studies trading-policy generalization beyond
offline data [28]. Neither controls the probability of ever promoting
a null strategy during adaptive search.

7 Limitations and Governance
The guarantee is narrow by design. It concerns the clipped, cost-
adjusted score and a null that is uniformly nonpositive after birth;
it does not certify that a formerly positive edge remains profitable.
Clipping can also suppress rare economic losses. It occurs in 128
of 68,832 historical candidate–session scores (0.186%), so every
evidence row is retained beside its unclipped return.
The two simulations answer different questions. Shared indepen-
dent signs preserve magnitudes and contemporaneous cross-rule
dependence, but remove serial sign dependence and freeze observed
cost fractions; the resulting null is generally strict. The adaptive
audit is exactly mean zero and exercises selection and staggered
births, but its iid Rademacher scores are not a market model. Neither
construction asserts that realized returns satisfy (2).
Validity is only as trustworthy as the filtration. Revised data,
survivorship-biased universes, pretrained-model knowledge of the
replay era, or same-close execution can import future information.
A production agent must therefore retain data and prompt hashes,
candidate births, allocations, and rejected proposals. An idea with-
out a deterministic executable rule receives no testing wealth.
The economic replay uses adjusted closes and zero cash yield.
Five-basis-point turnover costs are explicit, but financing, borrow
fees, bid–ask dynamics, impact, taxes, and capacity are absent;
French industries are not directly tradable. The design and pseudo-
out-of-sample periods were chosen retrospectively, and realized
trading-day ranks can encode rare unscheduled closures. These
facts make Table 5 an audit, not a live performance claim.
Finally, familywise control becomes conservative when an agent
proposes many correlated variants. Hierarchical predictable allo-
cations expose but do not eliminate that cost; discovery-oriented
systems may prefer FDR control. In all cases, promotion should
trigger a separately governed forward evaluation rather than unre-
stricted scaling.
In production, a crossing is an authorization request, not an
order. A pre-trade gate still checks data availability, venue and
borrow eligibility, spread and impact, concentration, turnover, and
duplicate portfolio exposure. It may veto or cap a trade without
changing the evidence. A predictable risk kill switch may stop
exposure or betting, but cannot reclaim testing mass or justify a
same-day restart.
The ledger should fail closed: missing prices, ambiguous calen-
dars, stale features, or a code-hash mismatch suspend updates and
deployment until an operator records the resolution and first usable
session. The e-process asks whether the historical null was crossed;
operations controls ask whether the position is safe to trade now.
7.1 Certificate lifecycle under failure
A certificate moves through registered, accumulating, crossed, au-
thorized, suspended, and expired states. Crossing concerns one
frozen version; authorization is separate and time limited. Expiry
requires a funded epoch from Section 6.1; corrected history cannot
backdate promotion.
The release manifest binds the fingerprint to its rule package,
data vintage, calendar, costs, bet grid, budget, and expiry. This
supports audits of replay, post-decision inputs, and the first trade.
It does not prove profitability; it prevents implementation changes
from voiding the theorem’s premises.
8 Conclusion
Adaptive research changes the unit of inference. The relevant object
is the sequence of proposals, inspections, and stopping decisions,
not one terminal backtest.Promotegives that process a finite error
budget: candidates inherit no evidence and must cross a cost-aware,
wealth-adjusted boundary. The guarantee survives continuous mon-
itoring and an adaptively expanding library.
The experiments expose the price: repeated inspection finds win-
ners readily, whereas the valid gate may wait for weak effects. That
delay is the cost of an optional-stopping guarantee, not evidence
of current profitability. Frictions, scale, and forward risk remain
deployment questions.

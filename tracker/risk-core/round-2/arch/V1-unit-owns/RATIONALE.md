# V1, the unit owns it: what it buys and costs

**Buys.**
- **One thing to understand.** A unit's document says everything it obeys. A newcomer, a copilot and a developer read the same file. No level exists above it for size.
- **Least new contract.** The seats exist (`qml/unit/model.py:470-492`) and the refusals are named. Most PRs lift a refusal. One shared handler already runs both hosts, so same arithmetic is inherited, not built.
- **Standing alone is real.** A unit with no pod and no group is the default path, not an empty-pod case. A brake travels with its unit into a backtest.
- **Performance by default.** Own full size under one cut-only cap. Nothing above a unit can raise or size it, and only one small thing can cut it.
- **The record stays clean.** A size edit keeps the logic record, by a real partition that also fixes a live hazard (probe 4).

**Costs, stated fairly.**
- **Pod edits are mass edits.** About 150 s of compile twice and 200 new versions per edit, where Shape B writes one document. It works, but it's bulk work behind one click, and a pod is not a live object anyone can point at at run time, except through provenance.
- **Cross-unit logic is thin on purpose.** Caps and facts cover parent limits, day stops, chorus, bet keys. "Best of those firing" can't be said. Rules that need more become off-path readings, and those are late.
- **One-unit backtests don't see the book.** The driver for a group exists only from slice 13.
- **One thread.** Atomic caps come free, but serial sends at a bell and a hung block stop all 200. A bad block in Shape B's F4 B stalls one pod.
- **Stages must be foreseen.** Undeclared ones are edits.
- **Pod on his screen.** He pictures a pod owning money and method. Here the money is on a group and the method is stamped. The card can show one thing, but two documents sit behind it.

**Against the others.**
- **Shape B (split pod)** is simply better at: one edit, one document, comparing whole setups, and a group stage that could batch. It costs placement fields, a pod reference, a size trail crossing two stages, and the most contract.
- **Shape C (live group)** is better at: agents watching pods, one rank table. It costs a unit that can't stand alone, and the unit's own sockets and five halts go unused.
- **F4 B** is better at fault isolation. **F5 C** is better for a scalper's tighter cut at a stage. **F2 A** gives bands he can see move.

**Graft worth taking whichever wins.** The logic and risk partition (AD-2, AD-3), `opened_by` on the logic fingerprint, compiled-in stages (AD-7), the frontier outbox with exits first (AD-17), `move_risk` (AD-6).

**What would make me abandon it.**
- A pod that needs a joint decision across members at the same instant, and he wants that pod.
- Pod edits becoming frequent, say daily across hundreds of units, so bulk versions become noise.
- A measured send delay at the bell that harms fills and can't be fixed with an async outbox.
- A ruling on Q6 requiring a G run per member per pod edit: that hurts every shape, but this one most.

**Honest size.** About 37 PRs. Not small. Slices 1 to 11, 23 PRs, are a complete single-unit baseline with no group or pod at all; if "too much machine" is the fear, that is this shape's best argument. Slices 12 to 14, 8 PRs, are the node chain, the group and pods; the last 6 are shipping, the fleet watch, identity and step 3.

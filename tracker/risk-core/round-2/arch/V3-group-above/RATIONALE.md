# V3 rationale

**What it buys.**
- One edit for 200 units, and it moves no unit fingerprint. Because size and brakes left the unit, the funnel's largest named rework risk (E2 row 3) disappears by construction, rather than through a second fingerprint.
- After format 5, new brakes, sizers and modifiers need no compiler bump. K8 is rarely paid.
- Shared limits, same-bet counting, parent limits and pod-pool cushions need no cross-unit mechanism, because the evaluator already sees every member. ORB across opens and his "own size under a parent limit" are simply rows.
- A stage change costs units nothing: their record and fingerprint stay.
- One rank table, one trail, one thing for agents to watch.
- Block code runs in a process with no keys, which no other arrangement gives at the same price.
- Cheap search over sizing: proposal tapes replayed under pod drafts.
- The five shipped halts and the shipped sizer run with their block API unchanged. Only their host moves. "Their sockets go unused" is true of the unit's sockets and false of the blocks.

**What it costs.**
- **Correlated failure.** An evaluator bug, or one common block going bad, stops entries for every member of a pod. A unit-owned design loses one unit. Exits are safe; entries are not.
- **A unit cannot open a trade alone.** V7 is restated. A unit isn't a self-contained thing to move around without its pod template.
- **Slices 8 and 9 are extra machine,** about 6 of about 50 PRs: a worker process per pod, a relay, reservations, multi-member replay. "Too much machine" is a fair charge against the worker. F4 B is what the brief assigned; inline evaluation in the node would remove three PRs and put block code beside the keys.
- **Two proof objects.** A unit's money result means nothing apart from its pod. Methodology inherits that.
- A scalper's brake sits one process away, one tick late, and its exits' width cannot change by stage without a new unit version.
- An account-wide Library brake cannot be atomic. A mandatory signed list is a real limit on the canvas.
- Copilots must learn which output is a unit version and which a pod version.
- F3 A means a new fast sensor needs a signed package change, and SQS and the MIS keep separate spread measures.

**Where another version is simply better.**
- A compiled-in design is better at "a unit stands alone", at having least new contract, and at failure blast radius.
- A split-by-scope design is better at keeping a scalper's brake in its own call, and at leaving QML's unit sockets in use. It is also better for a fleet of unrelated units with no shared limits, where the pod here is overhead.
- F3 B's open sensor and shared curve artifact are better for his departments and for one spread measure. I would graft them if F3 is free at the fold; nothing in the evaluator resists it.
- `/ask` is a graft from the unit-owned shapes, and an honest admission that one document cannot be the whole truth.

**What would make me abandon it.**
1. Slice 0 shows the node's single thread cannot step 200 units at 1m bars. Units would need to move out of the node process too, and the shape's cost grows past its gain.
2. In practice most pods hold one or two members. Then the level above is ceremony, and compiled-in plus a thin group wins.
3. Evaluator faults prove common enough that pods stop entries often. Then a design with no single point per pod wins.
4. He answers that a unit must be able to trade by itself. Then the shape fails a stated line.

**My honest view.** It delivers his 200-unit picture and his fund picture best. It is the wrong choice if the first year is a few scalpers on a few hundred dollars, except that a pod of one costs little to run and the money-path code is one path. The best grafts for the fold, whatever shape wins: the typed `Amount`, the two-phase bar, `Proposal` as a recorded tape, and reservations.

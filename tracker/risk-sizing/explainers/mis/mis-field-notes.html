QMX · market information system

MIS Field Notes

The MIS reads every market QMX trades and publishes what it sees: how volatile a market is against its own usual level, whether it is trending or ranging, how wide the spread is, and which session or event window is open, each stamped with the time it was read.

Units pick from these readings if they want them. No unit needs the MIS to trade: if it stops, every unit keeps trading on its own conditions. It starts with plain indicators and simple statistics that need no training, and it takes the models you train later in the Lab without any unit changing.

How fast each reading reacts

Minutes on 1-minute bars, at one false alarm per 10 trading days. The dot is a strong change, the ring a weak one. Numbers come from simulations on made-up bars, so real markets will be somewhat slower. Pick a market class to see which readings it gets.

What each market class gets first

Every class
  • Volatility against usual, from bar ranges
  • Volatility state: quiet, normal, elevated, stressed
  • Spread against usual, and spread quality
  • Tick rate against usual (never called volume)
  • Quote gaps, feed state, failing sensors
  • Liquidity stress
  • Small change detectors
  • Efficiency ratio, variance ratio, Hurst (1,000 points at least)
  • Trend against range: your meaning of regime
  • Calendar states: sessions, holidays, dead zones
Forex adds

    On your PC only: currency strength, dispersion (how far related markets drift apart), crowding counts (how many units sit on one market), and dated readings between pairs.

    What the research found

    Where it runs, and what you see

    Where it runs
    • On each trading node. The node runs the readings that need every tick: spread, quote gaps, feed state, tick rate, depth, order flow. A small MIS app beside it runs the bar, session and day readings.
    • On your PC. The slow readings for markets no node trades, the readings that compare markets, the board, and any model in shadow. It works alone on Library history.
    • One writer per reading. Your PC never recomputes what a node already publishes.
    • Everything comes home to the Library, so QMB, the copilot and your screens see one history.
    What you see
    • A Readings section on each node's page: that node's markets, their readings now and recently, each with its health word, and a badge when a unit used a fallback.
    • The MIS tab on your PC: every market and reading, its version, clock and health word, which units read it, a history chart of each change of state, and a shadow model beside the default with your yes to make it live.
    • The copilot: ask what the MIS read for a market last week and get the stored answer.
    How units use it without depending on it
    • A unit lists the readings it uses, and for each one a fallback: compute it itself, treat it as absent, or take no new entries.
    • A unit, QMB and the MIS run the same reading code, so a backtest and live trading see the same number. A replay of one recorded day checks it to the last digit.
    • A reading older than its freshness limit counts as absent: 2 seconds for tick readings, one bar, one session or one day for the others.
    • A build test stops the MIS and checks that a dummy unit still trades.

    Build order: the MIS on your PC lands in stage 5 of 8, after QML and the dictionary. The MIS beside each node lands with the node in stage 7.

    Later, through the Lab: how a model replaces a default

    1. A model trains in the Lab on Library history, pooled by class or by underlying, never one per market.
    2. It lands with its data fingerprint, code version and seed.
    3. It runs on your PC beside the default and writes shadow records.
    4. You compare the two on the MIS tab and say yes.
    5. The reading keeps its name and shape; only its version moves, so no unit changes.

    Refits run monthly in shadow. Agents may propose new readings; they run in shadow too and go live only on your yes. To prove the MIS helps a unit, QMB replays it twice: once with readings stamped at entry, once with readings computed afterwards. The gap shows how much a backtest would overstate the MIS.

    Open questions for later

    1. Futures data. Pay for an exchange feed, which gives real futures order flow and lets a gold CFD read the COMEX tape, or stay on broker-side estimates. A placeholder ships either way.
    2. The word regime. It now means trend against range, and the old volatility labels become volatility state. Keep it, or veto it.
    3. Your own labels. May the weekly market labels you and the copilot write train models, in shadow only?
    4. One market apart. When a market behaves unlike its class, does it get its own pack version, or only its own usual levels, which every market already has?
    5. The board on the server. Should units on a node see which of their markets the board favours now? It ships last and optional.

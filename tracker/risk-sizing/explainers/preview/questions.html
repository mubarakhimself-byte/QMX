Your 8 questions

Only real ambiguity: your intent, words, money or irreversible scope. Each has a recommended answer, so "yes" accepts it.

Q1Same logic on another market: new unit or same unit?

On 13 Oct you said a variant on a new timeframe becomes a brand new strategy. On 20 Oct you wrote that 1m and 5m are two registry entries, each with its own proof.

RecommendedA new instrument, timeframe or broker makes a sibling unit: complete on its own, self-calibrating from its own journal, linked in one family tree. The family shares one try count, and siblings count as one bet where they move together. Sessions and sniping windows stay inside one unit, as its session profile.
Q2When is something "new"?

Needed so tries are counted fairly and journals stay clean.

RecommendedChanging the logic or an exit makes a new version linked to its parent. Moving a setting inside its range keeps the same unit, but each value tried counts as a try. The unit recalibrating its own risk is a journal entry. A model's rewrite is a new unit marked "rewritten from", in the same family.
Q3Who promotes, and small new variants?

Your click admits each unit to live, from its journal page. This reopens the written rule "no per-bot paper or probation".

RecommendedA sibling of a unit you already admitted may go live without your click, only at the smallest lot, paid from a monthly evidence budget you set (start: 5% of equity a month), and only after its own backtest and stress test. It grows only as its own trades prove it. A new broker or a new kind of market still needs your click.
Q4May size grow back after a cut?

Example: a unit drops from 0.5% to 0.3% risk after a bad week.

RecommendedYes, but only after fresh trades show the edge is still there (say 60 new trades), after a minimum time, and never above the ranges you set. If size could only shrink, normal bad patches would add up and every unit would sit at the minimum within a year. The one-way ratchet goes on the account floor instead.
Q5House money: everywhere, or only where it helps?

House money and reverse house money stay.

RecommendedEach unit switches them on only when its own record shows streaks (for example: after a win it wins 60% of the time, after a loss 45%). If wins and losses come in random order, pressing only adds risk, and loss points do the safety job.
Q6Keep the word "unit"?

You used "bot", "algo" and "trading algorithm"; Claude coined "unit".

RecommendedKeep "unit" in QMX, meaning one complete self-calibrating trading algorithm, and say "bot" freely in conversation.
Q7Split the router?

Your seed said the node holds the MIS.

RecommendedPut the planning half on the PC (any machine later) and the cheap live checks on the node. The node always runs from its cached plan, so a PC that is off only means the plan stops being refreshed. After the plan expires, no new entries; open trades and the kill switch carry on.
Q8Scalper types and seed additions?

From the markets research and the router report.

RecommendedKeep 5 types (momentum, mean reversion, volatility expansion, session open, VWAP). Split the order-flow sniper by data type; make liquidity window and funding router settings, not types. Drop cross-venue latency. Add the liquidity-sweep reversal and the passive maker, plus a guard that flags martingale and grid.

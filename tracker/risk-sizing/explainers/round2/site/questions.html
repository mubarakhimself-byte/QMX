Your 8 questions

Each question is explained first, then its options. No answer is picked or recommended. We go through them one at a time.

1Account guard and pod

Why it matters

Your lean is that the pod is the BMS, and the BMS already did the guard's jobs. The real risk to weigh: whether a copilot remaking a pod can ever lower the safety floor.

Options

aOne objectPod = BMS = guard + shared budget + same-bet limits, one per account. Simplest; one edit can reach the floor.
bTwo layersA fixed guard per account nobody can remove; pod templates above it that a copilot may remake.
cOne shape, locked blockEvery pod template carries the guard as a part that can't be edited, checked by code.

See: Guard and pod

2What your click admits

Why it matters

The router now directs and never switches, and only you promote. This decides what 'admitted' covers, so the router knows where it may direct live trades.

Options

aOne unit on one marketEvery new market needs a new click.
bAn envelope you tickMarkets, sessions, brokers. The router directs live only inside; outside becomes a paper sibling proposal.
cAnything the unit's conditions allowWidest: the unit's own needs decide.

See: The admission envelope

3With the router off

Why it matters

Units stay independent, so they keep trading when the router is down. The question is how far.

Options

aHome market onlyThe safest; loses the rest of the envelope.
bThe whole admitted envelopeOn the unit's own conditions.
cFollow the last offers until they expireThen fall back to home.

See: Try the three 'Router off' buttons

4Units reading market labels

Why it matters

In your dictation you leaned toward units reading both live prices and the market labels. The 20 October note said unit rules never read them. If they do, part of the label system moves inside the unit.

Options

aNeverLabels serve the router and guards only.
bOptional, with a declared fallbackTested in QMB with and without.
cFreely, like any feedA variant, c', keeps independence: the unit computes the label itself when the shared copy is missing.

See: Units stand alone

5The paper sequence

Why it matters

Judging uses history and paper trading together, which lowers overfitting. This sets when paper starts. Paper is never live, so it also settles whether an agent may start a paper sibling without your click.

Options

aStrictPaper only after the sealed check, for every unit.
bParallelPaper starts when stress passes, beside the sealed check.
cSibling fast laneSiblings of live units paper for less, judged against the parent.

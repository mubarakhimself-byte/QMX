# 35: Strategy anatomy through a trader's eyes

## 0. The short version

1. **Every platform and every practitioner book splits a strategy into the same few jobs.** Context or setup, trigger, filters, entry order type, initial stop, profit-taking exits, trade management, sizing, and account-level halts. Tharp's book has one chapter each for setups, entry, stops, profit-taking and position sizing. CHECKED (https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/ecip0615/2006020425.html). The STRATS schema already keeps this split: primitives are role-neutral and roles are bound per strategy. CHECKED (`C:/Users/Mubarak/Desktop/Stats/dictionary/README.md`, `schema/strategy-dna.md` sections D to F). The question is not *which* parts a strategy has. That is settled practice.
2. **Where the lines differ is who owns the exit and who owns the size.**
   - MT5's Wizard and SQX treat exits and money as separate plug-in modules. CHECKED (https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/standardlibrary/expertclasses).
   - NinjaTrader moves exits out of code into ATM templates that cannot be backtested. CHECKED (04).
   - Pine, Jesse and freqtrade keep exits inside the strategy, as order brackets or per-trade callbacks. CHECKED (https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/strategies/ ; https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-callbacks/).
   - LEAN puts generic exits in a portfolio-level risk model. CHECKED (https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/algorithm-framework/risk-management/supported-models).
3. **Exits are a finite, well-known catalogue of about a dozen families** (section 5). Each family has shipped implementations in at least two platforms. What practice does *not* settle is which exit suits which context. Platforms ship the parts, and the fitting is left to the user.
4. **Market context is mostly computable.** Trend vs range, swing structure, volatility regime, session, higher-timeframe bias, key levels, liquidity pools and event windows all have indicator or rule forms that retail tools already ship, ICT and Wyckoff included. CHECKED (LuxAlgo ICT Concepts, Wyckoff tutorial, TradingView volume profile; URLs in section 3). Only a few judgements stay by-eye in practice, such as Wyckoff phase labelling and "quality" of a level.
5. **What tunes safely is narrow.** Practitioners and the overfitting literature agree on three things:
   - Distances should be scaled to volatility (ATR multiples, KAMA's efficiency ratio).
   - Exposure should be scaled down when volatility is high (Moreira and Muir).
   - Few parameters should be searched, judged on out-of-sample or walk-forward data, with the number of tries counted (Pardo; Bailey et al.).
   - Changing *structure* automatically (which blocks, which exits) is the riskiest kind of search. CHECKED for each source in section 4.
6. **At least eleven questions in the planning run's open list are things any trader takes as given.** Section 6 lists them, with the run's own line numbers.

### 1.2 Where each platform draws its lines

- **MT5 Wizard / MQL5 standard library.** Four families: `CExpertSignal`, `CExpertTrailing`, `CExpertMoney`, plus the `CExpert` shell. Twenty signal modules, four trailing modules (`FixedPips`, `MA`, `PSAR`, `None`), five money modules. CHECKED (https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/standardlibrary/expertclasses). The line runs *between* signal, trailing and money. Profit targets are levels the signal returns (`TakeLevel`). CHECKED (04).
- **TradingView Pine.** Six order commands: `strategy.entry`, `strategy.order`, `strategy.exit`, `strategy.close`, `strategy.close_all`, `strategy.cancel`. `strategy.exit` builds a bracket of take-profit, stop and trailing stop for one entry ID. Only the first leg to fire is filled and the rest are cancelled. Several `strategy.exit` calls with `qty_percent` reserve parts of the position for partial exits. Account halts are the `strategy.risk.*` family. CHECKED (https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/strategies/). The line runs *between* entry, the exit bracket, and the account halts.
- **NinjaTrader.** Managed code (`SetStopLoss`, `SetProfitTarget`, `SetTrailStop`, signal names) vs unmanaged code. Manual trading uses ATM templates with multiple targets, a stop strategy (Auto Breakeven, Auto Trail in up to 3 steps), Auto Reverse, Auto Chase and Shadow Strategy. ATM templates run in real time only. CHECKED (ATM pages above; 04). The line runs *between* the strategy and the trade manager.
- **cTrader cBots.** A position carries `StopLoss`, `TakeProfit`, `HasTrailingStop` (the server moves the stop) and `StopLossTriggerMethod`. It is changed by `Modify*` calls. CHECKED (https://help.ctrader.com/ctrader-algo/references/Trading/Positions/Position/). There is no module system. Everything is in the bot.
- **StrategyQuant X.** Building blocks: Signals, Indicators, Stop/Limit entry price blocks, Order types (Market, Stop, Limit, Enter/Reverse), Exit types with a probability weight ("how big probability is to use given exit method"). Trading options carry session and calendar exits. CHECKED (https://strategyquant.com/doc/strategyquant/building-blocks/ ; https://strategyquant.com/doc/strategyquant/trading-options/). The features page says "4 types of trade entries and 6 types of exits". CHECKED (03). The line runs *between* entry logic, exit-type choice, trading options and money management. The genetic search picks exits as structure.
- **QuantConnect LEAN.** Universe → Alpha → Portfolio → Risk → Execution. Generic exits live in Risk models: per-security drawdown, trailing stop on unrealized profit, unrealized-profit cap, sector exposure, portfolio drawdown. Each defaults to 5 %, except sector exposure at 20 %. CHECKED (https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/algorithm-framework/risk-management/supported-models). LEAN's own docs say technical entries with coupled exits fit the framework poorly. CHECKED (04).
- **Jesse.** `should_long` (whether), `go_long` (entry, stop, take-profit lists), `update_position` (manage), `should_cancel_entry`, and position events such as `on_reduced_position`. CHECKED (04). The line runs *between* deciding, placing and managing.
- **freqtrade.** Vectorised signals (`populate_entry_trend`, `populate_exit_trend`), then per-trade callbacks. Its docs carry a table of which exit method to use when: signal exit for indicator logic, `custom_exit` for per-trade conditions, `custom_stoploss` for a moving stop, `custom_roi` for a profit threshold that changes during the trade. Exit reasons: `roi`, `stop_loss`, `stoploss_on_exchange`, `trailing_stop_loss`, `exit_signal`, `force_exit`, `emergency_exit`, plus liquidation. Within one loop, the checks run in a fixed order: exit signal or custom exit, then stop loss, then ROI, then trailing stop. CHECKED (https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-callbacks/). This is the most explicit exit taxonomy of any platform read.

### 4.2 What the overfitting literature adds

- Hold-out "tend[s] to be unreliable and inaccurate in the context of investment backtests". Bailey et al. estimate the probability that the in-sample best underperforms the out-of-sample median (PBO, via CSCV). They suggest rejecting models with PBO above 0.05. CHECKED (https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf).
- Pardo's development order: optimise, check the optimisation *profile* (share of profitable runs, shape), then walk forward. "Does the strategy deserve further development?" is a step in its own right. CHECKED (Pardo TOC, https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-evaluation-and/9781118045053/).
- Kaufman gives the testing chapter sections called "Too large to test everything", "Anatomy of an optimization" and "Testing across a wide range of markets", and puts "Mistaking luck for skill" at the head of risk control. CHECKED (Kaufman TOC).
- INFERRED synthesis, which practice supports but does not prove. Safe to adapt automatically: volatility scaling, adaptive indicators with fixed rules, sizing from the unit's own record, and stop rules. Adapt by hand or with counted tries: parameter re-fits, per-coordinate sets, regime sets. Highest risk: structure search. This bears on the operator's framing that "algorithmic trading is logically, if else statements" (`operator/transcript-2026-10-20-to-28.md:264`, CHECKED). The question of how those if-else rules self-tune is the brief's wording of it, not a verbatim quote. The thresholds in the if-else self-tune easily. The branches themselves should change only through counted, reviewed variants.

### 5.2 What the evidence says about exits

- **Stops do not help on a random walk. They can help when returns trend or switch regimes.** Kaminski and Lo show that the "stopping premium" is always negative under a random walk. It is positive and "directly proportional to the magnitude of return persistence" under momentum. They give conditions for mean reversion, momentum and Markov regime switching. CHECKED (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.07.001 abstract and intro via Exa). INFERRED consequence: the right exit depends on the context the unit trades. Trend context favours trailing and stop-loss exits. Mean-reversion context favours fixed targets and time exits. This is the one place where exit choice and market context meet, and practice leaves it to the user.
- **Exits and sizing matter as much as entry.** Tharp gives entry one chapter, opening with "Trying to Beat Random Entry", and gives stops, profit-taking and sizing a chapter each. Step 9 of his 14 is "Add Your Profit-Taking Exits and Determine the R-Multiple Distribution". CHECKED (Tharp TOC).
- **Excursion analysis is the classic way to place stops and targets from data.** Sweeney defines MAE, MinFE and MaxFE. He uses them for "stop size", "profit definition by stop level" and "changing stops by range or volatility". CHECKED (Wiley page).
- **Exits that the backtest cannot replay are a known trap.** NinjaTrader ATM strategies cannot be backtested. CHECKED (04). Bar-only tests misjudge tight exits. Pine assumes a path inside each bar and "no gaps" within it. CHECKED (Pine strategies page).

## 7. Open questions that real practice does not settle

1. **Which exit family suits which context.** Kaminski and Lo give the theory (stops help under momentum and regime switching, hurt under a random walk). No platform maps exits to context. SQX picks exit types at random with weights and lets the search decide. CHECKED (SQX building blocks). Whether QMX derives the exit from the unit's declared context, searches it as structure, or lets agents propose it remains open.
2. **Whether exits belong to the strategy or to a shared manager.** Pine, Jesse and freqtrade keep them in. MT5 and SQX make them modules. NinjaTrader moves them to templates. LEAN puts generic ones at portfolio level. Practice is split (section 1.2).
3. **How much a trailing or break-even rule should adapt live.** Step trails, profit-gated trails and time-stepped stops are all fixed rules with parameters. Nothing in practice settles whether their parameters should self-tune from the unit's own excursion record (Sweeney-style MAE/MFE) or stay fixed.
4. **The probability exit.** Triple barrier and meta-labeling exist in the literature. A live "P(stop first) vs P(target first)" exit, the run's own seed, has no retail implementation to copy. Its calibration, lag and behaviour on bar data are open. CHECKED that no platform read ships one.
5. **How to judge by-eye context mechanically.** Swing lookback, zone quality and Wyckoff phases are where traders disagree. Retail tools fix a lookback as a setting (LuxAlgo "Swing Lookback"). No source says what lookback is right.
6. **Walk-forward vs fixed parameters.** Pardo recommends WFA. Practitioners argue it creates a new strategy each window (03). Neither side is settled by evidence for short-horizon scalpers.
7. **Regime-switching parameter sets vs one robust set.** The detector's lag can cost more than the switch gains. Practice offers both (Hamilton-style models, simple volatility buckets) with no consensus. INFERRED.
8. **How order-flow context transfers to OTC FX and CFDs**, where no central volume exists. The operator wants order flow for scalpers and indices ("Order flow is good for scalpers ... especially now that I'm adding indices", `operator/transcript-2026-10-29-to-30.md:132`, CHECKED). Practice does not say how well futures order flow proxies CFD flow. INFERRED.
9. **Counting tries for structure changes.** The literature covers parameter trials (PBO, deflated Sharpe). It has no settled way to count a change of exit family or filter as a fraction of a try. INFERRED.

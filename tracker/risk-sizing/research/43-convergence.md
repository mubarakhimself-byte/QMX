## 2. The foundations

Per **foundational-thinking**, the data shapes come before any logic: if these six are right, most of the 339 mapped questions become lookups or numbers (`wayfinder/MAP.md` "the one most others wait on", CHECKED). They are listed in build order. Each says why, what it reuses from shipped code (research 42), and what it would break.

### F1. The stamped trade

**What.** Every trade in every world (backtest, stress, paper, demo, live) carries its result in money and in R, its worst and best excursion in R, and a **context receipt**: the ids, versions and ages of the readings the unit saw at entry and at exit, the market-journal minute it traded in, the market settings it used, and every tuned value in force. The receipt holds references and a few compact values, never copies of the reading history. When the unit computed a reading itself (for example with the MIS down), the receipt keeps the value it computed, so the trade replays without the MIS (review E02).

**What else the trade row records, so others can replay it** (updated 31 Oct, research 26). For any sizing rule to be replayed later, each trade row also carries the account equity before the trade, the session and day it belongs to, its place in the unit's sequence and the win or loss streak before it, the account and the unit it traded in, and costs in R and money; a stopped or skipped unit keeps writing would-have-done rows (research 26 §6, INFERRED there from what the old sizing read, `R19` §5). This is recording only: how sizing is replayed, and whether it is, is the risk session's. Excursions in R are what the entry and exit diagnostics of §3.4 read (research 26 §4).

**R.** R comes from the initial risk at entry as the risk socket supplies it. A unit with no price-based stop (fast invalidation, a time exit, a grid) has no 1R; it shows R as a typed "undefined", its money result is judged with a flag, and the risk session is asked whether it wants an invalidation distance per trade (review E03; pack §2.10 C7, stop or not, is unresolved).

**Why.** The playing field's cross-map shows six question families that cannot be measured without it (`playing-field.md` §4 row 1, CHECKED). Research 05 named "QMB results aren't in R, and backtest trades carry no market state" as the gap (STATE research table, CHECKED).

**Reuses.** The `trade` kind already has `world`, `initial_risk` and `r` (CHECKED in r42 §6.1 and r44). QMB already returns a typed refusal for an undefined measure and lands one `backtest-result` with `consumed`, `uses` and `tests` edges (CHECKED in r18 §2).

**Breaks.** QMB's CT-32 and CT-29 results are money only, so R joins them as a measure (CHECKED in r18 §2). Imported broker fills carry no initial risk (CHECKED in r18 §6). DEC-0162 (no stored composite score) is untouched: R is a measure, not a score.

### F2. Readings stored with the moment they were made

**What.** A reading (spread ratio, tick-rate ratio, trend state, regime label, perps funding state) is **library code that runs the same in QMB and live**. Every published value is stamped with reading id, version, compute time, venue and receive time of its inputs, and a health word (fresh, stale, degraded, shadow, retired). History is append-only: a retrain or a new version writes a new column and never rewrites old values. Backtests, studies and agents ask "what did it say at 08:01 on 3 March" and get the stamped value. Values computed later for old dates carry a backfill flag. A **golden replay** checks the one-code claim: one recorded day runs through QMB, the MIS and a unit, and values are compared per reading id and version (review V04). Values recorded while the machine's clock was not synchronised carry a flag of their own, because receive time is only honest with a synchronised clock (updated 31 Oct, research 33 §2: his PC's Linux clock reports "System clock synchronized: no", CHECKED in r33 and r31).

**Why.** A label is only honest with a version and a time (research 16 via STATE, CHECKED); lift studies, agent scoring and "where does this unit win" need what was known then (`mis-field.md` §5 row 1, CHECKED). Research 22 measured the size of the trap: with 2-hour market states, a fade scalper gated by the label known at entry gained nothing in total over no gate, while the same label computed with hindsight claimed a 76% better edge per trade (research 22 §4, simulated bars, CHECKED as command output there). Feature stores solve the same problem with point-in-time joins that return only values known at the time and can leave out later backfills (research 23 §5, Feast docs, CHECKED there). Confirmed (research 22, 23). One code in two callers is also what lets a unit stand alone: with the MIS down, the unit computes the reading itself (`qml-field.md` B124, P-I3, CHECKED).

**Reuses.** QMN's six rule labelers, the fitted liquidity-stress model and the shadow lane (CHECKED in r18 §4). Library append-only refs, store versions (PR 213) and the typed monthly price partitions (CHECKED in r42 §0, §2.1).

**Breaks.** The docs say the MIS reaches only the Book door and the KSA (`docs/glossary.md:558`, CHECKED in r44), so a doc pass follows. The labelers live inside QMN today; they move to a package both QMB and the trading runtime import (INFERRED).

Risk and exit numbers sit in the socket's slots; how they move is the risk session's (pack §4 "expects back" item 1). ### F4. Journals: one row shape, one writer per stream, links instead of copies

**What.** Five streams, each with exactly one writer per partition:

| Stream | Writer | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit journal | QMB for backtest and stress; the trading runtime for paper, demo and live | trades (F1), signals seen but not taken with one reason, calibration rows (what moved, from what, to what, driven by which rows), risk events from the socket, state changes, guard verdicts as rows of kind `guard` |
| Market journal | the feed keeper | per broker, account type, instrument and minute: spread, fees, slippage, quote health, session flags |
| Reading history | the MIS | the stamped values of F2 |
| Node journal | the trading runtime | start, stop, update, self-test, account-level guard events, reconcile results |
| Notes | agents | analysis and proposals, each linked to the rows it annotates |

Every row has the same shape: id, venue time and receive time, stream, subject, kind, world, links, body, schema version, and the writer's build version. Old rows never migrate. Rows live in monthly Parquet partitions with one Library card per partition; agents read through a journal door, never by opening files. **The partition key is unit, world and machine**, so a demo twin on the PC and the live copy on the server never write one file, and a failover takes a lease per unit and account before it writes (review E01).

### F5. The try ledger

**What.** Every test of any variant, settings profile, market, timeframe or window in a family is counted **at the QMB door**, not by the agent that asked for it, and that includes runs he or the copilot start by hand. The judge corrects the best result of a family by its count. A script can open the price door directly today (`TPL/download-dukascopy/scripts/download.py:41`, CHECKED in r42 §1.3), so two checks close the bypass: the judge refuses any result that does not carry a door-issued run id, and the price door records reads per family so an uncounted scoring run shows up (review V01).

**Why.** "When strategies are nearly free to make, judging them is the product" (STATE correction 10, CHECKED). Without a door-side count, luck moves from backtests to journals to agent shortlists (`playing-field.md` §4 row 6, CHECKED).

**Reuses.** QMB ledgers every trial already (CHECKED in r18 §2); the deflated Sharpe in `TPL/_shared/overfit-gates`; `d4`'s variant label table (CHECKED in `workflows-field.md` Q32); PBO and CSCV are designed in `qmb/robustness/` (CHECKED in r18 §2).

**How a change of structure is counted** (updated 31 Oct, research 26 §3). Every run lands its trade stream on a shared daily clock (daily R per run), and the family's effective try count is the number of clusters of streams that move together, by López de Prado and Lewis's ONC method or similar. So an exit tweak that barely changes the trades counts as almost nothing, and a block swap that changes every trade counts fully, without classifying edits. In a simulated family of 51 variants (40 exit tweaks and 10 different blocks), clustering found the true 11 effective tries; the common shortcut formula found 1.9 (CHECKED in r26 as command output; ONC paper CHECKED there). The identity table in §3.1 still decides version against new unit; this only sets the cost in tries. The same ledger also counts every market judged for its own settings, every market an agent shortlists, and every paper market chosen by reading backtests (research 24 §1, §2.8).

**Breaks.** QMB applies no correction today, and two templates carry their own copy of the gate scripts (CHECKED in `workflows-field.md` §0 item 2).

### F6. The instrument record

**What.** One record per instrument per venue: identity, contract facts (tick value, lot step, minimum lot, margin), measured costs (from the market journal), a trading calendar in venue time and broker server time with daylight saving, the carry schedule (swap time, **perps funding interval and rate history**, roll and expiry dates), the data levels the venue offers (0 calendar, 1 bars, 2 quote ticks, 3 depth, 4 trade tape, 5 derivatives fields, 6 outside data), the data licence terms, and, on the account record beside it, whether the account nets or hedges (review E04). The trading machine keeps a snapshot of every instrument record its units use, refreshed on change, so the sizer has contract facts with the Library down (review E08). For a contract that expires (IC's CFD futures expire early and are never rolled, STATE research 02 row, CHECKED), the record holds the expiry; what a unit does at expiry is an exit-set question for the risk session, and QMB replays whatever that session declares (review E06).

**The fields, now known** (updated 31 Oct, research 32, 45). Research 32 §1 lists every field per instrument per venue with where it comes from: about half from the venue's API (sizes, steps, digits, swaps, commission, schedule, holidays, trading mode, maximum exposure, leverage tiers, margin estimates, funding, open interest), some from broker and exchange documents (expiry tables, dividend schedules, close-only dates, roll rules, server time), and the rest only by measuring (spread, slippage, fill latency and rejections by hour, from the market journal) (CHECKED in r32 against the Spotware protocol file, MQL5 docs, CME rulebook and live venue reads). IC's futures CFDs, the expiring case above, are on MT5 only, not cTrader (CHECKED in r32 and r38 §3.1), so they do not reach the first door.

- **The data levels split three ways** (research 45 §2, CHECKED there): level 1 carries a volume kind (exchange volume, taker split, tick count, one venue's own); level 3 a depth grade (top of book, size by price, order by order); level 5 a clock and history depth per field (CME open interest is end of day, Binance's every 5 minutes; liquidation history is a provider sample). Each data level also carries its licence (research 33 §3).

(`qml-field.md` B128, CHECKED); Hyperliquid funds hourly (STATE research 02 row, CHECKED), so QMB must charge funding as a carry cost on replay or perps results come out too good (review A04).

### 3.3 The MIS

| Option | Hard cases | Maintain | Explain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A central service units read live (the 13 and 14 router shape) | Fails standing alone; a slow or wrong MIS harms every unit | A hot-path dependency to keep alive | "Units ask it before trading" |
| **A telescope (recommended)**: readings are library code; the MIS runs them across many markets on per-tier clocks, stores stamped history, publishes a board and match lists | Strong: units compute their own needed readings; the MIS only widens what they can see | One code in three callers (unit, MIS, QMB) | "It looks at every market and points; it never drives" |
| No central MIS at all: shared code and an almanac only | Loses cross-market readings (currency strength, dispersion, lead-lag) | Simplest | "Each unit looks for itself" |

**Recommendation.** The telescope. His order (style, then timeframe, then the data a market offers; STATE 29/30 Oct, CHECKED) becomes three labels on every reading (`mis-field.md` B001-B004, CHECKED): its **clock tier** (tick, bar, session, day, week), its horizon, and the **minimum data level** it needs. A unit's trading style picks which tiers it subscribes to; an asset class enters as a pack of rung tables, calendar and sensors. Every reading is published three ways: the raw number, its percentile against the usual for that instrument and minute of the session, and a word. The fast tier holds "now versus usual" ratios (spread, tick rate, top-of-book depth, realised range, cost against the unit's target) computed where the feed lands; direction and regime labels live on the session and day tiers, because at one false alarm per 10 trading days a strong trend takes about 4 hours to show on 1m or 5m bars, strong bar-level mean reversion about 4 to 5 hours, and weak versions a day or more (updated 31 Oct, research 22 §2, simulated bars, CHECKED there as command output; the first draft's "about 8 hours" came from research 05 via `mis-field.md` F-R05). A 50% volatility jump shows in about 27 bars from closes and about 8 bars from bar ranges, down from the 44 minutes he flagged (same runs). Small online change detectors (CUSUM, Kalman level, two-scale volatility) also belong on the fast tier; HMM-type regime models belong on the session and day tiers, because they flip falsely and need long windows (research 22 §6, r37 E19). So his line "you are not going to put an HMM inside a scalper's MIS. That makes no sense. But you can put multiple indicators inside a scalper's MIS" (`T2:219`, CHECKED in r22) holds in a sharper form: the rule that sorts readings is lag against the unit's holding time, and an HMM goes in the scalper's day list, not nowhere. Cross-sectional readings only a shared service can make (currency strength, dispersion, herding counts, dated pair similarity by joint state) are published for the risk session to use; the MIS describes and never controls. One live version per reading at a time (his August "constant", `mis-field.md` QO09), with new versions and new readings in shadow; a reading goes live only with his yes: "the MIS can be upgraded as the agent see fit, obviously with my approval" (`T1:1951`, CHECKED). Labels he and the copilot write ("these markets were mostly mean reverting this week") are stored dated, with what they could see, and may train a shadow version; that version goes live only with his yes (`mis-field.md` QO04; "we can retrain a version of the MIS that can also see such", cited at `T2:219` in `mis-field.md` S-COPILOT).

**How a unit uses the MIS.** Three uses were mixed (funnel3 CIS §4.3 insight 1, CHECKED): placement, a rule input, a veto. The recommendation, open for him in section 8: placement reads the finder's match list inside his ticked markets; a rule input or a veto binds a reading the unit computes itself from its own feed with the same library code, so nothing changes when the MIS is down. This would meet all three of his dated positions: units "pick the output and they propose whether to use it or not" (`T1:1951`, CHECKED in `mis-field.md` S-READS), "the unit itself accesses both market and MIS" (S-LIVE), and "if the MIS is not working, it doesn't mean that these bots won't trade. They will" (`T2:135`, CHECKED).

**When a unit binds the same indicator the MIS uses** (updated 31 Oct, research 23 §4). That is one input in two roles, not double counting, and it fits his "it means it is already specific": such a unit carries its own context, and the MIS's job for it shrinks to "where else is this true now". Double counting bites in three places only, so each gets a mark: a lift study must not count the unit's filter and the MIS label as two pieces of evidence; tuning the filter and the label it mirrors is one search in the try count; and many units binding one reading on one market is one bet seen many times (herding, for the risk session). Readings are keyed by underlying, not by venue (a gold CFD and COMEX gold are one underlying with two data ladders; COMEX leads gold's price discovery with a tenth of London's volume, r37 E32), and most readings "train" by refreshing their own usual levels nightly and at each session close; fitted regime models are pooled by class or underlying and refit in shadow (research 23 §2, §5).

**Readings that can mislead, and their checks** (updated 31 Oct, research 22). Price-only mean-reversion readings on short bars are fooled by bid-ask bounce: bounce noise at a quarter of a bar's sigma doubled to quintupled their false alarms, at half it raised them 10 to 130 times (r22 §2, simulated). So a bounce check runs per market before such a reading goes live, and they read mid or one-sided quotes. A direction label may gate trades only when the market state usually lasts several times the reading's window (at a ratio of 0.5 the honest gate earned nothing, at 3 it kept about 70% of the perfect-knowledge edge, at 12 about 81%; r22 §4); the ratio is published with the label. Order flow predicts seconds ahead and is gone by 30 to 60 seconds (r37 E06, E46), so it is a per-trade pressure reading, never a regime. On spot FX the stand-ins are tick volume (tracks traded volume hourly in one vendor study, r37 E30), spread and tick rate against usual, and the cTrader depth book, whose predictive power nobody has tested (r22 §5, r37 §4).

**Proof that the MIS helps, defined so it can fail** (review V03). Since a unit computes its own rule inputs, cutting the MIS off changes only placement. So: the **dark-MIS run** trades the same period with placement from his ticked list only, no finder; the difference is the finder's lift. The **shuffled-readings run** shuffles the unit's own context readings in time on paper or in QMB; if results do not change, the unit's context rules do nothing. Two cheap optional judge stages, not a battery. A third comes from research 22 §4: the **hindsight-premium run** replays one unit gated once by readings stamped at entry and once by the same readings recomputed over a centred window; the gap is how much a hindsight label would have flattered that unit (updated 31 Oct, research 22).

**Forge changed.** Dispersion became a must-publish after "twenty matches can be one bet" (research 16 break 3 via STATE, CHECKED). The fallback when the finder is stale is his choice (decision 2).

**What the research found** (updated 31 Oct, research 22, 23, 29, 30, 37). The telescope shape and the tier labels are confirmed; the tier contents are now as written above (research 22 for speed, research 23 for organisation: its option H, tier and data level as labels on every reading with the asset class as a pack, is the one that passed every hard case it tried). HMM-type labels earn a day-tier place, not a fast-tier one. Four more findings:
- **Reading market state does reveal tradeable patterns, and they decay.** Rest-of-day returns predicting the last half-hour in 60+ futures, "stocks in play", extreme negative funding before a 24-hour rebound, the gold fix windows (r37 §7, E10, E11, E32, E34, E35); published stock predictors lose about 58% of returns after publication (r37 E42). "Gold moves at the New York open" is honest; "my unit makes money at the New York open", picked from the same backtest it is judged on, is look-ahead. The almanac is computed from market data only, dated and versioned, never from unit results.

## 6. Decisions for him

Nine decisions. Each one is his because it touches money, his intent, words he cares about, or scope that is hard to undo. The recommendation comes first, except in decision 7, which is about his values.

**1. Where the real-money key lives.** Today a lead's rule (not your words) keeps any key that can trade real money off your PC and off any machine an agent can reach; your rule says anything that runs on a server must first run on your PC.
- **Recommended** (updated 31 Oct, research 31, 38): your PC runs the whole trading setup inside the Linux layer it already has, on demo accounts, and it watches live prices through a view-only cTrader sign-in, which cannot place a trade and which the stacked code already accepts on your PC. Only the key that can trade real money sits on the trading server. The two machines then differ in the key, the name and the broker app. The cost: when the server is down, nothing trades live, and open trades keep only the protection already sitting at the broker, because cTrader has no switch that acts when a machine goes silent.
- The catches on your PC, all fixable in setup: its Linux clock is not set to keep exact time, so a time service must be added; the Linux layer shuts itself down when idle unless two settings are changed; its disk sits on C:, which has 16 GB free, so recordings go to D:; and a laptop sleeps and loses power, which is why the server exists at all. The node design also refuses to run off its own pinned server today, so the PC must be added as an allowed machine.
- Or: your PC also trades live while it is on, and the server takes over when it goes dark. The cost: an agent working on your PC could reach a live key.
- Or: one small real-money account lives on your PC as a proof run, and the big account lives on the server.
- Or: the live key sits in a second Windows account on your PC, whose own Linux layer an agent working as you does not see by default. This exists as an option now (research 31), but nobody has tested how strong that wall is.
- Three smaller questions inside it. Does the Linux layer inside your PC count as "first run on my machine" (my answer: yes, it is the same code on the same machine, and it is already installed)? Does a key that can trade but cannot withdraw still count as a live key (my answer: yes; a cTrader trading key cannot move money out and Hyperliquid's trading keys cannot withdraw, but both can lose money by trading)? Is a second, passive trading server worth its monthly cost before the first unit is live (my answer: not yet)?
- Dropped, with the reason: a small box at home fails the power cuts the server exists for; a key that exists only while agents are stopped fails on one forgotten restart; a different rule per broker fails the few-sentences test.
- Why yours: it is about money and about who can press the button.

**2. What your click lets go live, and what happens when the finder is down.**
- **Recommended:** you approve a unit together with a list of markets you tick and the broker account; inside that list the unit trades wherever its conditions hold; a market outside the list reaches you as a card. When the finder is down or stale, the unit keeps trading the whole ticked list on its own conditions, which follows your 27 October words: "the unit keeps trading if its own conditions, the pair and the broker allow" (STATE 27 Oct round).
- Or: you approve one unit on one market at a time.
- Or, for the finder-down case: the unit falls back to a few home markets only.
- Why yours: only you promote, and this sets how far one click reaches.

**3. What your System 2 is.** On 28 October you said System 2 can mean "changing or enhancing different logics for different markets", like an EMA crossover variant; on 29 October you called it more specific amplification, sniping, and "the end goal of QMX".
- **Recommended:** keep both halves, under two names. A market's own settings: the same unit, with that market's numbers earned from its record there. A market's own version: the rules changed or enhanced for that market, which is a new unit in the same family, born only from the parent's record there. Both go live only by your click.
- **One trap is left, with a cheap fix** (updated 31 Oct, research 24). Your rule that a market's version comes from the unit's own record there removes most luck. The luck that remains is in picking: look at twenty markets and pick the best-looking one, and the best-looking one is partly lucky. In a simulation, the best of 20 markets looked like +0.23R a trade when it was truly +0.06R. The fix: one rule for every market, set before anyone reads the results. Every market that reaches the record is judged the same way, each one counts as a test, and each market's result is pulled toward the unit's overall result when it has few trades.

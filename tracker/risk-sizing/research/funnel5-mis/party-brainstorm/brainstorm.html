Ideas by theme

Style, timeframe, data16
  • B001 seed S-ORDER
    A reading card with three required headers: which style tier it serves (tick, bar, session, day), which clock its horizon runs on, and which data rung it needs. A reading missing any header is not published.
  • B002 seed S-ORDER
    Style tiers named by their alarm clock, not by trader type: every tick, every bar close, every session open, every day. A unit subscribes to tiers; an intraday unit can take the session tier and the bar tier together.
  • B003 seed S-SCALP
    Lag-to-hold ratio as the one admission number for a reading into a style tier (starting default: lag at most a third of the median hold; a number, editable). Extends R15 idea 44 from per unit to per tier.
  • B004 seed S-ORDER
    A data ladder, rungs 0 to 6 (calendar, OHLC bars, quote ticks, depth, trade tape, derivatives fields, outside data), as the way "the data a market offers" is stated, per instrument at a venue.
  • B005 new
    Rung downgrade path: each reading defined at its best rung also names its fallback at lower rungs (order-flow imbalance at rung 3, tick-rule imbalance at rung 2, range pressure at rung 1) and the expected loss of quality.
  • B006 seed S-CORR
    One measurement at the finest data available, summed up to every horizon (ticks to 1m to 5m to 1h). 1m and 5m share one source and are correlated by construction; no model per timeframe.
  • B007 new
    Three clocks a horizon may run on: wall clock, tick count, traded volume. The rung decides which clocks a market can offer (no volume clock without a tape).
  • B008 seed S-SCALP
    The scalper fast tier holds only "now versus usual for this minute of the week" ratios: spread, tick rate, top-of-book depth, realised range, cost against target. Generalises SQS; extends R05 tier T1 and R15 idea 33 into the whole scalper tier.
  • B009 seed S-ORDER
    Swing and position styles get a week tier: carry, swap or funding state, positioning where it exists, higher-timeframe structure. Styles the MIS has not been thought through for yet.
  • B010 seed S-NEWS
    A news or event style row whose clock is event time (minutes from the release), with readings such as surprise size, range since release and spread recovery time.
  • B011 new
    Sub-minute scalping left to the unit's own feed by default; the MIS supplies only the session and cost tiers for it. A setting, not a ban.
  • B012 seed S-SNIPE
    A sniping-window tier: readings that exist only inside named windows (London open, New York open, overlap, fixes), computed per window instance and compared with past instances of the same window.
  • B108 seed S-NEWS
    Reverse "news is only a danger": news becomes a context the MIS offers to news units (surprise, reaction size, recovery), while the global news kill switch still guards every other unit.
  • B115 new
    Session as measured, not assumed: "is London actually active today" read from participation, so holidays and half-days fall out (extends R15 idea 34).
  • B118 new
    A state-age reading: how long the current state has lasted against its usual run length ("this quiet spell is older than usual"); R15 idea 47 turned into a live reading.
  • B119 new
    Transition readings: the chance the state changes in the next hour, estimated from the history of the same hour, for units that want to step aside before a switch.
Asset classes11
  • B013 seed S-CLASS
    FX row: rungs 0 to 3 at cTrader, no tape, tick volume as an activity proxy, and currency strength computed across all pairs at once, a cross-sectional reading only a shared MIS can make.
  • B014 seed S-CLASS
    Gold row: two ladders (XAUUSD CFD rungs 0 to 3, GC futures rungs 0 to 5); dollar and real rates as slow outside data; fix windows on the calendar rung; GC tape as a borrowed rung for the CFD.
  • B015 seed S-CLASS
    Index CFD row: price follows the cash index and the future; cash open and closing auction on the calendar rung; the future's tape as a borrowed rung is a research question.
  • B016 seed S-CLASS
    Index futures row: full tape; delta, absorption, volume profile, opening drive, overnight inventory; roll week on the calendar; regular and extended hours as two session regimes.
  • B017 seed S-CLASS
    Crypto spot row: per-venue volume, fragmented; listings, delistings and weekend thinness as calendar-like events; cross-venue price gaps as a reading.
  • B018 seed S-CLASS
    Perps row: funding, open-interest change, liquidations, basis to spot, mark versus last; funding hour on the calendar; crowding as a class-only dimension (extends R15 idea 30).
  • B019 new
    Commodity futures row (oil, gas, grains) kept as a visible empty row with its known events (inventory reports, roll, limit days), so the matrix shows the gap instead of hiding it.
  • B020 seed S-LIMBS
    A class pack as the unit of extension: one folder per asset class with its rung table, calendar, sensors and vocabulary extras. Adding indices means adding a pack (ties QML asset-class packs, QF B041-B060, to the MIS).
  • B021 seed S-INEFF
    Per-class inefficiency hunts run as studies of stamped MIS logs: sort readings by hour of week and distance to events, look for repeatable shapes, write each as an almanac row with a mechanism line (extends R15 ideas 59-60).
  • B022 seed S-HMMCFG
    Per-instrument sensor settings ("three states on BTC, two on ETH") kept as profiles on the sensor, seeded from papers as priors and refit on own data.
  • B116 new
    Cost per venue: the same market's cost reading differs per broker, so the MIS publishes cost per venue and each unit sees its own terrain cost in context.
Reads and publishes13
  • B023 seed S-REPEAT
    The finder job: for a unit whose rules already state its context, the MIS only answers "where else is this true now". It never re-filters that unit's own condition.
  • B024 seed S-READS
    Publish three forms of every reading: the raw number, its percentile against the usual for this instrument and hour, and a word. Units bind numbers; people read words.
  • B025 seed S-CLUSTER
    Output as a board: markets grouped into co-moving clusters, with each market's readings, so a unit takes one per cluster by its own rule (extends R15 idea 42 into the output format).
  • B026 seed S-LIVE
    Each unit declares, per reading it uses, whether it keeps its own cheap copy from its own feed. With a fresh MIS value it may prefer either, and the journal records which one the trade used.
  • B027 new
    A context receipt on every trade: the readings the unit saw at entry, their source (own copy or MIS), version and age, in one linked row (extends R15 ideas 126-127 with source and age).
  • B028 new
    Subscription instead of broadcast: a unit names the readings and markets it wants; the MIS computes only what is subscribed, plus a small always-on core for the almanac.
  • B029 new
    Ask the MIS: a unit or agent can request a one-off reading for a market outside its universe, for a proposal card, without that market entering any live list.
  • B030 seed S-CONTEXT
    The unit's context claim is written in the same words the MIS publishes, and in trader language he can read ("ranging, quiet, London, no red news"), so matching is a lookup.
  • B031 new
    Stand-aside readings published as first-class ("this market is in the state that hurts mean reverters today"), so the finder also says where not to go (extends R15 idea 36 from claim to output).
  • B032 new
    Units publish too: a unit's own live state ("in drawdown", "skipped 5 signals for spread") becomes a reading others can see. Never a control.
  • B114 seed S-CONTEXT
    A context sentence across tiers, in the trader's own three-timescale form (R35 section 3: "daily bullish, in the London killzone, after a sweep"): a day-tier word, a window and a fast reading.
  • B121 seed S-S1S2
    Markets join a universe through readings: a market whose readings resemble a unit's proven markets for N weeks gets a proposal card to test; nothing trades from the card.
  • B125 seed F-R19-CAP
    The old capability_spec and compatibility rules (BLOCK, WARN, REQUIRE over archetype, timeframe, session) as the shape of a unit's context claim: REQUIRE is a hard need, WARN a preference, BLOCK a stand-aside.
Sensors that grow7
  • B109 seed S-SENSOR
    Reverse "he approves each sensor": he approves a class of sensor once (any seasonal ratio on any rung-2 market); agents add members inside that class in shadow freely; going live still needs his yes.
  • B110 new
    Year one: FX and gold packs only; the fast tier is seasonal ratios; the day tier is the agent brief; every other row shows as empty so the gaps are visible.
  • B111 new
    Year three: per-class packs, scored sensors, an almanac with years of stamped history, and units proposing markets from twin search.
  • B120 new
    A context-coverage view: which contexts have many units and which have none, steering where agents build new logic (the empty-niche view of R15 idea 90, fed by MIS states).
  • B123 seed S-PHYS
    Old physics code as a quarry: the Chaos and Ising readings from the old sentinel are re-tested on the new reading card instead of being restored or binned unseen.
  • B124 seed S-QUALITY
    Sensor sprawl control: near-duplicate readings (agreement above a set level) are merged or one retired, and a sensor nobody reads for N weeks gets a retire card (extends R15 idea 50).
  • B126 seed F-R39-RULE
    August's placement rule as a field on each reading card: shared by many units, expensive, market state, the same in test and live, worth versioning, so it sits in the MIS; cheap, strategy-specific or fast-changing, so it stays in the unit.
Agents as analysts11
  • B033 seed S-AGENT
    Morning brief workflow: an agent reads the daily charts, the MIS log, the calendar and fundamentals, and writes a pre-session shortlist in the MIS's words, timestamped before the open.
  • B034 seed S-AGENT
    The agent's shortlist is scored like a sensor: after the session its words are compared with the readings that actually happened; hit rate kept per market and per class.
  • B035 seed S-AGENT
    Three daily shortlists logged side by side (agent, MIS alone, all admitted markets) and scored weekly by a named scoring workflow. Answers who compares (PF L12-04) with an owner, not a rule.
  • B036 seed S-AGENT
    A unit-analyst workflow, a separate family: reads one unit's journal against MIS history and writes where and when it wins, naming the readings that separate wins from losses (extends R15 idea 125).
  • B037 seed S-SENSOR
    A sensor-scout workflow: an agent reads new MT5 articles and papers, maps each to a reading card, runs it in shadow on stored history and drafts a proposal card for the user.
  • B038 seed S-AGENT
    A disagreement digest each day: where the agent's reading, the MIS and a unit's own copy disagreed, and what happened next.
  • B039 seed S-FUND
    A fundamentals workflow writes slow outside-data readings (rate expectations, macro surprise, risk mood) into the day tier with sources attached; never into the fast tier.
  • B040 seed S-COPILOT
    The user and copilot weekly label ("these markets were mostly mean reverting this week") kept as dated human labels with what they could see; usable as training labels and as a week-tier reading.
  • B041 new
    Agent tools that can't be cheated: the agent's reading tool returns only data stamped before the agent's own timestamp, so a morning brief cannot see the afternoon. Look-ahead closed by the tool, not by trust.
  • B042 new
    A chart-reading agent with a vision model gives a by-eye reading (structure, zone quality, Wyckoff phase) where R35 section 7 item 5 says mechanical forms are disputed; scored like any sensor.
  • B117 seed S-FUND
    Fundamentals start as calendar plus surprise only (time, impact, forecast, actual from the free calendar); deeper fundamentals stay agent-written day-tier text.
Correlation, herding10
  • B043 seed S-LEDGER
    The MIS publishes, the correlation side reads: co-movement of readings across markets is an MIS output; any control stays with risk and sizing.
  • B044 new
    A dispersion reading: one number per class per moment for how alike all markets' states are. Low dispersion warns that many matches are one bet.
  • B045 new
    A herding count: per market, how many units read it as a match right now. Published for the risk side and the UI, never acted on by the MIS.
  • B046 seed S-CORR
    Same-idea groups: units sharing logic across timeframes (1m and 5m) count as one group in the herding count, per K4.
  • B047 seed F-R39-SIM
    Correlation by joint state, not overall: EURUSD and GBPUSD co-move differently when both are quiet than when both are wild; stored per joint state (extends R39 I4-I5, the dated ledger, with state).
  • B048 new
    A local swarm rule for picks: each unit takes its best match, then skips any market whose cluster already holds K trades from its own group (K a setting). No central allocator.
  • B049 new
    Leader-follower readings: which market in a cluster moves first (dollar index before majors, ES before the US500 CFD, BTC before alts), as a lead-lag reading per cluster.
  • B050 new
    Crowding of the outside world, not only of his units: perps already measure it with open interest and funding; for FX, broker-published retail positioning where it exists.
  • B051 new
    A reflexivity flag: once his size on a market passes a share of its usual volume or depth, a flag says his own trades may be moving the readings (scaled-account concern; a number, editable).
  • B127 seed F-R39-SIM
    Pair similarity as many dated readings, not one number (shared currency, session participation, cost, volatility, news sensitivity, trend tendency), so a pair can sit in several clusters at once.
Speed9
  • B052 seed F-R05
    Speed by information, not by model: every fast reading names its information source per bar (ticks, range, depth), because R05's detection bound cannot be beaten by a cleverer model.
  • B053 new
    Event-driven readings instead of polling: spread blowout, depth vanishing and tick-rate spikes are pushed the moment they cross a band, not on the next bar.
  • B054 new
    Precompute tomorrow: overnight the MIS prepares each market's usual curves for the next session (seasonal baselines, calendar), so the live part only divides now by usual.
  • B055 seed S-TWO-SPEED
    Two speeds taken literally: a slow board (session and day, from the PC and agents) and a fast stream (tick and bar, near the feed). A unit can live on the board alone. R09's plan and gate without the router machinery.
  • B056 new
    A freshness budget per tier (starting defaults: tick tier 2 seconds, bar tier one bar, session tier one session, day tier one day; editable). A reading older than its budget is stale.
  • B057 new
    "Time to notice" shown per reading: how long after a change the reading usually catches it, measured on history, so he sees that trend readings are slow.
  • B058 new
    Fast readings compute where the feed lands; slow readings compute wherever compute is cheap. Place follows tier, not a design rule.
  • B059 new
    Warm start: a market newly added to a universe gets its readings from stored history at once, so there is no warm-up wait (touches the DEC-0261 warm-up question in STATE).
  • B060 new
    A speed check per sensor: replay a recorded day at ten times speed on his PC and measure how far behind real time each reading falls.
Failure, independence11
  • B061 seed S-STAND
    A dark-MIS run in every unit's record: the same period traded with no MIS input. The gap is the MIS lift and also proof that the unit stands alone.
  • B062 seed S-STAND
    Finder-list fallback: a list older than its freshness budget falls back to the unit's home markets. Home markets become a required field for any unit that reads a list.
  • B063 seed F-R18
    A health field on every reading: fresh, stale, degraded, shadow, retired. Units read the field; degraded counts as missing (extends today's degraded-sensors labeler).
  • B064 seed S-QUALITY
    A meaning-flip guard: a new sensor version replays the last N weeks and shows its output beside the old version before going live; a big shift needs the user's approval.
  • B065 new
    Daylight-saving, holiday and roll calendars as first-class inputs to every seasonal baseline, with a "baseline unreliable" flag on the days around a shift.
  • B066 new
    Venue failure in 24/7 markets: a crypto reading built from several venues drops the dead venue and marks itself degraded instead of going dark.
  • B067 new
    A feed-gap rule: after a gap, readings recompute from stored ticks before republishing, and the gap window is marked in history so studies can skip it.
  • B068 new
    A kill drill: on paper, the MIS is switched off for a scheduled day and every unit is checked to still trade its home markets.
  • B069 seed F-R19-SENT
    Lesson from the old sentinel, whose ensemble was built but never wired live: an MIS part counts as built only once it has run on a live feed on his PC.
  • B070 new
    A "wrong but confident" alarm: when a reading's word stays the same while the outcomes it predicts turn sharply worse, flag the reading, not the unit (a per-sensor drift alarm; extends R15 idea 112).
  • B122 seed S-CENTRAL
    The shared services named as a set: the MIS, the news kill switch, SQS and the correlation reader. Each one's failure is designed so a unit keeps trading.
His view11

  • B072 seed S-SNIPE

  • B073 seed S-CONTEXT

  • B074 seed S-READS

  • B076 new

  • B077 seed S-NOISE
    A noise meter per unit: the MIS lift with its uncertainty. When the band covers zero, the MIS is labelled "no proven help" for that unit.
  • B078 new

  • B079 seed S-CLASS

  • B080 seed S-COPILOT

  • B113 new
    A handover test: a new person or a fresh agent reads one market card and one sensor card and can explain what the MIS says about that market today.
Stamped history8
  • B089 seed F-R16
    Every reading stamped at compute time with sensor version and input times; history never recomputed in place; a retrain writes a new column (extends R16 section 7 point 6 into a storage rule).
  • B090 new
    An as-of query: QMB asks the MIS history "what did the MIS say at 08:01 on 3 March" and gets the stamped value, so backtests use exactly what live would have seen.
  • B091 new
    Backfill marked as backfill: readings computed later for old dates carry a flag, so studies tell live-stamped history from reconstructed history.
  • B092 seed S-READS
    The MIS log as its own Library kind: per market, per reading, per minute, kept as long as the user sets (no invented cap; disk is the limit).
  • B093 seed S-REPEAT
    An entanglement mark: when a unit's own rule and an MIS reading are the same primitive with the same settings, the journal marks them as one input so lift studies do not count it twice.
  • B094 new
    Publishing changes behaviour: log which readings units bind, and watch whether a reading's predictive power drops once units use it (a slow observer-effect study).
  • B095 seed F-R05
    Labels known at entry only: a study of where a unit wins may use only readings stamped before the trade's entry, enforced by the as-of query.
  • B112 new
    Year ten: the stamped MIS history is the most valuable asset in QMX, worth more than any unit. Plan its storage and format for that from day one.
Wildcards20
  • B081 seed S-FEED
    Feed-ranking analogy from his social-media remark: the MIS as a recommendation feed for units ("markets you may like"), with explore slots for markets near a unit's proven ones; candidates only, never trades.
  • B082 new
    Weather-service analogy: forecasts (slow, issued at set times) versus nowcasts (radar, minutes). The MIS has both products with different issue times.
  • B083 new
    Air-traffic analogy: the MIS is the radar, not the controller. It shows every plane and the weather; the pilots (units) fly.
  • B084 new
    Triage analogy: vital signs (fast, cheap, always on) versus lab tests (slow, ordered when needed). Slow models run for a market only when the vital signs look unusual.
  • B085 new
    Seismograph analogy: each market has a background noise curve per hour; an event is "above background". The seasonal baseline, named for him.
  • B086 seed F-R39-KNOW
    August's two shelves: durable knowledge (how gold behaves at the fix) and dated intelligence (today's reading). The almanac on one shelf, the board on the other.
  • B087 new
    Scouting analogy: a unit's player card lists the conditions it plays best in; the MIS is the fixture list with pitch and weather for each match.
  • B088 new
    Rating analogy: readings with a grade and an outlook ("quiet, outlook turning busy") rather than a flat label.
  • B096 new
    The MIS that lies on purpose: on paper only, feed a unit shuffled readings for a week. If its results do not change, it never used the MIS.
  • B097 new
    The MIS as a unit: a context-only unit trades the MIS's best word with a dumb entry. If it cannot beat random, the readings carry no edge on their own.
  • B098 seed S-INEFF
    Strategies born from the MIS: a reading that predicts next-hour returns after costs becomes an idea record with origin "MIS study" (extends R15 idea 61).
  • B099 new
    An inverse MIS: track states where every unit loses and publish them as no-man's-land, so new logic can be built for exactly those states.
  • B100 seed F-R39-SIM
    Market twins: pairs of markets whose readings track each other across years; a unit proven on one is tested on its twin first (similarity from readings; extends R39 I4).
  • B101 new
    A reading with a price tag: each sensor shows its compute and data cost per day beside its lift.
  • B102 seed S-PHYS
    Physics readings as a research shelf: Hurst, entropy, Ising-like order, fractal dimension. They enter a tier only through the same lag and lift card as any other reading.
  • B103 new
    Bot as sensor: a small fixed scalper in shadow on every market; its live hit rate becomes a reading ("mean reversion is paying here today"). R39's "mini scalper for an indicator" turned toward the MIS.
  • B104 new
    Reverse "the MIS reads markets": the MIS reads units. It watches which units win where and publishes "mean reverters are winning on these 8 markets now" as a reading of the market.
  • B105 new
    Reverse "one MIS": no central MIS at all. Each unit carries its readings; the "MIS" is only shared sensor code plus the almanac. A pole of the field, not a proposal.
  • B106 new
    Reverse "the MIS is fast": the MIS is only slow (session and day), every fast reading lives inside units, and the MIS becomes the almanac and the morning board.
  • B107 new
    Reverse "a reading is about one market": cross-sectional readings only (currency strength, dispersion, lead-lag, each market's volatility rank within its class).
